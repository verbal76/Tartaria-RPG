### [DONE] (HIGH · rewards · mid) Storyline-tier loot inversion: The Reclaimer Relic Run pays a Common reward in a Rare-reward tier
- **Observation:** faction-storylines.json:113-120 — 'The Reclaimer Relic Run' is a 7-stage storyline gated at minRep 5, paying rewardTc 1000 + rewardRep 20 + rewardItem 'Echoing Steps Boots'. Echoing Steps Boots (app/data/items/armor.json) is Common, tcBuy 18 / tcSell 7, +1 AC / +1 DEX. Every other storyline awards Rare gear: Runic Mantle (armor.json, Rare, tcBuy 180 / tcSell 72, +2 AC / +2 INT), Tartarian Stoneband (rings.json, Rare, +2 STR), Aetheric Cloth / Aetheric Pelt (materials.json, Rare), Mud Monarch Seal (rings.json, Rare). It is also the lowest rewardTc (1000; next is 1200, most 1300-2000) and lowest rewardRep (20) of all 14 storylines, while tying for second-most stages (7).
- **Problem:** The storyline tier is the game's hardest/longest contract tier and reads as a Rare-reward tier everywhere else. This one hands out a starter-grade Common item worth ~1/10th the sell value of peers (7 TC vs 72 TC) for MORE work (7 stages). It is strictly dominated by 'Sasha's Gambit' (faction-storylines.json, minRep 6, 6 stages, 2000 TC, rewardRep 30, Rare Aetheric Cloth): fewer stages, double the TC, more rep, a Rare item — so no rational player runs the Reclaimer line for payout. Dead reward relative to its own tier.
- **Recommendation:** Raise rewardItem from 'Echoing Steps Boots' (Common, tcSell 7) to a Rare feet/armor piece matching the tier (e.g. a Rare boots or Runic Mantle-class item, tcSell ~70), and lift rewardTc from 1000 to at least the ~1300 storyline floor. Effect: the storyline tier stays uniformly Rare-reward and the 7-stage grind is no longer the worst-paying contract in the game.
- **Refined (2nd opinion):** The first designer's fix (raise to a Rare item, lift rewardTc to the ~1300 floor) is directionally correct but misses the deeper bug: the reward is the Reclaimer race's own starter item, so for the storyline's likeliest runners it is a duplicate worth nothing. Fix all three: (1) swap rewardItem to a Rare piece the player will NOT already own from creation — avoid any item in character.ts RACE_STARTER_EXPLORATION; a Rare feet/armor item (tcSell ~70) or a Reclaimer-themed Rare matches the tier; (2) lift rewardTc from 1000 to the ~1300 storyline floor and rewardRep from 20 to ~24; (3) re-run SA-3's monotone reward formula across all 14 storylines as a data test so a below-tier outlier like this is caught automatically rather than by eye. The core principle: no storyline capstone should award a Common, and none should award an item the eligible faction starts with.
- **2nd-opinion:** real_mistune — Verified against app/data/quests/faction-storylines.json: all 14 storylines listed. 'The Reclaimer Relic Run' (minRep 5, 7 stages) pays rewardTc 1000 / rewardRep 20 / rewardItem 'Echoing Steps Boots'. Confirmed it is the sole outlier: the other 13 storylines all pay a Rare item (Runic Mantle tcSell 72, Tartarian Stoneband, Aetheric Cloth, Mud Monarch Seal), rewardTc 1200-2000, rewardRep 22-30. This one is the lowest TC (1000, next is 1200) and lowest rep (20), yet ties for second-most stages (7). Echoing Steps Boots in armor.json is confirmed Common, tcSell 7, +1 AC / +1 DEX — ~1/10th the sell value of its tier peers.

I searched for a compensating mechanic and found none — the boots have no set bonus, no unique effect, just plain Common stats. Instead I found an AGGRAVATING factor the first designer missed: app/engine/character.ts line 60 lists 'Echoing Steps Boots' as one of the Reclaimer race's two STARTING items (RACE_STARTER_EXPLORATION.reclaimer). It is also sold by vendors for 62 TC (vendors.json) and handed out by a low-level exploration hook (hooks.ts black_market_lantern grant). So the capstone of a 7-stage, rep-gated Reclaimer-faction storyline hands a Reclaimer player a duplicate of the boots they began the game wearing — a bought-anywhere, starter-grade Common. This is worse than 'a Common in a Rare tier'; it's a functionally null reward for that faction's own players.

It is strictly dominated by Sasha's Gambit (rep6, 6 stages, tc2000, rep30, Rare) and effectively by every other storyline. This also directly violates the game's own shipped design rule: completed task SA-3 ('Re-tier contract rewards to a monotone formula'). This row is the outlier that escaped that re-tiering — strong evidence it is an oversight, not intent. The 'gentle intro storyline' defense fails because 7 stages is the opposite of a short/easy onboarding contract, and 8 other storylines share its minRep 5.
- **Status:** shipped OTA-917 (HAL) / OTA-894 (golem).

### [CONFIRMED] (MEDIUM · economy · early) Gear sell-back base (GEAR_RARITY_BASE) is fully nullified by the RARITY_BUY_FLOOR cap — Common/Uncommon gear sells for a flat 5/14 TC, not the intended 11/26
- **Observation:** sellPrice.ts:65-72 GEAR_RARITY_BASE = {Common 28, Uncommon 65, Rare 150, Legendary 320}; SELL_FRACTION 0.4 (line 73). Line 157 raw = base*0.4 → Common 11, Uncommon 26, Rare 60, Legendary 128. But line 158 calls applySellCaps, which (line 174) does price = min(price, RARITY_BUY_FLOOR[rarity]) where RARITY_BUY_FLOOR = {Common 5, Uncommon 14, Rare 40, Legendary 112} (lines 37-42). The gear raw exceeds the floor at EVERY tier, so gear always sells at exactly the floor: 5/14/40/112 (× durability). A Common material via RARITY_BASE (12*0.4=4.8→5, capped 5) sells for the same 5 as a Common weapon.
- **Problem:** The arb149 fix that gave equippable gear a higher base (comment claims 'a Common piece nets ~11 TC, an Uncommon ~26') is dead code for Common and Uncommon because the OTA-916 floor is applied last and always binds. Shipped behavior halves the intended early gear resale (5 vs 11, 14 vs 26) and erases the gear-vs-scrap differentiation the table was authored to create, deepening the early cash-starve — the opposite of the fix's intent.
- **Recommendation:** Give gear its own resale floor instead of sharing RARITY_BUY_FLOOR: add GEAR_BUY_FLOOR = {Common 11, Uncommon 26, Rare 60, Legendary 128} and, in applySellCaps for GEAR_KINDS items, clamp to that table. Restores the authored 11/26/60/128 gear values while keeping cross-stall arbitrage closed (gear cheapest buy via estimatedStallValue with acBonus*9 is >= these floors). Roughly doubles early gear-sale income.
- **Refined (2nd opinion):** Do not add a flat GEAR_BUY_FLOOR — it reopens weapon/low-AC arbitrage (a tc-less Common weapon buys at ~5 via bare rarityPrice but would sell at 11). Instead make the sell cap per-item and buy-grounded: for GEAR_KINDS, clamp resale to the item's OWN cheapest realistic buy, i.e. estimatedStallValue(item) × 0.8 (the same quantity RARITY_BUY_FLOOR only coarsely approximates), computed from the piece's actual AC/stats/resists. Well-statted gear then naturally clears the ~11/26 the designer wanted (a Common armor with acBonus 2 has cheapest-buy ≈ (6+18)×0.8 ≈ 19), while a bare Common weapon stays at its true ~5 break-even — differentiation by real worth, and the anti-arbitrage invariant is preserved exactly (sell can never exceed that item's cheapest buy). This also fixes looted-gear income (good drops sell for their worth) without letting bought junk arb. Bonus: delete GEAR_RARITY_BASE or make it feed the buy side, since as a resale table it is currently unreachable dead code. If a per-item cap is too invasive for a hotfix, the minimal safe step is to raise Rare/Legendary gear floors to 60/128 (both still below those tiers' cheapest buys of ~40/112... note: NOT safe — 60 > 40 reopens Rare arb; so per-item is the only clean fix). Recommend routing through estimatedStallValue, not new flat tables.
- **2nd-opinion:** real_mistune — Verified in app/engine/sellPrice.ts: gear raw (base×0.4×dur) is clamped by applySellCaps to RARITY_BUY_FLOOR at every tier, so GEAR_RARITY_BASE (28/65/150/320 → intended 11/26/60/128) is fully dead code, shipping 5/14/40/112 instead. At Common/Uncommon gear equals scrap. Git blame confirms this is an unintended regression: the gear re-level (7c913acc, 2026-06-14) predates the buy-floor (553b96dc, 2026-07-14) by a month, they are unrelated tickets, and the floor comment's bug-of-record ('Common armor selling back for 11 TC') is literally the gear table's target — the later B1 pass clobbered the earlier feature with no reconciliation. No compensating mechanic: vendor-offer-match and rapport both route through applySellCaps; durability only reduces. So the harm is real — an authored 'gear is worth more than scrap' feature nullified at the tiers early players live in, halving early gear/loot resale and deepening the intended cash-starve. That's a genuine bad-curve outcome, hence real_mistune. Severity medium not high: magnitudes are small (5 vs 11 TC on commons), it is a suppression rather than an exploit, the wider economy still functions, and Rare/Legendary keep partial differentiation (gear floors 40/112 vs non-gear 36/96). But the recommendation itself is off: a flat GEAR_BUY_FLOOR=11/26/60/128 reopens the arbitrage the floor exists to close. estimatedStallValue only folds AC/stat bonuses for category==='armor'; weapons (and low/zero-AC armor, runecaster, dog_armor) return bare rarityPrice, and many Common weapons carry no authored tc (canon-weapons.json), so their cheapest realistic buy is ~5 TC (rarityPrice-min 6 × 0.8). Letting such a weapon sell for 11 is a buy-5/sell-11 money pump — exactly OTA-802's closed exploit.

### [PLAYTEST] (MEDIUM · rewards · all) Random quest currency rewards are flat and equal-weighted — a 400 TC payout is as likely as 30 TC at any stage
- **Observation:** data/quests/rewards.json currency entries: tc_small 30, tc_medium 120, tc_large 400. questGenerator.ts:25 picks reward via pickWeighted(rewards, weightByMemory). weightByMemory (lines 13-20) = 1 + sum over tags of max(0,3-seen). All three currency rewards carry exactly two tags ('currency' + one of small/medium/large), so on a fresh save each weighs 1+3+3 = 7 — identical. Player level, quest difficulty, and location danger never enter the reward roll.
- **Problem:** The small/medium/large labels are cosmetic: a trivial level-1 quest is equally likely to pay 400 TC as 30 TC, and a late dangerous quest is no more likely to pay large than an early safe one. The reward curve is flat across the whole game and swingy within any stage — no progression and no risk/reward coupling, which reads as arbitrary payouts.
- **Recommendation:** Weight the currency tier by location danger/tier: bias tc_small early (e.g. small 4, medium 2, large 1 at low-danger locations) and invert toward large at high-danger/late locations, or scale a single currency reward's amount by location.tier. Even amount = base * (1 + dangerTier) gives a monotone early→late cash curve instead of a coin flip.
- **Refined (2nd opinion):** Treat as low priority — the primary contract economy already provides the risk/reward cash curve via contractJourneyBonusTc, and the leads novelty mechanic prevents a large-payout runaway. If you do add progression to the leads path, do NOT replace the whole roll (that would kill the anti-repetition variety that keeps rewards feeling fresh). Instead multiply just the size-tag contribution in weightByMemory by a factor derived from location.danger (bias 'small' at danger<=2, 'large' at danger>=4), preserving the novelty rotation while adding a monotone early→late lean. Note this cosmetic-tier-with-no-coupling pattern is systemic — relics (common/uncommon/rare) are flat the same way — so decide whether a single danger-weighting helper should cover both tiers rather than special-casing currency.
- **2nd-opinion:** needs_playtest — The claim's mechanics are accurate: rewards.json currency tiers (30/120/400) each carry two tags, so weightByMemory gives all three weight 7 on a fresh save, with no level/difficulty/danger input to the reward roll (questGenerator.ts:25). However two compensating mechanics reduce the impact. (1) weightByMemory is an anti-repetition novelty function: the shared 'currency' tag cancels within the currency subset, and the size tag decays after each award, so the distribution self-balances toward roughly equal thirds rather than letting a player repeatedly hit 400 — long-run average ~183 TC/currency-lead, no runaway. (2) Currency is a minority path: 3 of 13 reward entries, paid only through kill-shaped LEADS auto-completion (gameStore.ts:17724-17727), which sits atop a SEPARATE primary contract economy that DOES scale with location — contractJourneyBonusTc (contractMarkers.ts) adds remoteness-based TC capped at a fraction of base (gameStore.ts:19712). So risk/reward progression already exists in the main economy; the leads currency is low-stakes supplemental flavor. The genuine residual issue is that the small/medium/large labels imply a progression that was never wired to level or danger, producing per-drop variance with no early→late curve on this path. But that variance is bounded and its actual harm depends on lead fire-rate and how large 400 TC is versus early prices — an economy-feel question, not a demonstrably broken curve. Hence needs_playtest at low severity rather than real_mistune.

### [PLAYTEST] (MEDIUM · resources · late) Repair cost is a flat 1 TC per durability point and rarity-blind — negligible late-game, leaving no wealth-scaling sink once training is capped
- **Observation:** durability.ts:272-276 repairCost = max(1, durability.max - durability.current), i.e. exactly 1 TC per missing point, independent of rarity or value. Durabilities cluster at 20/30/45/65, so a full repair is 20-65 TC whether the item is a Common cudgel or a Legendary blade. The only wealth-scaling sink is stat training (vendorServices.ts:10,14 PAY_TRAIN_PER_POINT*value = 500-700/point) but it hard-caps at stat 15 (PAY_TRAIN_CAP) — a one-time ~3000 TC/stat, not recurring. Late income is 400-2000 TC per hunt/storyline.
- **Problem:** Sinks are flat in absolute TC (repair 20-65, gear buy 6-299, revive 300) while income scales ~20x from early to late (Common sale 5 TC → hunt 1080 TC). A 65 TC repair that stings early is a rounding error against a 1080 TC hunt. Once stats hit cap there is essentially no recurring drain, so the player drowns in TC late — the classic runaway-surplus curve.
- **Recommendation:** Scale repair by rarity so it stays meaningful for expensive late kit: repairCost = missing * {Common 1, Uncommon 2, Rare 4, Legendary 8}. A Legendary 65-durability piece then costs ~520 TC to fully repair instead of 65 — a real ongoing sink against hunt income — while Common repairs stay cheap (unchanged early feel).
- **Refined (2nd opinion):** Don't add a second parallel rarity-scaling repair sink — the game already has one (materials repair, scrapEngine.ts:255). The real issue is that the flat-TC Arbiter repair is an escape valve undercutting it. Pick one lever: either scale the TC repair modestly (e.g. Common 1 / Uncommon 1.5 / Rare 2 / Legendary 3, not 1/2/4/8, which overshoots when the player may also owe materials) so it stops being a trivial bypass, or gate expensive Rare+ kit to the materials-repair path only. Whichever is chosen, verify it composes with the Architect's Eye repair discount (gameStore.ts:19262) and tune the exact multipliers against observed late-game TC income in playtest rather than round numbers.
- **2nd-opinion:** needs_playtest — The TC repair channel is exactly as described (durability.ts:272-276: flat max(1, missing), rarity-blind), and paid training caps at 15 (vendorServices.ts). But the headline claim "no wealth-scaling sink once training is capped" is not accurate. There is a SECOND repair system the first designer missed: repairCostMaterials (scrapEngine.ts:255) = 2× scrapOutputFor, and scrap output scales with rarity (rarityScrapBonus +0..+3, scrapEngine.ts:73) plus bottleneck mats (Golem Core / Aetheric Shard) on Rare+. That is a genuine rarity-scaling maintenance sink for expensive kit, and materials are buyable for TC at full value (vendors.ts:271), so a TC drain does exist. The surviving real gap: the flat-TC and materials repair paths run in PARALLEL, so the cheap ~65-TC Arbiter repair is an escape valve that lets a wealthy player skip the materials sink — that undercut is the defensible core of the concern. But the flat TC repair is documented as intended, and the codebase reflects a deliberate philosophy (capped paid training, pervasive anti-money-pump guards) that late-game currency is materials/play, not TC. Whether the player actually accumulates a problematic TC surplus, and whether that reads as bad feel vs. intended gold-trivialization, depends on aggregate late-game TC flow (bulk consumables, gear restock, revives, material buys) that static analysis cannot settle. Plausible feel concern with a compensating mechanic partly closing it; only late-game playtesting can confirm surplus magnitude. Severity medium: worst case is a soft economy/feel issue (number goes up meaninglessly), not an exploit or crash.

### [UNVERIFIED] (MEDIUM · progression · mid) Rarity-cap ladder creates a 2-3x difficulty cliff when Rare unlocks at danger 3, and the world barely has any danger-3 zones to ramp through it
- **Observation:** Danger->rarity caps (encounter.ts:249, 308-309): danger<=1 Common, danger 2 +Uncommon, danger 3 +Rare, danger>=4 +Legendary. Enemy stat bands by rarity (enemies.json): Uncommon HP 24-95 / damage mostly 2D6(avg7); Rare HP 100-180 / damage 2D10-3D10(avg11-16.5). Crossing into danger 3 roughly triples enemy HP and doubles enemy damage in one step. But the world map has only 3 of 36 locations at danger 3 (locations.json: 11 at danger 2, then 8 at danger 4 and 13 at danger 5). Players effectively jump from Uncommon-cap (danger 2) zones straight into Legendary-cap (danger 4-5) zones with almost no Rare-tier middle ground to gear and level against.
- **Problem:** The intended smooth 0->1->2->3->4->5 ramp collapses to 2 -> (skip) -> 4/5. The Rare tier — the natural mid-game training band — is a 3-location sliver, so the Uncommon->Legendary gap is experienced as a wall rather than a slope.
- **Recommendation:** Re-tier several danger-4 locations down to danger 3 so the Rare band is a real zone (aim ~6-8 danger-3 locations, matching the danger-2 count), OR let danger 2 admit Rare at low rarityWeight (encounter.ts:31-36 already weights Rare=2 vs Common=10) so Rare appears occasionally before danger 3 and softens the cliff.

### [UNVERIFIED] (MEDIUM · combat · late) Boss AC ceiling (~26 at high Guardian tiers) plus double-counter-with-bonus-die makes late bosses a coin-flip DPS-loss for any non-dodge build
- **Observation:** enemyAC (combatRules.ts:375-389) = min(18, 5+ap) + traitAC + boss(+6). A T9 Guardian (coreGuardians.ts:94: hpMult3.1, acBonus+5, +armored, base AP e.g. Int9) reaches base 18 + armored2 + boss6 = AC 26. Player top-end to-hit stat ~14 -> d20+14 vs 26 = 45% hit. T9 HP at realistic over-level (power30/expected41 -> clamped 1.0) = 50*3.1 = 155. TTK = 155/(11*0.45) = 31 rounds. Meanwhile the boss takes 2 counters/round (gameStore.ts:27983) each adding +1d6 (28360), damage 2d6+5 -> 2*(12+3.5)=~31/round at ~90% hit; a 120-160 HP player dies in ~5-6 rounds. Even T5 (hpMult2.2, AC~22, ~92 HP) is ~13-round TTK vs ~5-round death.
- **Problem:** Boss to-hit defense hits the AC-18 base ceiling and then stacks +6 boss +2 armored, dropping player hit rate to ~45% exactly when the boss is dealing double swings with a bonus die. The fight is only survivable via the dodge loop (already flagged as strictly dominant, task #6); a straightforward melee/ranged build faces a 25-31 round TTK against a 5-6 round death clock.
- **Recommendation:** Lower the flat boss AC bonus in combatRules.ts:388 from +6 to +4 (T9 AC 26->24, hit rate 45%->55%, TTK 31->~25) AND move the boss +1d6 bonus damage (gameStore.ts:28360-28362) to fire once per round instead of on both counter swings (cuts boss bonus damage from +2d6 to +1d6/round, ~-3.5/round). Together these narrow the incoming/outgoing gap without touching the intended tier HP curve.

### [UNVERIFIED] (MEDIUM · rewards · mid) Gate/reward inversion: the highest-gated storyline (minRep 10) pays less TC than the same faction's minRep-5 storyline
- **Observation:** faction-storylines.json:61-68 — 'The Path of the True Tartarian' (true_tartarians) is gated at minRep 10, the single highest unlock gate of any storyline (next highest is 7), yet pays rewardTc 1200 — second-lowest of all 14 storylines. Within one faction the harder-to-unlock contract (2x the standing needed) pays 400 TC LESS than the easier one.

### [UNVERIFIED] (MEDIUM · resources · all) Environmental corruption accrues ~0.1/action — too slow to ever reach a penalty tier before it decays or is cleansed
- **Observation:** The heaviest corrupting weathers are whisper_fog (prob 0.30) and black_rain (prob 0.25), each adding corruptionDelta:1 (weatherEffects.ts:68,95). WEATHER_TICK_GAP=2 (gameStore.ts:8085) sets weatherTickCooldown=2 after any tick, so weather only rolls roughly every 3 actions. Expected gain in the worst biome ≈ 0.30 × (1/3) ≈ 0.1 corruption/action. Reaching the first penalty tier Tainted (11, corruption.ts:22) needs ~110 continuous corrupting-weather actions; Corrupted (31) needs ~310. Counter-pressure is far larger and cheaper: one clean-weather rest sheds 1-4 (gameStore.ts:11862), and a single Cleansing Tonic (-20) or Hollow-Cleanse (-22) — both Common/Uncommon (gear.json:636,804) — erases a whole tier.
- **Problem:** Weather corruption is effectively decorative: a player crossing a corrupting biome almost never accrues enough to cross even Tainted before the weather rotates, a rest sheds it, or one cheap tonic wipes it. The authored -1 CHA (Tainted) / -1 all-stats + +15% prices (Corrupted) penalties are rarely felt from the environmental source the system is built around; only the +2/cast mend verb meaningfully drives corruption.
- **Recommendation:** Raise corruptionDelta on whisper_fog and black_rain from 1 to 3 (weatherEffects.ts:68,95): ~0.3/action reaches Tainted in ~37 actions and Corrupted in ~103, so a real trek through a corrupting biome pushes into penalty territory. Optionally also skip the WEATHER_TICK_GAP for corrupting weather so it ticks every action instead of every third.

Leave the vulnerability tags and damage dice as-is (they are the intended anti-machine payoff and the altitude penalties justify staying at the band's low end, not its top).

### [UNVERIFIED] (LOW · combat · mid) Enemy AC tops out at 18 while attack stats train to 30 — to-hit saturates to ~95% by mid-game
- **Observation:** enemyAC = max(5, min(18, 5+ap)) + traitACBonus + (boss?6:0) (combatRules.ts:384,388). Enemy ability points cluster around 4-10 (enemies.json), so a typical foe is AC 9-15 and even the highest authored ap (13) is AC 18. Attack stat trains to MAX_TRAINED_STAT=30 (statTraining.ts:43) and adds directly to the d20 to-hit. At attack stat 15 vs AC 13: hit on d20 ≥ -2 → 95% (nat-1 only). At stat 20 vs an ap-10 BOSS (AC 5+10+6=21): d20 ≥ 1 → 95%.
- **Problem:** The to-hit axis dies once the governing stat reaches ~15-18, far below the 30 cap. No non-boss enemy — and few bosses — can present a meaningful miss chance to a mid/late character, flattening one of combat's two variables (connect vs damage) across all content.
- **Recommendation:** Raise the ceiling and boss term so high-tier foes keep a miss window against a stat-18 attacker: min(18)→min(24) and boss +6→+9 at combatRules.ts:384/388 (e.g. an ap-13 boss → AC 24+9-capped, ~40% miss at stat 18). ### [DEFENSIBLE] (MEDIUM · combat · mid) Dodge stance is a cost-free defensive layer — perfect_opening 2x dice exactly refunds the skipped turn
- **Observation:** Melee damage is PURE weapon dice: dmg = round(rawDmg × typeMult) + effect + title + surge (gameStore.ts:16901) — STR/DEX feed only the to-hit roll, never the damage. A successful dodge grants perfect_opening, which DOUBLES the damage dice on the next swing (combatRules.ts:592, count = dmg.count*2). The dodge is an opposed d20+DEX vs the enemy's attack total (gameStore.ts:28243-28245); with DEX trainable to 30 (statTraining.ts:43) vs typical enemy attack bonus 3-13, the only losses are the player-nat1 / enemy-nat20 floors ≈ 1 − 0.95×0.95 ≈ 9.75%.
- **Problem:** Over a dodge→attack loop, 2 turns produce 2×dice — identical to two normal 1×dice swings — so for a pure-dice build (no coating/title/surge riders) dodging costs ZERO net damage while cutting incoming damage on the dodge turn to ~10% (a doubled hit only on the ~9.75% pierce). Damage output is unchanged and damage taken is roughly halved: the stance is strictly better than trading blows, with no offensive sacrifice. OTA-795/815 capped invulnerability (nat-20 pierce) but left the offensive refund intact.
- **Recommendation:** Change perfect_opening from full 2×dice to a partial bonus — e.g. +1 damage die, or ceil(dice×1.5) — at combatRules.ts:592, so a dodge turn is a genuine DPS sacrifice. This keeps dodge valuable defensively while removing the 'free' loop; expected effect: dodging becomes a real risk/reward choice instead of a default.
- **2nd-opinion:** defensible_design — I read all four cited sites plus the surrounding logic. The first designer's raw math is correct but the "cost-free" conclusion misses three compensating mechanics that are present in the code.

WHAT'S TRUE: combatRules.ts:592 doubles the DAMAGE step's dice count only (`count = dmg.count*2`); the dodge contest (gameStore.ts:28243-28245) is d20+DEX vs enemy attack total with only nat-1/nat-20 floors; DEX caps at 30 (statTraining MAX_TRAINED_STAT). For a hypothetical bare-weapon build, a dodge→attack loop does produce the same dice output over two turns as two normal swings while evading ~90% of incoming — the pure-dice parity is real.

WHAT THE CLAIM MISSES (compensating counter-scaling, all verified):
1. Per-swing riders are NOT doubled. gameStore.ts:16901 computes dmg = round(rawDmg × mult) + effectBonus + titleDmgBonus + surgeBonus, with coating/coating2 folded in as separate PER-SWING rolls (OTA-362/403/873). perfect_opening leaves the `bonus` field untouched (combatRules.ts:596: bonus = damageBonus + aetherSurge, un-doubled). A trade makes TWO swings and collects all riders twice; the dodge loop makes ONE swing per two turns and collects them ONCE. So for any developed build — coating, earned title, Aetheric surge, weapon "effect" dice — dodging is already a genuine DPS sacrifice. The claim itself concedes this by scoping to "pure-dice build (no coating/title/surge riders)," which is an unusual, deliberately minimal loadout. The game's whole damage economy is built on per-swing riders, so the "free" corner is narrow by construction, and the un-doubled bonus field is an intentional choice, not an oversight.
2. Multi-enemy counter-scaling is explicit: a dodge reads ONE attacker; the code (gameStore.ts:~28305) logs "a dodge reads one attacker; the rest press in while you're committed." Group fights negate the loop.
3. The loop depends on the enemy ATTACKING every turn to generate perfect_opening. If the enemy casts/buffs/repositions/flees, the dodge turn yields nothing and is a wasted turn with no refund — an unmodeled risk.
Also: losses (~9.75%) deal 2× out-of-position damage, so the dodge turn carries punishing variance, and against a high-AC tank build the defensive delta over simply eating hits can be marginal. And the 9.75% floor assumes DEX 30, which OTA-800's own comment says "nobody hits in normal play" (~1000 uses/point past 23, and dodge DEX-training is capped at 3/scene by OTA-796). At typical mid-game DEX the contest is lost far more often.

VERDICT: The claimed problem is neutralized by mechanics already in the code (rider tax on single-swing builds, single-attacker limit, enemy-behavior dependency), so this is defensible_design, not a mistune. The narrow bare-weapon 1v1-vs-a-relentless-attacker corner is the only place the "free" framing survives, and even there the output is only neutral (not superior) while giving up half the crit/rider expectation.

WHY THE RECOMMENDATION IS WRONG: nerfing perfect_opening to +1 die / ×1.5 would hit the COMMON case hardest — developed builds already pay a rider tax to dodge, so cutting the dice bonus makes dodging strictly worse than trading for them and kills the stance for ~everyone, to close a corner only a max-grind minimal build reaches.
