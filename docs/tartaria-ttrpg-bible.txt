Introduction
  Overview
What if the world we know is built upon the buried ruins of a civilization so advanced it mastered the very forces of nature?  
In Tartaria Prima, players enter a world that was once the seat of an empire far more powerful than history remembers. The Tartarian Empire was a sprawling, technologically advanced civilization that harnessed the incredible, mysterious energy known as Etheric Power. With this seemingly limitless energy source, Tartaria flourished, constructing cities that soared into the skies, developing machinery that defied the laws of physics, and creating a utopia where knowledge, power, and innovation were boundless.
But this empire met its downfall in the mid-1500s, when their relentless pursuit of further mastery over Etheric energy triggered the Great Mud Flood—a cataclysmic event that buried their vast empire under waves of Etheric-charged mud. These mudflows preserved Tartaria’s wonders beneath layers of hardened earth, while only the towering spires and grand domes of the highest cities were left exposed, silent monuments to a lost golden age.
Now, in 2148, the world has rebuilt atop the ruins of Tartaria, unaware of the secrets that lie hidden beneath the surface. Few know the truth of this ancient empire or the Etheric relics that still pulse with energy below the hardened mud. But in the shadows, factions vie for control of these forgotten technologies and the immense power they offer. Players will explore these buried cities, uncover ancient technologies, and navigate a world where past and future collide, with the fate of the world hanging in the balance.
 The Setting: A Post-Apocalyptic Rebirth
The game takes place on Earth, a name derived from the Tartarian word for "rebirth", after the fall of the once-great empire of Tartaria Prima. In the distant past, Tartaria was the pinnacle of human achievement, having unlocked the mysteries of Etheric Power, a raw, untapped force present in the atmosphere, the ground, and the very fabric of reality. With Etheric energy, Tartaria created a world where cities were powered by invisible currents, mechanical automatons roamed the streets, and grand spires reached into the heavens.
However, their relentless experiments to control this energy went too far. In their quest to enhance their power, the Tartarians triggered an event that became known as the Great Mud Flood. Over weeks, dense, Etheric-charged mud surged up from beneath the Earth, covering entire cities in an impenetrable layer of mud and stone. The Tartarian empire crumbled overnight, and with it, much of its knowledge, culture, and technology was lost. 
What remained was a world forever changed. The surviving cities stood isolated as the lower levels were encased in stone-like mud, but their magnificent towers still pierced the sky, silent reminders of a forgotten power. The survivors split into factions, each with their own vision of the future, but the full understanding of Etheric power was lost. Now, centuries later, the world has mostly forgotten Tartaria, but a few know the truth. The Mud Monarchs control the remnants of the empire from the shadows, while the Forgotten Order seeks to rediscover Tartaria’s lost technology and bring about a new age of enlightenment. But another force, hidden even deeper in the shadows, also works toward reclaiming the lost glory of Tartaria—the Eternal Dynasty.
 Unique Themes and Atmosphere
 A World of Forgotten Power
The heart of Tartaria Prima is a world teeming with the ruins of a lost civilization, where players explore buried cities, unearth ancient relics, and harness the power of Etheric energy. These technologies have been largely forgotten, but their potential is immense, offering players the chance to wield incredible power—if they can unlock the secrets that have been buried for centuries. The core of the game is a struggle between those who wish to reclaim Tartaria’s power for themselves, and those who seek to suppress it or use it for their own gain.
The Etheric energy that powered Tartaria still lingers in the atmosphere, seeping into the environment and warping reality in unpredictable ways. Temporal anomalies, spatial distortions, and Etheric storms are common in areas where Tartaria’s ruins lie beneath the surface. These phenomena are dangerous, but they also offer clues to Tartaria’s technology for those brave enough to explore.
 Conflict and Conspiracy
The world of Tartaria Prima is one of constant tension between the factions that vie for control of the ancient relics and knowledge left behind. These factions operate from the shadows, with each group having its own secret agendas, spies, and political intrigue. As players dive deeper into the mysteries of Tartaria, they will find themselves entangled in a web of deception and power plays, with global conspiracies shaping the world behind the scenes. The powerful ruling faction known as the Mud Monarchs has long suppressed the history of Tartaria, ensuring that the masses remain ignorant of their true heritage. Meanwhile, the Forgotten Order seeks to revive the empire’s technology, and other groups like the Reclaimers Guild and True Tartarians compete for access to lost treasures.
The tone of the game is steeped in mystery, betrayal, and exploration, with an ever-present feeling that players are on the edge of discovering something greater, more dangerous, and world-altering.
 Bloodlines and Legacy
One of the game’s central themes is the idea of inheritance and legacy. Players may choose to play as Aetherborn, descendants of Tartaria whose Etheric-infused bloodlines have awakened. These characters are marked by their connection to the lost empire, giving them unique powers drawn from the Etheric energy that once powered Tartaria. For the Aetherborn, their lineage is both a blessing and a curse. They are hunted by factions like the Eternal Dynasty, a secretive cabal of ancient Tartarian families who seek to restore their empire through pure bloodlines. For the Dynasty, the Aetherborn are the key to unlocking Tartaria’s full potential, but only those whose genes prove their worth are allowed into their ranks. Those who fail are discarded or worse, harvested for their Etheric power.
In contrast, True Tartarians are survivors of the underground, descendants of those who were trapped below the mud when the empire fell. They have become masters of Aethercraft, using the Ether-infused stone to create tools, weapons, and even golems to defend their subterranean cities. Their ultimate goal is to reclaim the surface and reassert their dominance over the world above.
 Game Mechanics: Tension and Exploration
At the core of Tartaria Prima is a dynamic exploration and discovery system, where players must balance risk and reward as they delve into ancient Tartarian ruins, each filled with both priceless treasures and lethal traps. The gameplay is driven by a mixture of dice rolls, strategy, and player-driven choices, with a focus on narrative outcomes and strategic decisions. Here are some of the key mechanics that set Tartaria Prima apart:
 Exploration and Relic Recovery  
One of the most exciting elements of the game is the exploration of Tartaria’s buried cities. These ancient ruins are filled with technological relics, traps, and Etheric anomalies that players must navigate. Exploration is dangerous, with the ever-present threat of collapse, deadly automated defenses, and strange Etheric creatures. Players will need to use their wits, abilities, and Etheric knowledge to bypass traps, solve ancient puzzles, and avoid the Aether Golems that patrol the ruins.
 Factions and Intrigue  
The game’s faction system is a key element, with players able to align themselves with one of the many powerful groups vying for control of Tartaria’s secrets. Factions like the Mud Monarchs, Forgotten Order, Reclaimers Guild, True Tartarians, and Eternal Dynasty all have their own hidden agendas and objectives, and players may find themselves caught in their power struggles.
Players will engage in diplomatic missions, espionage, and even covert sabotage as they navigate the factional politics of the world. As players climb the ranks of their chosen faction, they will unlock access to more powerful tools, resources, and information, but betrayal and manipulation are always just around the corner.
 Etheric Powers and Technology  
At the heart of Tartaria Prima are the Etheric abilities that players can harness, particularly those with Aetherborn bloodlines. These powers are varied, ranging from manipulating energy fields to teleportation, and even shaping reality in localized areas. As players delve deeper into the mysteries of Tartaria’s technology, they will unlock more potent Etheric abilities, as well as access to the lost machines of the empire—automatons, energy weapons, and city-leveling devices. But Etheric power comes at a cost, as players will need to balance the risk of overusing these abilities, which can lead to devastating consequences like Etheric overloads and temporal distortions.
Historical Setting: The Great Reset – The Mud Flood
In the mid-1500s, Tartaria was not just the seat of human civilization; it was a beacon of unrivaled power and progress. With their mastery over the enigmatic force known as Etheric Power, the Tartarians built cities that touched the heavens, where grand spires and magnificent domes pierced the clouds. This power source was unique in its ability to draw limitless energy from the natural world, fueling every aspect of Tartarian life—from everyday conveniences to the towering marvels of architecture and machinery that defied imagination.
Tartaria’s capital cities hummed with Etheric energy, each structure designed to channel this mysterious force through intricate networks of devices and conduits. This free energy system created a utopia, where poverty, disease, and want were all but eradicated. Tartaria was the apex of civilization, where scholars and architects unlocked the secrets of the universe. However, as with many great empires, their ambition knew no bounds. Seeking to push the limits of their already boundless power, Tartaria’s scientists embarked on ever-riskier experiments with Etheric technology—attempts to control time, space, and matter.
This hubris would lead to their undoing.
The Catastrophe
The event that came to be known as the Great Mud Flood was as swift as it was catastrophic. It began with tremors—subtle at first, but growing in intensity as the days passed. The very ground beneath the empire began to shift unnaturally, as if some ancient force had been disturbed. Then, from deep within the Earth, a tide of dense, Etheric-charged mud began to rise. This was no ordinary mud; infused with Etheric energy, it defied natural laws, flowing upward, swallowing the Tartarian cities in slow, relentless waves.
Unlike floods that wash away everything in their path, this mud did not destroy the cities—it encased them. The lower levels of Tartarian cities were consumed, the structures entombed in a thick, impenetrable layer of Ether-infused sediment. Spire-tips, domes, and the uppermost sections of grand capitals remained visible above the mud, as if the earth itself had chosen to preserve these testaments to Tartaria’s greatness as a warning. Over time, the mud hardened into a substance stronger than rock, sealing the once-great empire beneath the Earth.
What followed was the complete collapse of the Tartarian Empire. The cataclysmic event shattered not only their civilization but also the delicate balance of power they had maintained for centuries. In a matter of weeks, the empire that had once spanned continents was reduced to isolated pockets of survivors, perched precariously on the few remaining city tops that jutted above the mud like islands in a sea of ruin.
The Rise of the Mud Monarchs
Among those survivors were the royal bloodlines and elite families of Tartaria, who had been fortunate—or perhaps cunning—enough to escape to the highest reaches of the surviving cities. These nobles, once the stewards of Tartaria’s vast Etheric technology, quickly recognized the potential to maintain dominance over what remained of the world. They hoarded the last of the functioning Etheric machines, ensuring that their control over this dwindling power source would give them a stranglehold on the remnants of civilization.
Thus, the Mud Monarchs were born. This secretive cabal seized control of the few remaining Etheric cores and the cities that still housed them. From these lofty heights, they established a new global order, rewriting history to place themselves as the rightful rulers of a fractured world. Over the centuries, the Mud Monarchs worked in the shadows, using their wealth and technological knowledge to exert influence over emerging nations, manipulating global events while erasing any trace of Tartaria from historical memory.
To maintain their grip on power, the Mud Monarchs suppressed all knowledge of Etheric Power and the true legacy of Tartaria. But as the generations passed, even they began to lose their once-intimate knowledge of the machines that sustained their reign. The Etheric technology that had once been second nature to their engineers became increasingly mysterious and difficult to operate. The once-great machines began to falter, and the Monarchs, despite their vast resources, could only maintain a tenuous hold over the power they had inherited.
Now, in the year 2148, the Mud Monarchs find themselves at a critical juncture. Their understanding of Tartaria’s technology has waned to the point of desperation, and they live in constant fear that their house of cards will collapse, revealing their weakness to the world they have so carefully manipulated. Their primary goal is to prevent any rediscovery of Tartaria’s true legacy, while they cling to the remnants of an empire they no longer fully understand.
The Forgotten Order: Keepers of Tartaria’s Lost Knowledge
While the Mud Monarchs were focused on consolidating their power, another group of survivors took a different path. These individuals—engineers, scholars, and scientists of the fallen Tartarian Empire—recognized that the true future of humanity lay not in suppressing the past but in recovering it. They understood that the lost Etheric technology buried beneath the mud held the key to reshaping the world.
This faction, known as the Forgotten Order, operates in the shadows, their mission one of rediscovery and restoration. Unlike the Monarchs, the Forgotten Order seeks not to hoard Tartaria’s knowledge, but to unlock its secrets for the betterment of the world. They believe that the Etheric Power buried beneath the hardened mud could save humanity from its current reliance on fossil fuels and inefficient energy sources.
For centuries, the Forgotten Order has meticulously searched the ruins of Tartaria, often venturing deep into mud-encased cities in search of relics and technologies lost to time. Many of these expeditions are perilous, as the mud is infused with strange, volatile Etheric energy, and the old Tartarian defenses remain active, protecting the empire’s secrets. Using ancient devices capable of manipulating the hardened earth, the Order has managed to bypass some of the mud’s natural defenses, though such technology is rare and often dangerous to use.
Despite their noble mission, the Forgotten Order is in constant conflict with the Mud Monarchs, whose primary goal is to suppress any attempts to recover Tartaria’s lost technology. To the Monarchs, the Order represents a direct threat to their control over the world’s remaining Etheric machines, and thus a threat to the very status quo they have fought to maintain. The Monarchs dispatch spies, assassins, and agents to sabotage the Order’s efforts at every turn.
Yet, the Forgotten Order remains resolute. They know that the survival of the planet may depend on reawakening Tartaria’s Etheric cores and bringing its technologies back to life. But their quest is fraught with peril—on more than one occasion, the activation of ancient machines has resulted in localized disasters, as the unleashed Etheric energy spirals out of control. These failures have only emboldened their enemies, who point to them as proof that Tartaria’s power is too dangerous to harness.
As the world enters a new century, both the Mud Monarchs and the Forgotten Order find themselves standing on the precipice of a great rediscovery—or a final descent into oblivion. Both factions know that the secrets buried in Tartaria’s ruins will either usher in a new golden age or bring about a catastrophic collapse.
Geography and Key Locations: The Ether-Infused Landscape
The Great Tartary Plains
At the heart of Tartaria’s buried empire lies the Great Tartary Plains, a vast and desolate expanse that stretches across Central Asia. Once the fertile heartland of a thriving civilization, the plains are now a barren wasteland, their surface covered by thick layers of Ether-infused mud left behind by the Great Mud Flood. Beneath this impenetrable crust lies the buried heart of Tartaria—a forgotten world sealed away by nature’s cruel hand.
Beneath the hardened surface of the plains are entire cities—sprawling metropolises that once thrived as centers of trade and knowledge, now entombed and unreachable. Spires, domes, and towers occasionally break through the mud, offering tantalizing glimpses of the wonders that lie below. However, the mud itself is no ordinary sediment; it has been fundamentally altered by Etheric energy, making excavation nearly impossible. Drills break, pickaxes shatter, and any attempt to dig deeper is thwarted by the mud’s unnatural resilience.
But there is more to the plains than just mud. The Etheric energy still coursing through the ground causes strange anomalies—magnetic disturbances, temporal distortions, and bizarre energy storms that crackle across the horizon with flashes of blue and purple lightning. Compasses spin wildly, electronic devices fail unpredictably, and explorers often report strange temporal loops, where hours are lost in what feels like minutes. Some regions even appear to be frozen in time, with ruins and creatures locked in stasis, waiting for the next surge of energy to release them.
The Etheric anomalies make the Great Tartary Plains a dangerous place for all but the most well-prepared explorers. The few who venture into these lands often do so in search of Tartaria’s lost technology, drawn by legends of vast Etheric cores and forgotten treasures. However, few return, and those who do speak of strange encounters with beings that should not exist.
The Guardians of Tartaria: Mud Golems
Among the strangest and most fearsome of these anomalies are the Mud Golems. These creatures, according to local legends, are the living remnants of the Etheric mud that buried Tartaria. Whether they are the result of ancient technology, Etheric magic, or some combination of both, remains a mystery, but their existence is widely feared by the locals.
Mud Golems are said to be nearly indestructible, their bodies composed of the same hardened Ether-infused mud that defies modern excavation. They rise silently from the earth, taking humanoid shapes that blend seamlessly with their surroundings. Some believe they are the ancient guardians of Tartaria, created to protect the empire’s secrets from intruders. Others claim they are manifestations of the Etheric energy itself—beings born from the disturbed forces that still linger beneath the surface.
Whatever their origin, the Mud Golems are rare but formidable foes, blending back into the earth as quickly as they appear. Many explorers tell tales of seeing humanoid figures rising from the mud, only for them to vanish just as suddenly, leaving nothing but silence and the faint hum of Etheric energy behind.
The Forgotten and Lost Capitals of Tartaria
Hidden in the depths of the Earth lie the Forgotten Capitals—the grandest cities of Tartaria’s golden age, preserved beneath layers of hardened mud. These cities, once hubs of culture, politics, and technology, now serve as silent tombs. Occasionally, fragments of these cities—ornate domes, massive obelisks, and carved facades—emerge from the mud, offering tantalizing glimpses of what lies beneath.
The Forgotten Capitals were once the cultural and political centers of the Tartarian Empire, and they hold within them the repositories of the empire’s knowledge. Vast libraries, archives, and vaults filled with ancient texts, technological diagrams, and Etheric schematics lie entombed beneath the mud. These cities are a treasure trove of information, containing secrets that could reshape the modern world—if only they could be accessed.
But the cities are not abandoned. Automated defenses, dormant constructs, and layers of Etheric-infused traps still guard these ancient vaults, programmed to protect the empire’s legacy. Explorers who attempt to delve into the Forgotten Capitals must contend not only with the physical dangers of the mud but also with the technological terrors that still patrol the long-buried streets.
In contrast to the Forgotten Capitals, the Lost Capitals lie even deeper, buried in some of the most remote and inhospitable regions of the world. These cities, swallowed by forests, mountains, and wilderness, are little more than myths among treasure hunters. Legends speak of vast underground vaults filled with the greatest artifacts of the empire, waiting to be discovered by those brave enough to seek them.
Yet, accessing these Lost Capitals is no easy feat. Nature, combined with Etheric anomalies, guards these locations fiercely. Ancient mechanical guardians, unpredictable weather, and Etheric fields make any journey into the Lost Capitals a deadly gamble.
The Mud Seas: A Drowned World of Secrets
Perhaps one of the most enigmatic remnants of Tartaria’s fall are the Mud Seas—vast, shallow waters that cover the remnants of once-thriving cities. Formed in the aftermath of the Great Mud Flood, these seas are a mixture of silt, debris, and treacherous currents. Their surfaces may appear calm, but beneath the waters lie dilapidated structures, colossal spires, and ancient ruins, hinting at the lost civilizations that once flourished there.
Navigating the Mud Seas is a dangerous endeavor. Etheric storms frequently rage above the waters, disorienting travelers with flashes of blue and purple lightning that arc through the sky. The very air is charged with Etheric particles, creating a constant hum that disrupts technology and causes hallucinations among sailors. Compasses spin wildly, and traditional maps quickly become obsolete as the landscape beneath the mud shifts unpredictably.
For the brave few who venture into the Mud Seas, the reward can be great. Tartarian relics, long-lost technologies, and ancient scrolls are rumored to lie beneath the waters, waiting to be recovered. However, these treasures are fiercely guarded by strange creatures and ancient defenses that have survived the ages.
The Mud Sirens, descendants of Tartarian citizens altered by the Etheric energy of the flood, are said to lurk beneath the waters, using their haunting songs to lure sailors into the depths. Leviathans, massive serpentine creatures covered in mud, patrol the deeper channels, while Mud Elementals—spirits of the Etheric-charged earth—rise from the waters to protect the sunken ruins.
A World on the Brink
As 2148 dawns, the fate of Tartaria hangs in the balance. The Mud Monarchs, now desperate to maintain their crumbling control, face off against the Forgotten Order, whose relentless pursuit of Tartaria’s technology threatens to reshape the world. Meanwhile, the secrets of the Forgotten Capitals, the Lost Cities, and the Mud Seas lie tantalizingly close to discovery, offering untold power to those willing to risk it all.
But Tartaria’s legacy is more than just a buried empire—it is a world filled with Etheric anomalies, ancient machines, and forgotten creatures that defy the laws of nature. The rewards may be unimaginable for those seeking to uncover its secrets. Yet, the cost of failure could be catastrophic.

The Arbiter describes the situation.
The Players decide how they will attempt to overcome/interact with the gameplay environment using equipment and abilities.
The Arbiter decides the difficulty of the player-proposed plan, breaking it down into smaller tasks if necessary.
The Players attempt to out-roll the difficulty level.
The Arbiter reacts to the outcome of the engagement, and the players either continue with the difficulty, die, or continue in their adventure.

Instructions for Applying Modifiers:
In this system, the roll needed to succeed is influenced by the task difficulty and modifiers from the player's gear, titles, and racial perks. These modifiers can either increase the player’s chances of success or reduce the difficulty of a task. Here’s how it works:
Base Roll Calculation:
The Arbiter sets the difficulty level of the task or event (ranging from 1 to 10).
The required roll to succeed is calculated by multiplying the difficulty level by 3 (for example, Level 5 = 15+).
Modifiers:
Gear Bonuses: Many items and equipment in the game offer bonuses to specific tasks. For example, Tartarian Climbing Gloves might give a +3 bonus to any climbing-related task, reducing the difficulty of scaling a cliff.
Titles & Achievements: Players who have earned specific titles through faction work or achievements might receive permanent bonuses for certain task types. For example, the title "Master Relic Hunter" could grant a +2 bonus to investigation rolls when searching for relics.
Racial Perks: Some races in the game have innate abilities that make certain tasks easier. For example, Mud Dwellers may receive a +2 bonus to tasks involving swamp terrain, while Ether-Born might get a +3 bonus when interacting with Etheric energy or devices.
How Modifiers Work:
Add Modifiers to the Dice Roll: When players attempt a task, they roll the dice and add any relevant modifiers to the result. The task succeeds if the final total (dice roll + modifiers) meets or exceeds the required value.
Example: A player must roll 15+ (Very Hard) to scale a sheer cliff. They have Tartarian Climbing Gloves (+3) and the title "Wilderness Survivor" (+2 for climbing tasks). They roll a 12 on the dice, and with the +5 from modifiers, their total is 17, meaning they succeed.
Modifiers Reduce Task Difficulty: Alternatively, the Arbiter may decide to reduce the difficulty level of the task if the player's gear, racial perks, or titles are especially suited to it. This gives flexibility in making tasks more accessible based on the player's capabilities.
Examples of How Modifiers Apply:
Retrieving a Bag from a Cliff Edge (Moderate - 9+):
A player with the Tartarian Rope (+2 to climbing checks) tries to retrieve a dropped bag from a cliff.
The player rolls a 7, but their +2 gear bonus makes the total 9, just enough to succeed.
Defeating a Giant with a Dagger (Nearly Impossible - 24+):
A player attempts to take on a giant with a dagger, a nearly impossible task.
The player is a Tartarian Giant Hunter (title bonus of +5 to combat against giants) and wields the Etheric Dagger of Piercing (+4 to damage).
They roll a 15 but add +9 from their bonuses, giving a total of 24, just enough to succeed.
Running Across a Slick Slope (Difficult - 18+):
A player with Mud-Cured Boots (+2 to movement in wet terrain) tries to run across a slick slope.
They roll a 16 on the dice and, with their +2 bonus, reach 18, successfully crossing the slope.

Character Creation
In Tartaria Prima, characters are defined by Core Attributes, which serve as the foundation for their abilities and strengths. These attributes, such as Prowess (strength-based tasks like lifting and grappling), Acrobatics (balance and agility), Chicanery (manual dexterity and deception), Stealth (remaining unseen), and Etheria (understanding arcane and magical knowledge), are essential for determining how well a character performs various tasks throughout the game. Each attribute is assigned a modifier based on the character's race, reflecting natural strengths or weaknesses—some races may have a positive modifier to Prowess. In contrast, others may excel in Stealth or Etheria.
You’ll roll for each core attribute and record the results on your character sheet when creating your character. However, once all the attributes are rolled, you are granted a one-time opportunity to roll a d6 and reroll one attribute of your choice. This mechanic represents your character’s innate potential to grow beyond the limitations of their surroundings, gaining abilities that may surprise others. It symbolizes the unpredictability of personal growth and the possibility of exceeding expectations, allowing your character to improve or refine an aspect of themselves in ways not initially foreseen.
This reroll reflects the unique nature of development in Tartaria Prima—a world where individuals can defy their starting circumstances and unlock hidden potential. Once the reroll is used and attributes are finalized, the core attributes and their modifiers will guide your character’s interactions with the game world, shaping their strengths and weaknesses as they face the challenges ahead.

Determining Starting Tartarian Coin
In the character creation process, each player's starting gold is determined by rolling a specific number of dice based on their character's race. The roll's result will then be multiplied by the amount of Tartarian Coins (TC) specified for their race, representing the standard currency in Tartaria Prima. This amount reflects the resources a character could realistically have on hand at the start of their adventure, influenced by their societal status, access to wealth, or background.

The Seven Races of Tartaria
Tartarian Giants
Masters of the Lost Empire
The Tartarian Giants once strode across the landscape like living gods, the architects and rulers of a civilization unparalleled in human history. Towering over 10 feet tall, their very presence commanded awe and respect. They were physically imposing and intellectually superior, blending vast knowledge of Etheric Power with unrivaled engineering prowess. These Giants created the monumental structures that still stand today—half-buried beneath Aetherstone but defiant against time. Their spires and domes, the remnants of their glory, remain etched into the landscape, their purpose half-forgotten but still humming with dormant Etheric energy.
The Giants embedded their technology deep within their architecture, integrating it so seamlessly that it became indistinguishable from the natural environment. Etheric grids powered their cities, pulling limitless energy from the atmosphere. This was second nature to the Giants—tools and technology so advanced that they seemed like magic to the world outside. Yet, despite their immense power and intellect, they overreached. In their quest to harness even greater forces of the Ether, they triggered the Great Aetherstone Flood. This catastrophe buried their empire beneath a thick layer of Etheric-charged Aetherstone. Their towering cities crumbled, and the world as they knew it was lost.
Now, millennia later, the Tartarian Giants are spoken of only in legends. Their possible survival remains a tantalizing mystery. Sightings of cryptids like Sasquatch, Yeti, or Bigfoot fuel theories that these ancient beings may still walk the Earth, their descendants mutated but holding fragments of the power and knowledge of their ancestors. Those who believe the Giants still live see them as hidden guardians of the world’s secrets, watching from the shadows.
Daily Life (In Ancient Tartaria):A typical day for a Tartarian Giant in the height of their empire was one of grand ambition. In the morning, they would gather in their ethereal citadels, where colossal council chambers floated above the ground, supported by invisible streams of Etheric energy. From these vantage points, they oversaw vast cities, planning expansion projects stretching across continents. Giants did not toil—they commanded legions of Etheric constructs that carried out their designs with flawless precision.
Some Giants were scholars dedicated to advancing their understanding of Etheric Power. They delved deep into the mysteries of the universe, experimenting with Etheric currents and manipulating time, space, and even life itself. Others were warriors, protectors of the empire, wielding weapons infused with Etheric energy, capable of tearing through any foe with a single strike. Their elite warriors trained endlessly, their exercises visible from miles away as they clashed with tremendous force, sending shockwaves through the very air.
Daily Life (In Modern Times):If any Giants survive, they are isolated, living in the most remote, impenetrable parts of the world. Their days would be spent maintaining the last flickering remnants of their once-mighty civilization. Hidden away in ancient citadels perched atop high mountains, they tend to the old Etheric machines that still power the few remaining bastions of their empire. These tasks are not ones of routine maintenance but of survival—their ancient devices faltering, each day bringing them closer to failure.
Despite their dwindling numbers and fading knowledge, the Giants cling to the hope of rebuilding their lost empire. They remain vigilant, watching the modern world from afar, their ancient instincts as rulers and protectors still intact. They keep the last Etheric cores active, knowing that their time may come again.
Racial Traits:
Towering Strength: Giants are known for their incredible strength. Gain +2 to Prowess and Grip.
Ancient Insight: The Giants’ deep understanding of architecture and technology gives them an innate ability to interact with Tartarian relics. Gain +1 to Etheria and Woodsman when investigating ancient structures or artifacts.
Legacy of Power: Once per day, tap into the hidden Etheric Power within. This ability allows them to channel energy into a relic, providing unexpected results like repairing a broken artifact or powering up dormant technology.
Etheric Resilience: Giants naturally resist magic and arcane effects due to their long exposure to Etheric energies. Gain +1 to all saving throws against magical effects.
Role in the World:Giants hold the key to Tartaria’s past and perhaps its future. Choosing a Giant as a character allows you to walk in the footsteps of the lost empire’s greatest rulers, with the chance to unlock ancient secrets or reclaim the world that was once theirs.
True Tartarians / Mud Dwellers
Survivors of the Subterranean World
The True Tartarians are the last direct descendants of the original Tartarian people, those who survived the Great Aetherstone Flood but were trapped beneath the mud for centuries. Over time, their bodies and minds adapted to the harsh, subterranean world they call home. They live deep underground, beneath the forgotten cities of Tartaria, in vast caverns and ruined halls that echo the memories of a fallen empire. The True Tartarians have become masters of scavenging, reverse-engineering ancient technology, and manipulating the potent energies of Aetherstone—a substance they consider both a curse and a blessing.
Despite their harsh environment, the True Tartarians revere their ancestors, believing that the Tartarian Giants were not just rulers but gods. They worship the relics left behind, viewing the fallen empire as a sacred past they must preserve. Their culture revolves around the belief that the Giants will return one day, and when that time comes, the True Tartarians will rise again to reclaim the surface. Until then, they remain hidden from the world above, scavenging what little they can from the ruins and surviving through their mastery of Aether Tech—manipulating Aetherstone to power their makeshift settlements.
However, the True Tartarians are more than just scavengers. Over centuries, they have developed a unique form of magic known as Aethercraft, which allows them to harness the energy of Aetherstone in ways the surface world can barely comprehend. From constructing Aether Golems to shaping the very stone and earth around them, Aethercraft has become their lifeblood. However, its use often leaves its practitioners physically and mentally scarred by prolonged exposure to the unstable energies of Aetherstone.
Daily Life (In the Underground):The life of a True Tartarian is one of constant hardship and faith. Each day begins with a ritual honoring the Tartarian Giants, who they believe will one day return to lead them back to the surface. After the morning rites, groups of scavengers, called Seekers, venture into the deepest parts of the ancient ruins, navigating labyrinthine tunnels and long-forgotten cities, searching for relics and Aetherstone deposits.
In their underground enclaves, the True Tartarians live in tight-knit communities. Their homes are built within the hollowed-out ruins of the old world, reinforced with scavenged Aether Tech. These caverns glow with the eerie blue light of Aetherstone reactors, providing just enough power to sustain life in the pitch-black depths. Water and food are scarce, with underground farms growing strange bioluminescent fungi that sustain them.
At the heart of each settlement is the Aetherforge, a sacred place where Aethercraft practitioners work tirelessly to shape Aetherstone into weapons, tools, and devices that keep their society running. The most skilled Aethercraft users are revered as priests, able to manipulate the raw power of Aetherstone to defend their people or create powerful golems to protect the underground cities.
Despite the grim conditions, the True Tartarians have a strong sense of identity and pride in their ancient heritage. They train their young to be warriors, scholars, and engineers, ensuring the knowledge of their ancestors is passed down from generation to generation. Their ultimate goal is to reclaim the world above, but they must survive in the shadows for now.
Daily Life (In the Present):True Tartarians would see themselves as part of a sacred mission if they were to reach the surface. Above ground, these individuals are secretive and cautious, often disguising their features and avoiding large cities. The surface world is foreign and dangerous, full of people unaware of their true history. For the True Tartarians, every interaction with surface dwellers is an opportunity to gather resources or knowledge for the eventual return to glory.
Above all, True Tartarians believe they are the rightful heirs to the world, and everything they do is a step toward reclaiming that legacy.
Racial Traits:
Dark Vision: Generations spent underground have sharpened their eyesight. True Tartarians can see up to 60 feet in darkness.
Aethercraft Mastery: Gain +2 to Etheria when using Aethercraft. True Tartarians can manipulate Aetherstone to create tools, weapons, and golems or shape the earth around them.
Relic Savvy: True Tartarians have developed an instinct for scavenging and repurposing ancient Tartarian relics. Gain +2 to Chicanery when dealing with Aetherstone or Tartarian technology.
Aetherstone Resilience: Prolonged exposure to Aetherstone has made them resistant to Etheric damage. They take half damage from all Etheric or arcane-based attacks.
Aethercraft Abilities:
Aether Golem Constructor: True Tartarians can craft temporary Aether Golems from Aetherstone and Etheric energy to assist in combat or protect their settlements. These golems, however, are unstable and may collapse without warning.
Aetherstone Manipulation: Shape the earth and stone around you, creating barriers, pathways, or small weapons using Aetherstone energy. The effects are temporary but powerful.
Aetheric Healing: A rare skill among Aethercraft practitioners, this ability allows True Tartarians to use Aetherstone to mend wounds. However, it comes at a personal cost of mental strain or physical scarring.
Role in the World:True Tartarians see themselves as the rightful heirs to the world of Tartaria, driven by the need to reclaim their lost empire and return to the surface. As a player, embodying a True Tartarian means balancing survival with pursuing ancient knowledge, fighting to rebuild a world that the surface-dwellers don’t even know exists.
Reclaimers
Adventurers of the Forgotten World
The Reclaimers are human adventurers descended from the people of Tartaria, though most do not know their true heritage. These individuals are explorers, scavengers, and opportunists who make their living diving into the ruins of ancient Tartaria to retrieve relics of immense value. Unknown to them, the artifacts they unearth are relics of their ancestors, imbued with powerful Etheric energy. Many Reclaimers sell these treasures on the black market, unaware of the true significance of their finds.
Some Reclaimers operate as part of the Reclaimers Guild, a loose but powerful network of explorers who trade information, relics, and ancient knowledge. The Guild controls access to many of the most profitable ruins, maintaining secret archives on the locations of Tartarian cities and vaults. Other Reclaimers prefer to work independently, seeing themselves as mercenaries or treasure hunters, selling their finds to the highest bidder. Regardless of their methods, all Reclaimers share a common drive—the thrill of discovery and the promise of untold wealth.
Daily Life (In the Field):The life of a Reclaimer is one of constant danger and risk. Each morning begins with preparation—gearing up with ancient maps, climbing tools, and Aetherstone-powered devices to descend into the labyrinthine ruins of Tartaria. These expeditions are perilous; forgotten traps, collapsing structures, and the ever-present threat of Architectural Sentinels make each dive a test of skill and survival. Reclaimers must rely on their wits, agility, and knowledge of relics to navigate these ancient death traps.
Once inside, the day is spent scouring the depths of the ruins, searching for hidden passages or sealed vaults filled with Tartarian technology. A successful dive can bring immense rewards—an ancient artifact or Etheric core can fetch a fortune. But failure often means death, as many Reclaimers never return from their dives. The lucky ones live to sell their finds on the black market or to secretive collectors, who are always eager to pay for forgotten technology.
Reclaimers often gather in hidden taverns or camps near the ruins at night, exchanging stories of their adventures, bartering relics, or negotiating their next job. While some enjoy the camaraderie, most are guarded, knowing that every Reclaimer is also a competitor for the rarest and most valuable finds. In this world, trust is fleeting, and betrayal is common.
Daily Life (Outside the Ruins):Above ground, Reclaimers live on the fringes of society. Some disguise their work as mere archaeology or relic hunting, but most keep a low profile, avoiding the authorities and powerful factions that seek to control Tartaria’s secrets. For Reclaimers, every interaction with others is a potential opportunity to gather information about new ruins, locate buyers for their relics, or form temporary alliances for dangerous dives.
Many Reclaimers work for shadowy organizations or wealthy elites who have their own agendas concerning Tartaria’s lost technology. While some see themselves as noble explorers reclaiming their ancestors' heritage, others are driven purely by greed, willing to sell their findings to the highest bidder, regardless of the consequences. Either way, Reclaimers are deeply connected to the ancient past, though they may not even realize it.
Racial Traits:
Urban Explorer: Reclaimers are skilled at navigating the ruins and forgotten cities of Tartaria. Gain +2 to Acrobatics and Stealth when exploring city ruins or navigating underground structures.
Ruins Specialist: Reclaimers have a natural aptitude for finding hidden traps, secret passages, and valuable artifacts. Gain +1 to Investigation and +1 to Woodsman when searching for relics or navigating dangerous areas.
Rogue’s Ingenuity: Reclaimers are quick thinkers, skilled with their hands. Gain +1 to Chicanery and a reroll when picking locks or disarming traps.
Relic Hunter: Reclaimers can detect hidden Aetherstone signatures, allowing them to sense nearby relics within 30 feet.
Role in the World:As a Reclaimer, you are an adventurer, treasure hunter, and opportunist, delving into the forgotten ruins of Tartaria to uncover ancient relics and make your fortune. Whether driven by the thrill of discovery, the promise of wealth, or a secret desire to reclaim your ancestors’ lost legacy, your actions will shape the future of Tartaria. Choose wisely, as each relic you uncover holds great power and risk.
Architectural Sentinels
Guardians of the Ancient World
The Architectural Sentinels were once the elite, semi-conscious machines created by the Tartarian Giants to protect their vast cities and the Etheric technology within. Programmed to ensure that only those with the knowledge of Tartaria’s past could access its most powerful relics, these tireless sentinels still patrol the forgotten ruins of the empire, guarding the buried relics from intruders. They are remnants of an age long gone, yet their loyalty to their creators and purpose remains unwavering.
Powered by the same Aetherstone that caused the Great Aetherstone Flood, the Sentinels are more than simple machines. They are sophisticated constructs capable of adapting to new threats, and their mechanical bodies are imbued with Etheric energy, allowing them to heal over time, repair broken structures, and maintain the ancient technologies left behind by the Giants. Though many Sentinels have been damaged or fallen into disrepair over the centuries, those that remain are formidable guardians, ensuring that the secrets of Tartaria are not easily disturbed.
Daily Life (In Ancient Tartaria):In the age of the Tartarian Empire, the Sentinels were the silent protectors of the cities. Their daily existence was defined by vigilance, ensuring that the relics and technologies of Tartaria were safe from external threats. Each Sentinel was assigned to patrol a specific district, temple, or vault, enforcing the Giants' will by defending the Etheric cores and protecting the ancient libraries of Tartarian knowledge.
Though the Sentinels did not have emotions or desires like organic beings, they were highly intelligent and capable of recognizing patterns and learning from past encounters. Their duty extended beyond mere defense—they could repair broken sections of their city, maintain power grids, and even perform ritualistic maintenance on the Etheric cores. For the Tartarian Giants, the Sentinels were essential to the day-to-day function of their empire, an unyielding force that ensured its longevity.
Daily Life (In the Present):In modern times, the Sentinels remain a terrifying presence in the ruins of Tartaria. They patrol the crumbling cities daily, tirelessly guarding the ancient relics. They do not sleep or rest, and the passage of centuries means little to them. While their original programming still drives them to defend the relics, many Sentinels have developed quirks due to prolonged exposure to Aetherstone energy. Some display signs of curiosity or hesitation, while others may seem overly aggressive, their programming distorted by time.
When intruders enter the ruins, the Sentinels activate, their eyes glowing with Etheric light as they scan for threats. Those who trigger their defenses are met with Etheric-powered weapons, shields, and a relentless pursuit that often ends in the intruder's destruction. Few have survived a direct confrontation with a fully powered Sentinel.
Some Reclaimers and factions have attempted to deactivate or control the Sentinels, but these efforts rarely succeed. The few who have managed to bypass the Sentinel’s programming have found temporary reprieves, though the machines inevitably revert to their original function of guardianship.
Racial Traits:
Etheric Constitution: Sentinels are powered by Aetherstone cores. They take half the damage from all energy-based attacks.
Indomitable Strength: Sentinels possess unmatched physical power. Gain +2 to Grip and +1 to Prowess.
Immunity to Time: Sentinels do not age, require food, or suffer from exhaustion. They are immune to the effects of aging and fatigue.
Defensive Protocols: Sentinels can activate a defensive shield once per day, reducing incoming damage by half for three rounds.
Role in the World:Playing as an Architectural Sentinel places you in the unique position of a living relic of Tartaria. Whether continuing your ancient duty to guard the ruins or discovering a new purpose in the modern world, you possess knowledge and power that few others can claim. As a Sentinel, you stand at the intersection of the past and the future, with the ability to shape both in ways that no organic being can.
Aether Golems / Mud Golems
Sentinels of Aetherstone
The Aether Golems are living manifestations of Etheric energy, creatures created by the chaotic fallout of the Great Aetherstone Flood. Born from the collision of raw Etheric power and the Aetherstone that now blankets the world, these golems are elemental in nature, composed of stone, mud, and energy. They are not natural beings but a byproduct of Tartaria’s downfall—walking remnants of the disaster that destroyed the empire.
Aether Golems are feared and revered in equal measure. Some believe the Tartarian Giants once constructed them as sentinels designed to protect the cities during the war. Others claim they are living extensions of the Aetherstone itself, manifestations of the energy that once powered an entire empire. Regardless of their origins, Aether Golems are immensely powerful creatures, capable of wreaking havoc on anyone who crosses their path.
Though they are typically seen as mindless creatures, their connection to Aetherstone gives them a mysterious awareness of their surroundings. The energy that binds them to the earth pulses with life, allowing them to sense nearby Etheric disturbances or intruders in the ruins they roam. This makes them formidable guardians, often appearing without warning to crush intruders who dare approach the lost technologies of Tartaria.
Daily Life (In Ancient Tartaria):In the ancient world, Aether Golems were rarely seen but highly revered as the ultimate line of defense for the Tartarian Empire. Summoned by the most powerful Etheric mages, these golems were created to defend sacred sites, powerful relics, and Etheric cores from outside forces. Unlike the mechanical Architectural Sentinels, the Aether Golems were elemental beings of immense power, capable of merging with the environment to launch devastating ambushes on enemies.
The Golems were connected to the Etheric ley lines that powered Tartaria’s cities, drawing strength from the very ground they were summoned from. Their purpose was singular: to protect the empire’s most valuable assets at all costs. Many saw them as walking gods, divine entities that embodied the raw power of Tartaria’s Etheric mastery. Even the Tartarian Giants treated them respectfully, knowing that once summoned, the Golems would defend their charge until their destruction.
Daily Life (In the Present):In modern times, the Aether Golems wander the ruined cities of Tartaria, tethered to the Aetherstone deposits that still pulse with life beneath the surface. Though their creators are long gone, the Golems continue their ancient duties, protecting relics, Aetherstone cores, and lost Etheric technology from those who would disturb it. Their forms have been eroded over centuries, and they now resemble massive, hulking figures of rock and mud, glowing with the soft, eerie light of Aetherstone energy.
Aether Golems are rarely active unless provoked by the presence of intruders. When they sense movement or Etheric disturbances near their domain, they rise from the earth, their bodies reforming from the Aetherstone around them. Once engaged, they are relentless in their pursuit, smashing through stone and metal to reach their target.
Despite their fearsome reputation, some factions and individuals have tried to tame or control the Golems with varying degrees of success. Those who have attempted to bind an Aether Golem using relics or ancient spells have often been destroyed by the force they sought to control. Only a few have used ancient Tartarian technologies to command these behemoths, though even these methods are unpredictable and dangerous.
Racial Traits:
Aetherstone Resilience: Aether Golems are nearly impervious to physical damage. Gain +2 to Prowess and reduce all non-Etheric damage by half.
Elemental Control: Aether Golems can manipulate the earth and stone around them, creating barriers or launching boulders at enemies. The surrounding Aetherstone and terrain fuel this ability.
Etheric Pulse: Once per day, Aether Golems can release a wave of Etheric energy, stunning nearby enemies for one round.
Regenerative Core: As long as an Aether Golem remains near Aetherstone deposits, they can regenerate, slowly restoring lost health.
Role in the World:As an Aether Golem, you are a walking manifestation of Tartaria’s lost power. Bound to the ancient ley lines of Aetherstone, your very existence is a testament to the empire’s mastery over Etheric energy. Whether you continue your ancient duty to protect the relics of Tartaria or seek a new purpose in the modern world, you are a force of nature—unstoppable and eternal.
Unknowing Masses
The Ignorant Inheritors of a Forgotten Past
The Unknowing Masses represent the majority of humanity living on the surface—descendants of Tartaria who have long forgotten their empire’s past. They go about their lives unaware of the ancient powers and secrets buried beneath their feet. To them, the world is a place of progress, industry, and normalcy, with little knowledge of the ancient ruins, lost relics, or the Etheric energy that once powered the Tartarian Empire.
For these people, the idea of a lost empire is a myth, an urban legend dismissed by scholars and the general public. Conspiracy theories about the Great Aetherstone Flood and stories of Tartarian Giants are scoffed at and ridiculed. Yet, some among the Unknowing Masses are starting to awaken to the truth through personal discoveries or stumbling upon ancient relics that hold the key to their forgotten heritage.
The Unknowing Masses are often the last to realize the full scope of the events still shaping their world. Though they live in ignorance, some individuals question the official histories they’ve been told, especially after coming into contact with strange artifacts or mysterious factions seeking to reclaim Tartaria’s lost power. Once exposed to the truth, members of the Unknowing Masses find themselves on a path that could lead them to great power, danger, or the truth about their origins.
Daily Life (Before the Discovery):For most members of the Unknowing Masses, life is typical of any modern society. They live in bustling cities, engage in business, pursue education, and do their daily routines without a second thought. They enjoy modern comforts like technology, electricity, and communication, not realizing that much of what they take for granted is derived from ancient Tartarian technology repurposed by the ruling factions.
Most people of the Unknowing Masses grow up hearing rumors of buried cities, hidden technologies, or relic hunters. Still, these stories are typically brushed off as conspiracy theories or folklore. Tales of Tartarian Giants lost civilizations and Aetherstone floods are passed around as harmless urban legends, with few realizing just how much of it is true. To the Unknowing Masses, the idea of a grand empire lying beneath their feet seems far-fetched, and most are content to live their lives within the confines of their daily experiences.
Daily Life (After the Discovery):Everything changes for those who stumble upon an ancient relic or discover hidden knowledge of Tartaria’s past. Finding a single piece of ancient technology—a forgotten Etheric device, a map of Tartarian ruins, or even a mysterious artifact—sets them on a journey that unravels everything they thought they knew. The discovery of such an artifact often attracts the attention of powerful factions, Reclaimers, or True Tartarians, who want to recruit, manipulate, or silence those who have stumbled upon the truth.
These individuals begin to uncover the secret histories buried beneath centuries of misinformation. They may join secretive groups that seek to reclaim Tartaria’s lost technology, or they may go on the run, pursued by those who would stop them from revealing the truth. Life as a member of the Unknowing Masses after the discovery is one of danger, curiosity, and the gradual realization that the world is not what it seems. For some, this is a path to enlightenment and power; for others, it leads to peril and conspiracy.
Racial Traits:
Curious Mind: Once exposed to Tartaria’s secrets, members of the Unknowing Masses gain +2 to Investigation as they become more adept at researching ancient lore and uncovering hidden knowledge.
Beginner’s Luck: Characters from the Unknowing Masses have an innate ability to adapt quickly to new situations. Once per day, reroll a failed check related to survival, combat, or interacting with relics.
Quick Learner: Gain additional skill proficiency when discovering new technologies, artifacts, or factions related to Tartaria.
Ignorant of Etheric Power: Initially, members of the Unknowing Masses have no resistance to Etheric energy or knowledge of Aetherstone. However, upon exposure to relics or Etheric technology, they can gain abilities related to relic use.
Role in the World:As a member of the Unknowing Masses, you begin your journey unaware of the true history and power of Tartaria. But once you uncover your first relic, everything changes. Your path will take you from ignorance to enlightenment as you become a key player in the battle for Tartaria’s forgotten knowledge. Whether you join the Reclaimers, ally with the True Tartarians, or forge your own destiny, your discoveries will shape the world's future.
Aetherborn
The Hidden Heirs of Tartaria
The Aetherborn are rare individuals who carry the ancient bloodline of Tartaria. Though they live among the Unknowing Masses on the surface, they are not truly part of the modern world. Family whispers and old rumors haunt their past—stories of long-lost relatives who displayed strange gifts, relatives who were outcasts and shunned for being different. Most Aetherborn grow up unaware of their true lineage, hearing only fragmented legends of distant ancestors who once wielded great power before they were forced into hiding.
As they come of age, the Aetherborn begin to experience odd phenomena: inexplicable visions, strange sensations when near relics of the past, or surges of Etheric energy they cannot explain. Though initially terrifying, these gifts are remnants of their forgotten heritage—powers connected to the long-lost Tartarian throne. Some Aetherborn may even be the last heirs to noble houses, their ancestors hiding them away to avoid persecution or death during Tartaria’s fall.
For the Aetherborn, the truth of their origins is a double-edged sword. On one hand, they may be the last surviving members of Tartaria’s ruling elite, capable of reclaiming their throne and shaping the world's future. On the other hand, these powers set them apart, making them targets for factions, scavengers, and those who seek to control the lost power of Tartaria. As they discover more about their lineage, they may find themselves drawn into the tangled web of Tartaria’s politics, hunted by those who fear the return of its rightful rulers.
Daily Life (Before Awakening):Before their powers manifest, the Aetherborn grow up just like any member of the Unknowing Masses. They attend schools, work ordinary jobs, and live in bustling cities. However, they often feel like outsiders, sensing a deeper connection to the world around them that they cannot explain. Family stories about long-lost relatives who were “different” or “cursed” sometimes linger in their thoughts, though they rarely give them much credence.
Their lives begin to change when they first come into contact with ancient relics, Aetherstone, or Etheric energy. They might feel strange vibrations in the air or experience visions of the past—brief flashes of Tartaria’s lost glory. As they draw closer to their true heritage, these powers intensify, marking them as something other than human.
Daily Life (After Awakening):Once the Aetherborn begin to manifest their powers, their lives change forever. They are no longer able to deny their connection to Tartaria, so they become obsessed with finding the truth about their origins. Some may seek out the True Tartarians, hoping for answers about their bloodline, while others may join the Reclaimers or shadowy factions to unearth more relics and uncover their destiny. In their search for answers, the Aetherborn realize they may be the last of Tartaria’s royal bloodlines, heir to a throne buried under centuries of mud and rubble. With this knowledge comes great responsibility—and great danger. Whether they embrace their destiny or flee from it, their actions will ripple through the world, for better or worse.
Racial Traits:
Etheric Awakening: Aetherborn can instinctively sense nearby Aetherstone and Etheric relics, gaining +2 to Etheria when interacting with these objects.
Noble Heritage: As descendants of the Tartarian elite, Aetherborn have an innate charisma and commanding presence. Gain +1 to Charisma and a reroll when attempting to persuade or intimidate.
Latent Powers: Aetherborn possess latent Etheric abilities tied to their lineage. Once per day, they can activate a surge of Etheric energy to enhance their capabilities, such as speeding up their movements, increasing their strength, or creating a protective barrier for three rounds.
Destiny Unfolding: The longer an Aetherborn interacts with Etheric relics and Aetherstone, the more their powers grow. They can unlock hidden abilities tied to their noble bloodline, but this comes at a cost—prolonged exposure may cause physical mutations or mental instability.
Role in the World:The Aetherborn are hidden heirs to Tartaria’s throne, torn between their ordinary upbringing and extraordinary destiny. As players, they must navigate the complexities of their dual heritage, facing the challenges of their newfound powers while uncovering the secrets of their noble lineage. Whether they embrace their birthright or forge a new path in the modern world, their decisions will have far-reaching consequences, and their powers may change the fate of Tartaria itself.
Achievement-Based Titles
Gaining new skills
The title system adds character depth and personal growth without locking them into a rigid class or profession. Instead of being tied to race or starting choices, titles are awarded based on a player's actions, achievements, and experiences in the world. This allows players to evolve naturally and gain a sense of accomplishment for their unique journey. The Arbiter (the game’s GM) has the flexibility to grant titles at pivotal moments in the game, making each award feel special and earned.
The system is designed to:
Encourage Exploration and Creativity: Titles incentivize players to explore different aspects of the game, such as uncovering secrets, interacting with ancient relics, or mastering skills in unique situations.
Promote Replayability: No two characters will develop similarly since titles are achievement-based. This allows for rich replayability, as players want to unlock different titles or combinations in future sessions.
Add Meaningful Rewards: Each title has a perk, such as a bonus action, skill enhancement, or situational advantage. These perks are designed to enhance gameplay without making characters overpowered, keeping the game balanced.
Customization Without Complexity: Players can craft their own unique characters, allowing their gameplay to reflect their personal achievements rather than selecting a class at the outset.
The Arbiter can use titles to reward characters who’ve completed important story arcs, survived dangerous encounters, or contributed something meaningful to the group's journey. This allows for flexibility in how and when titles are awarded, giving the Arbiter full control over character progression.

Why Race-Specific Backgrounds Work as Role-Playing Mechanisms
Race-specific backgrounds provide players with an optional layer of immersion, connecting their characters' personal histories to the rich lore of Tartaria. These backgrounds deepen the role-playing experience, tying characters more directly to their race’s culture, past, and unique struggles. They help ground characters in the world’s history, making them more than just adventurers—they become active participants in the unfolding mystery of Tartaria’s return.
These backgrounds are entirely optional and intended for players who want to engage in more involved role-playing and storytelling. Players who choose these backgrounds will find them useful for creating personal story arcs, interacting with the world’s factions, and exploring the broader themes of the game. The small perks associated with these backgrounds are designed to reflect the character’s background without overpowering their core abilities. Whether the campaign focuses on action, diplomacy, or exploration, these backgrounds add flavor and narrative depth without complicating the game mechanics.
For campaigns focused on personal growth and immersive storytelling, race-specific backgrounds allow players to connect more intimately with the game world. The Arbiter can use these backgrounds as hooks to create customized plotlines, challenges, or relationships that emphasize the character’s race and history, giving every player a unique journey through Tartaria's forgotten past.

Accomplishment and Equipment-Based Progression System
In the Tartaria Prima system, characters progress through acquiring Titles by accomplishing feats and buying new or upgrading equipment, relics, and faction-specific gear. Rather than a traditional leveling system, advancement comes from carefully choosing, upgrading, and maintaining gear across multiple categories and tackling feats of skill or daring. Players will navigate the game's world by engaging in relic discovery, faction tasks, and black-market dealings to enhance their fame and abilities.
1. Core Mechanics of the Equipment-Based Progression System
In the Tartaria TTRPG, characters gain strength and utility primarily by acquiring and using equipment that fits into specific slots. Equipment is divided into relics, faction items, and black-market items. This allows players to customize their load-outs based on their playstyle, faction alliances, and exploration discoveries.
Equipment Categories:
Relics of Tartaria: Ancient artifacts granting unique abilities or Etheric powers.
Faction-Based Equipment: Gear awarded by factions in exchange for Faction Points (FP), earned through completing tasks for factions like the Reclaimers Guild, Forgotten Order, etc.
Black Market Items: Rare, powerful, and often dangerous items purchased using Tartaria Coins (TC).
2. Equipment Slots Overview
Every character has a set number of slots where they can equip items. These slots are broken down into body slots, weapon slots, and carrying items. Players need to make strategic decisions about how to fill their slots, as they won’t be able to carry everything at once.
Body Slots (6 Total)
Headgear (1 slot): Helmets, visors, or relic-enhanced hats.
Example: Etheric Crown: Boosts Etheric manipulation by +1 and protects against mental effects.
Durability: Long-term, doesn't degrade unless under extreme conditions.
Mask (1 slot): Masks offering stealth, environmental protection, or disguise.
Example: Aetheric Filtration Mask: Filters harmful particles in Etheric anomalies, providing a +1 bonus on saving throws vs. environmental hazards.
Durability: Degrades after prolonged use in harsh environments.
Torso (1 slot): Armor or relic-woven clothing for defense or utility.
Example: Tartarian Combat Vest: Increases defense by +2 and offers small item storage for tools or relics.
Durability: Long-term but can degrade under heavy damage.
Legs (1 slot): Pants or leg armor that boost movement or protection.
Example: Explorer’s Trousers: Adds +1 to agility, reduces penalties for rough terrain navigation.
Durability: Long-term, does not degrade quickly.
Upper Outerwear (1 slot): Jackets, cloaks, or armor plates.
Example: Aetheric Cloak: Grants invisibility for short durations, with risks of Etheric instability over time.
Durability: Limited to 5 uses before degradation.
Carrying Item (Backpack or Satchel): These items allow players to carry extra tools, relics, or consumables.
Small Backpack (3 slots): Holds basic tools or supplies.
Example: Exploration Kit (1 slot): Contains a grappling hook and basic climbing gear.
Large Backpack (5 slots): Holds more but reduces mobility.
Example: Surveyor’s Pack (2 slots): Includes advanced excavation tools and a relic detector.
Satchel (4 slots): Lightweight and balanced option for carrying essentials.
Example: Medic’s Satchel: Holds healing supplies and antidotes for Etheric anomalies.
3. Weapon Slots Overview
Players must carefully manage their weapon choices, with limitations on weapon type. A character cannot wield all weapon types simultaneously, so strategy comes into play when choosing a loadout. You can only have one primary weapon set at a time.
Weapon Slots (4 Total)
Two-Handed Weapon (1 slot): Greatsword, long rifles, polearms.
Example: Ether-Pulse Rifle: A long-range energy weapon that uses Aetherstone for powerful shots.
Durability: 10 shots before recharge is required.
Single-Handed Weapon (2 slots): Pistols, swords, short relic weapons.
Example: Reclaimer’s Blade: A relic-infused sword that grants a +1 bonus to melee combat.
Durability: Long-term but may need repair after heavy use.
Ranged Weapon (1 slot): Bows, crossbows, rifles, or Etheric weapons.
Example: Repeating Crossbow: Fires multiple bolts at short-to-mid range.
Durability: Ammo-based, long-term, but requires bolt replenishment.
4. Item Durability and Lifespan
To add realism and balance, items have varying durability:
Single-Use Items: Consumables like Etheric grenades or stim-packs.
Degrading Items: Armor, shields, and tools that wear down after use. These items will need to be repaired or replaced.
Example: Combat Mask: Protects against environmental hazards but wears down after 3 extreme uses.
Long-Term Relics: Permanent items that don't degrade but may lose power in extreme conditions.
Example: Tartarian Armor Plate: Offers significant defense but may require recharging after absorbing heavy damage.
5. Faction-Based Equipment and Progression
Each faction in the game offers exclusive equipment, but these items must be earned by completing Faction Tasks and gaining Faction Points (FP). As players perform missions for factions, they accumulate FP that can be spent on better gear, tools, and relics.
Faction Points and Tasks
Faction Points (FP): Earned by completing tasks such as retrieving relics, sabotaging, or assisting faction leaders.
Factions include groups like the Reclaimers Guild, Forgotten Order, or Black-Market Brokers. Each faction specializes in certain types of equipment.
Spending Faction Points
Faction gear is only available by spending FP. Higher-tier equipment requires more FP and faction loyalty.
Example Faction Equipment:
Reclaimer’s Grappling Hook: Enables fast traversal in ruins. Costs 10 FP. Durability: 10 uses before repairs are needed.
Forgotten Order’s Etheric Staff: Allows players to manipulate Etheric energy in their surroundings. Costs 20 FP. Long-term, but needs regular recharging.
6. Black Market Equipment and Tartaria Coins (TC)
The Black Market is the go-to place for rare, powerful, and often risky items. Players spend Tartaria Coins (TC) to acquire these items, often paying a high price for immense utility or dangerous effects.
Earning Tartaria Coins (TC)
Players earn TC by selling relics, completing jobs, or trading with NPCs.
TC can be spent on weapons, tools, and consumables that offer high risk but also high reward.
Example Black Market Items:
Etheric Cloak: Provides short-term invisibility but increases the chance of Etheric anomalies (random distortions) when worn. Costs 200 TC. Durability: 3 uses.
Cursed Relic Fragment: Boosts strength but causes fatigue after prolonged use. Costs 300 TC. Durability: Single use with permanent side effects.
7. Equipment Progression and Customization
a. Slot Customization
Players unlock more slots through equipment upgrades or faction rewards as they progress. Slots allow characters to carry specialized gear from factions or upgraded relics.
b. Upgrading Equipment
Players can enhance relics and tools through crafting or faction-specific modifications.
Example Upgrade: Etheric-Powered Grappling Hook: Increases the range by 50%. Requires rare Aetherstone components.
c. Item Utility and Lifespan
Each item provides utility specific to combat, exploration, or survival. Lifespan and degradation add strategic depth, as players must repair, maintain, or replace items after use in extreme environments.
8. Weapon and Equipment Carrying Rules
Players must carefully balance their weapon loadout and carrying items:
Two-Handed Weapons: Use both hands and prevents carrying additional weapons in combat.
Single-Handed Weapon + Shield: A common combination for defensive players.
Ranged + Single-Handed Weapon: Allows ranged combat with a quick switch to melee.

Gear, Racial Perks, and Modifiers: Influencing Outcomes
One of the defining aspects of Tartaria Prima’s system is how gear and racial perks play into each action, offering significant advantages when used strategically. The right combination of tools, equipment, and abilities can turn an impossible task into a manageable one.
For example:
Gear such as an Etheric Grappling Hook might give a +2 bonus when attempting to climb or swing across a chasm.
A True Tartarian character might have the racial perk Aethercraft Mastery, granting a bonus to manipulating Etheric materials, which could help disable a Mud Golem's Etheric defenses.
A Reclaimer equipped with relic-detecting goggles might gain a +2 bonus when searching ancient ruins for hidden traps or artifacts.
These elements add depth to the gameplay, where each roll becomes more than just a chance event—it’s shaped by how well you equip and develop your character.
Everything is a Roll: How Events Unfold
In Tartaria Prima, every challenge—no matter how big or small—is a chance for players to engage with the game world. Whether you’re jumping across a dangerous chasm or trying to hack a forgotten Etheric device, everything is resolved with a dice roll. This turns every moment into a potential tipping point, where success and failure carry real weight.
Here’s an example of how a series of related tasks might unfold in the game:
Scenario: Navigating a Dangerous Mud Chasm
Your party is traveling through the Great Tartary Plains, a barren wasteland covered by hardened Etheric mud. You've reached a deep chasm that must be crossed to reach the ancient ruins you seek. There’s no bridge, but a narrow ledge on the edge of the chasm might allow you to get close enough for a jump.
Task One: Running to the Ledge
The Arbiter sets a Moderate (3) difficulty level for running to the ledge without slipping on the slick Etheric mud. A character with Dexterity +2 and wearing Anti-Slip Boots (+1 bonus) would roll their d20 with a +3 total modifier.
If the player rolls a 6 or higher, they succeed, reaching the ledge. If they fail, they might lose their balance, forcing a secondary roll to avoid falling—where their True Tartarian racial perk of Enhanced Reflexes (+1 to recovery rolls) could give them a critical edge.
Task Two: Jumping the Chasm
The jump is risky, so the Arbiter assigns a Very Hard (5) difficulty, meaning the player needs to roll a 10 or more on their d20 to succeed.
The player, equipped with an Etheric Grappling Hook (+2 bonus for jumps or swings) and having the Agility +3 modifier, would roll their d20 with a +5 modifier.
Success means they land safely on the other side, but if they fail, they might barely grab the ledge. Here, the Arbiter could assign a secondary Hard (4) roll to hold on and climb up—where their racial perk of Etheric Strength (giving +1 to physical feats involving climbing or pulling) might help save them from falling into the depths.
Task Three: Hanging on
After a failed jump, the character manages to grab the ledge, but they’re slipping. The Arbiter assigns a Hard (4) difficulty to pull themselves up, rolling d20 with any Strength modifiers and gear bonuses (perhaps they have climbing gloves or Etheric-powered enhancements).
A failure might mean they drop some gear, forcing a tough decision: let it go or risk another challenge to retrieve it.
Task Four: Retrieving Lost Gear
If a player decides to retrieve dropped gear that fell into the chasm, the Arbiter might set an Impossible (7) difficulty, requiring a perfect 20 roll. This could lead to another tense moment, where they may have to use specialized equipment or invoke a faction bonus from the Forgotten Order, who have tools designed for extracting items from Etheric-charged environments.
A Living World of Consequences
Each action unfolds as part of a chain of events, where every roll matters. Success might mean the party crosses the chasm unscathed, while failure could lead to someone losing valuable gear, falling into danger, or triggering an ancient trap hidden within the ruins. These cascading events create narrative tension, where the outcomes of each roll shape the course of the adventure.
Characters must rely on their gear and racial abilities to gain the upper hand in these moments. A Mud Monarch using their Etheric Cloak of Invisibility might sneak past guards unnoticed, but a tear in the cloak would force a roll to determine if they stay hidden. The Forgotten Order might use an ancient Mud-Tech drill to penetrate through Etheric-charged barriers, but they’d still need to roll for success, with bonuses based on the quality of their equipment.
The constant interplay between dice rolls, modifiers, and story-driven action keeps the game exciting, ensuring that no two challenges are ever resolved the same way.
Dice Pools and Specialized Rolls

d10: Typically used for damage rolls in combat or to determine the scale of success in critical moments, such as breaking through an Etheric shield.
d6: Often used for smaller checks or incremental effects like managing resources, determining environmental hazards, or applying minor rerolls granted by certain perks or gear.
These extra dice add another layer of complexity, making the game feel rich and unpredictable. Damage might vary based on the weapon used—like an Etheric Pulse Rifle, which does additional damage based on the d10 roll—or the roll of a d6 might determine how long an effect like poison or bleeding lasts during combat.
The Core of the Game: A Series of Challenges
At its essence, Tartaria Prima is a game where everything is a roll—a series of challenges connected by the narrative and players' choices. Each task, whether unlocking a Tartarian relic, sneaking past an enemy patrol, or surviving a deadly fall, is resolved through this system of difficulty levels and dice rolls.
With every roll, the outcome of the story shifts. Success opens up new paths, while failure forces players to adapt. The thrill of the game comes from balancing strategy, gear, and racial abilities to tip the odds in your favor. And in a world filled with Etheric energy, ancient technology, and lost civilizations, every decision could mean the difference between life, death, and the discovery of Tartaria’s greatest secrets.
In Tartaria Prima, a typical turn follows a structured flow similar to other TTRPGs but with unique elements that integrate the world’s Etheric power, gear-based bonuses, and racial perks. Players resolve actions by rolling against difficulty levels, leveraging their attributes, gear, and perks to navigate the challenges of Tartaria’s mysterious and dangerous world.
Here’s a breakdown of how a typical Tartaria Prima turn might unfold, especially during combat or a challenging encounter:
1. Start of Turn
Resolve Ongoing Effects: Before the player acts, any ongoing conditions or effects (such as Etheric burns, energy traps, or lingering environmental hazards) are resolved. If the player suffers from damage over time (e.g., from Etheric exposure), this is applied now.
Example: A character exposed to Etheric Storm fallout might take 1d6 Etheric damage at the start of their turn as their Etheric shield begins to crack.
2. Movement
Movement Phase: The player can move up to their character’s speed (usually 30 feet). This can happen before, during, or after their action. Movement can be modified by terrain or gear bonuses.
Example: A character equipped with Magno-Boots (+5 feet to movement speed) can move 35 feet across uneven ground without worrying about slipping on the Etheric-charged mud.
Racial Bonus: A True Tartarian with the Dark Vision perk can move confidently through pitch-black tunnels, allowing them to avoid penalties to movement in low-light areas.
3. Action Phase
During this phase, the player chooses their main action. This could be attacking, using gear, interacting with the environment, or attempting to overcome a challenge. Actions are resolved using the d20 system, where players roll against the difficulty level set by the Arbiter, adding modifiers from attributes, gear, and racial perks.
Common actions include:
Attack: Making a melee or ranged attack against an enemy.
Example: A player attacks a Mud Golem with their Etheric Blade. They roll a d20, adding their Strength modifier (+2) and the Etheric Blade's bonus (+1), and compare it to the difficulty level (say, a Hard difficulty of 8).
Success results in the Mud Golem taking 1d10 Etheric damage as the blade carves through the creature’s hardened mud shell.
Use Etheric Power: Some characters can use Etheric manipulation to harness power from their environment or Etheric cores.
Example: A player tries to tap into a nearby Tartarian Etheric Core to power a defensive shield, rolling against a Very Hard difficulty (10). They roll d20, adding their Etheria modifier (+3) and specialized Aetheric Gloves bonus (+2).
Success results in activating the core, surrounding the party with a protective Etheric shield.
Interact with Environment: Players may interact with objects like pulling a lever, activating an ancient Tartarian device, or disarming traps.
Example: Disarming an Etheric tripwire (Difficulty 6). A Forgotten Order engineer uses their Etheric Tools (+1) and rolls d20, adding their Dexterity modifier (+2).
Use Gear: Players can use various tools, relics, or gear they’ve acquired to perform specific tasks or gain bonuses.
Example: A Reclaimer might use a relic-detecting scanner to locate a hidden artifact, rolling against a Moderate difficulty (6) while adding their Intelligence modifier and tool bonus.
4. Bonus Action
Some characters, depending on their race, faction, or gear, can perform a bonus action in addition to their main action. This might involve quick tactical decisions, activating relics, or triggering secondary effects.
Racial Bonus Example: A Mud Monarch might use their Royal Protocol Device to issue a quick command, forcing a nearby enemy to reroll their next attack as a bonus action.
Gear Bonus Example: A Reclaimer with a Wrist-Mounted Grapple might use it as a bonus action to zip across the battlefield, gaining repositioning after their main action.
5. Free Action
Players can perform small actions that don’t require a roll or significant effort, such as drawing a weapon, activating a gadget, or interacting with minor objects.
Example: A player activates their Mud-Tech Gauntlet as a free action, preparing to use its energy-draining ability in the next turn.
6. End of Turn
At the end of the turn, players resolve any conditions or effects that might occur after their actions.
Etheric Effects: A character trapped in an Etheric anomaly might need to roll against a Hard difficulty (8) at the end of their turn to escape before taking further damage.
Racial Bonus: A Tartarian Giant Descendant may have the ability to roll a saving throw at the end of their turn to resist the effects of being stunned, thanks to their Resilient Bloodline perk.
7. Reactions
Reactions allow players to act outside of their turn in response to certain triggers. This could be dodging an incoming attack, making an opportunity attack, or reacting to a triggered event.
Example: A Forgotten Order operative might trigger their Shield Generator when attacked, using a reaction to gain temporary protection from an Etheric blast.
Opportunity Attack: If an enemy attempts to flee, a Mud Monarch's bodyguard might strike using their Etheric Pike, rolling d20 against the enemy’s escape difficulty.

Let’s break down an exciting scenario in the world of Tartaria Prima: your party is crossing an ancient Etheric bridge over a chasm, and it begins to collapse. You need to make a quick escape before being swallowed by the mud below. Here’s how a typical turn might play out:
Start of Turn: The Arbiter rules that the bridge is collapsing quickly. Each player must roll against a Moderate difficulty (6) to avoid taking structural damage (1d6) from falling debris before their turn begins. Success means you make it across without trouble. Failure causes you to slip, leading to a secondary roll to avoid falling entirely.
Action: Seeing that your teammate is struggling to make it across, you use your Etheric Grappling Hook to help them. The Arbiter sets a Moderate difficulty (6) to successfully hook the other side and pull them up. You roll d20, adding your Strength modifier (+2) and gear bonus (+2).
Success allows you to assist your teammate in reaching safety, while failure might leave them hanging by a thread.
Bonus Action: You quickly activate your Shield Generator as a bonus action, knowing more debris could fall next turn. This provides temporary protection from Etheric energy for you and your team.
Free Action: You shout a warning to the others as part of a free action, helping them prepare for the next set of rolls or danger ahead.
End of Turn: The Mud Golem chasing the party is still lumbering behind, and you resolve any final effects or saving throws before ending your turn.
Reaction: As the bridge gives way, an opportunity arises to strike the Mud Golem trying to cross. Using your reaction, you unleash an Etheric blast from your gauntlet, attempting to collapse the bridge section beneath the Golem.

Common Conditions and Status Effects
1. Stunned
Description: The character is temporarily incapacitated, overwhelmed by an impact or force, rendering them unable to act.
Effect:
Cannot take any actions (including attacks, casting abilities, or using items) for the duration of the stun.
Loses any reactions during the stunned period.
Attacks against stunned characters have advantage (or receive a bonus to hit, depending on your combat system).
Duration: Typically lasts 1 round, depending on the source of the stun; The Arbiter can extend the effect.
2. Paralyzed
Description: The character is completely immobilized, unable to move or perform any actions due to a physical or magical effect.
Effect:
Cannot move, speak, or take actions.
Automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws or checks.
Melee attacks against a paralyzed character automatically hit and are critical hits if the attacker is within melee range.
Ranged attacks have advantage against the paralyzed character.
Duration: Until the condition is cured or 5 rounds, depending on the source of paralysis, The Arbiter can extend the effect.
3. Poisoned
Description: The character's body is ravaged by toxins, leading to weakness and impaired functionality.
Effect:
Disadvantage on all attack rolls, skill checks, and saving throws.
Poisons deal ongoing damage at the start of each round 1d6  poison damage per turn.
Duration: Until cured by an antidote, magic, or after a certain period  10 rounds.
4. Bleeding
Description: The character suffers from a serious wound that causes them to lose health over time.
Effect:
At the start of the affected character's turn, they take ongoing damage 1d6 per round.
Any healing or first aid can stop the bleeding and end the condition.
Duration: Until healing is applied or a successful medical check is made.
5. Blinded
Description: The character’s vision is obscured, making it difficult to perceive their surroundings.
Effect:
Disadvantages of attack rolls and skill checks that rely on sight.
All attacks against the blinded character have the advantage.
The character automatically fails ability checks that require sight (such as Perception checks for spotting enemies).
Duration: 10 rounds, or the character restores their vision with any advanced health kit.
6. Deafened
Description: The character cannot hear, affecting their awareness of their surroundings.
Effect:
Automatically fails any skill checks related to hearing.
Disadvantage on Perception checks to detect threats (e.g., when trying to hear an ambush).
Duration: 5 rounds, or the character restores their vision with any advanced health kit.
7. Charmed
Description: The character is under the influence of an ether or mental effect that alters their attitude toward the source of the charm.
Effect:
The charmed character cannot harm the source of the charm and views them as a trusted ally.
Advantage on social interactions and Persuasion checks made by the source of the charm.
The charmed character may act against their party if the source commands it, depending on the nature of the charm.
Duration: 5 rounds, or the character restores their vision with any etheric relic the Arbiter feels can help.
8. Frightened
Description: The character is overwhelmed by fear, causing them to avoid the source of their terror.
Effect:
Disadvantage on all attack rolls and skill checks while the source of fear is within line of sight.
The character cannot move closer to the source of the fear and must attempt to flee if possible.
Duration: Until the fear is resolved (e.g., passing a saving throw) or after a set duration 5 rounds.
9. Exhausted
Description: The character is physically and mentally drained, unable to perform at full potential.
Effect: Exhaustion is typically tracked in levels. Each level increases the severity of the condition:
Level 1: Disadvantage on ability checks.
Level 2: Speed is halved.
Level 3: Disadvantage on attack rolls and saving throws.
Level 4: Hit point maximum is halved.
Level 5: Speed reduced to 0.
Level 6: Death.
Duration: Reduced with food and drink or healing kit to 3 rounds, 10 without.
10. Petrified
Description: The character is transformed into stone, becoming a statue.
Effect:
The character is incapacitated and cannot move or take actions.
The character becomes an object, immune to poison and disease but vulnerable to shattering.
Attackers have advantage on attacks against the petrified character.
Duration: Typically until cured by mud or ether magic/relics, though some effects have a set duration.
11. Restrained
Description: The character is bound or otherwise hindered in their movements, severely limiting their mobility.
Effect:
Speed is reduced to 0, and the character cannot move.
Disadvantages of Dexterity-based skill checks and saving throws.
Attacks against the restrained character have an advantage, while the restrained character has a disadvantage on attack rolls.
Duration: Until the character breaks free with a Strength check or is rescued.
12. Incapacitated
Description: The character is unable to take actions due to a mental or physical effect that prevents them from functioning.
Effect:
The character cannot take actions, reactions, or bonus actions.
They are still conscious and aware but unable to act.
Duration: Until the effect ends 5 rounds or healed.
General Notes on Conditions and Status Effects
Duration: The duration of each condition depends on its source (e.g., magical effects, environmental hazards, or poison). Some effects last a set number of rounds, while others require specific actions to remove (like healing or saving throws).
Resisting Effects: Some conditions allow the character to make a saving throw (e.g., a Constitution save for Poisoned, or a Wisdom save for Charmed) to avoid or reduce the effect.
Stacking Conditions: Conditions can stack in some cases. For example, a character could be both Poisoned and Blinded, suffering the penalties of both conditions simultaneously.

Money and Economy in Tartaria Prima
In Tartaria Prima, the economy operates using two main forms of currency: Tartarian Coins (TC) and Faction Points (FP). These represent the backbone of the financial and social economy, determining a character's purchasing power, influence, and access to resources. Players will encounter traditional shops, black markets, and barter systems when trading items and relics in this world, and how they use TC and FP will have significant impacts on their progression.
Tartarian Coins (TC)
Tartarian Coins (TC) are the primary currency used throughout the world, exchanged for goods, services, and equipment. The rarity and Etheric power behind Tartarian relics make TC a highly valuable form of currency, circulating between factions, traders, and NPCs alike. TC is the most common currency for day-to-day transactions and is essential for acquiring basic gear, consumables, and services from NPCs or shops.
Using Tartarian Coins (TC):
Buying Items: Players can use TC to purchase common items, equipment, weapons, armor, and consumables such as healing potions or food from merchants or shops.
Upgrading Gear: TC can be used to pay for minor upgrades, repairs, or modifications to weapons and armor at a blacksmith or specialist vendor.
Services: Players can spend TC on services such as hiring mercenaries, paying for transport, medical treatment, or staying at inns.
Selling Items: Players can sell most items to NPCs or shops, typically for 50% of the item's original value (rounded up). More valuable or rare items, such as relics or Etheric devices, might fetch a higher price in specialty shops or when sold to collectors or factions.
Example: A player wants to sell an old weapon with a value of 100 TC. When selling it back to an NPC, the shopkeeper offers 50 TC for it, which is 50% of its value. However, they may find a black market buyer willing to pay slightly more, depending on the item's rarity.
Currency Breakdown:
1 TC = 1 Tartarian Coin: The standard unit of exchange.
100 TC = 1 Etheric Shard: A rare and high-value unit of currency used primarily for large-scale purchases or trades involving relics or valuable Etheric devices.
Faction Points (FP)
Faction Points (FP) are a non-monetary form of social currency that represent a character’s reputation, influence, and standing with the various factions operating in Tartaria. Unlike TC, FP cannot be traded or used for normal purchases, but they are vital for gaining access to faction-specific resources, gear, and special abilities. The higher a player's FP with a faction, the more valuable the benefits they can unlock.
Using Faction Points (FP):
Gaining Favor: FP allows players to gain the favor of specific factions (e.g., the Mud Monarchs, Forgotten Order, or Reclaimers Guild). The more FP a character accumulates with a faction, the more trust and resources they can access.
Unlocking Faction Rewards: Players can "spend" FP to unlock special faction-related gear, weapons, missions, and unique abilities. For example, a player with high FP in the Forgotten Order might unlock access to advanced Etheric technology.
Faction Missions: Certain missions or opportunities are only available to players with enough FP in a particular faction. Completing these missions often yields rare rewards, relics, or powerful allies.
Example: A player with 10 FP in the Reclaimers Guild might be able to request a high-risk mission that offers valuable Etheric relics as rewards. A player with higher FP may gain access to black market discounts or unique Reclaimer tech.
Earning Faction Points (FP):
Completing Faction-Specific Missions: Players can earn FP by completing quests and tasks that directly benefit a faction.
Donating Valuable Relics: Donating rare relics or Etheric devices to a faction can earn significant FP, sometimes more valuable than selling the items for TC.
Influence in Storylines: Characters that align themselves with certain factions and play pivotal roles in faction-specific events or conflicts can earn large FP rewards.
Selling Items Back to NPCs and Shops
Players will often acquire gear, relics, and other items during their adventures. While many of these items can be useful, some will be sold to NPCs or shops for Tartarian Coins (TC) to fund new purchases or upgrades.
Selling Items:
Standard Items: Most common items, weapons, and armor can be sold to shops or NPCs for 50% of their purchase value. This standard exchange rate applies to day-to-day merchants and general stores.
Rare or Relic Items: Certain rare relics, Etheric devices, or powerful artifacts may be sold for a higher price. Specialty buyers or factions interested in these rare items may offer 75% to 100% of the item’s value, depending on its rarity and condition.
Black Markets: Some locations, such as hidden markets or underground trading posts, may offer players a better deal for rare items, especially those tied to Tartarian technology or forbidden knowledge. Black market buyers might offer more than normal NPCs but at a risk of reputation loss or dangerous consequences.
Example of Selling Items:
A Common Sword (value 100 TC) can be sold back to a merchant for 50 TC.
An Etheric Blade Relic (value 400 TC) may fetch 300 TC at a relic shop or with a faction buyer who specializes in Etheric technology.
Trading and Barter:
In some cases, especially in remote or faction-heavy regions, players may encounter NPCs or groups that prefer bartering over TC. In these instances, players can exchange items directly, trading gear or relics of equal value.
Barter Example: A Reclaimer may trade a basic Etheric device for a rare map fragment to help them on their quest, skipping the need for TC entirely.
Economic Strategies in Tartaria Prima
Balancing TC and FP: Players will need to strategically balance their use of TC for everyday purchases with the accumulation of FP to unlock special faction perks and missions. TC will be essential for survival, while FP will unlock faction-specific advantages and powerful relics.
Rare Relic Market: Relics and Etheric devices are rare and valuable, and players can choose to either sell these items for large amounts of TC or donate them to factions to gain FP. This decision will impact their access to high-level faction rewards.
Dynamic NPC Shops: Prices and availability of items may vary depending on the region, faction control, or local events. NPCs in some areas might have scarce supplies, driving up costs, while black markets or allied factions may offer discounts or exclusive items to players with high FP.
Example of Economy in Action:
A Mud Dweller with 500 TC wants to purchase an advanced piece of Etheric armor worth 600 TC. They lack the funds but have 8 FP with the Forgotten Order. By completing a side mission for the Forgotten Order, they gain another 2 FP and unlock a 15% discount, making the armor more affordable. Alternatively, they could trade a rare relic they found during their mission for 300 TC to cover the remaining cost, but doing so would lose potential FP gains.

Technology and Magic:
- Etheric Power:  
At the heart of Tartaria's technological prowess was Etheric Power, an advanced energy system harvested from the very atmosphere. Tartarian engineers had mastered the ability to draw limitless energy from the air using the towering spires and domes that defined their architecture. These structures were far more than ornamental; they were intricately designed to function as energy receivers and conduits, channeling power from the Ether, a mysterious, ever-present energy source. Etheric Power fueled the entire empire, driving everything from their machines to their daily lives.
Though the Mud Flood buried most of Tartaria’s infrastructure, Etheric Power still flows through many surviving structures, lying dormant within forgotten capitals, lost temples, and the remnants of ancient cities. Even after centuries, the technology is so advanced that much of it remains functional, albeit inactive. For the Forgotten Order, reactivating this power is the key to humanity’s future, while the Mud Monarchs fiercely guard what little Etheric knowledge they still possess. Many factions, especially the Tartarian Revivalists, believe that reclaiming Etheric Power could change the course of history once more, and they hunt for the tools and schematics hidden in the lost temples to reactivate these systems.
However, reactivating Etheric Power is a double-edged sword. When improperly handled, the power can overload, leading to local disasters, much like the cataclysm that caused the original Mud Flood. This makes accessing and controlling Etheric Power as dangerous as it is valuable.
Mud Tech:  
Mud Tech is a crude and dangerous branch of technology that emerged in the aftermath of the Great Mud Flood. The dense, Etheric-charged mud that encased the Tartarian cities altered the environment in strange and unpredictable ways, infusing the Earth with residual Etheric energies. Over time, some factions have managed to develop rudimentary technologies by tapping into these residual energies, creating a new field of technology known as Mud Tech. Though functional, these inventions are notoriously unstable, prone to catastrophic failure, and pale in comparison to the sophisticated Etheric systems of the ancient Tartarian Empire.
Examples of Mud Tech innovations include:
- Mud Reactors:  
  Makeshift power generators that extract energy from the Etheric-charged mud. These reactors are used by some outlying settlements and Reclaimer camps to power crude machinery or provide heat in the cold wastelands. However, Mud Reactors are highly unreliable, prone to overheating or producing energy surges that can destroy nearby equipment. It's not uncommon for these reactors to fail spectacularly, leading to dangerous explosions or toxic mud spills.
- Mud Forgers:  
  Basic forges powered by the residual Etheric energy in the mud, used to create crude weapons and tools from Etheric-infused metals. These weapons, while powerful, often possess erratic properties. For example, a Mud Blade may hum with unstable energy, delivering unpredictable bursts of power, but risks disintegrating after a few uses. Mud Forgers are typically used by Reclaimers and outlaws who seek quick, powerful weapons, regardless of the risks.
- Mud Golem Constructors:  
  In certain hidden labs or temples, some factions have experimented with creating Mud Golems, using the Etheric-charged mud to mold and animate these creatures. The golems are often unstable and difficult to control, and their creators risk being attacked by their own creations. These golems are used by desperate Reclaimers or as a last-ditch defense by secretive cults, but the golems' volatile nature makes them as much of a threat to their summoners as to their enemies.
- Mud Drones:  
  Simple mechanical constructs crafted from scavenged materials and powered by the energy within the mud. Mud Drones are used as scouts by factions like the Forgotten Order to explore buried Tartarian ruins where humans dare not tread. While useful for limited reconnaissance, these drones often malfunction, with their guidance systems going haywire or their Etheric power cells overloading, causing them to self-destruct.
- Mud Barriers:  
  A rudimentary form of energy shield crafted by certain Reclaimer sects. By harnessing Etheric mud’s properties, Mud Barriers can be erected to defend temporary camps or excavation sites. These barriers can absorb energy attacks or physical impacts but are unstable, sometimes collapsing without warning or sending a disruptive energy shockwave through nearby equipment. Use of these barriers is risky, as the mud’s energy is prone to fluctuation.
The energy drawn from the mud is volatile, and users of Mud Tech often find themselves battling against the technology as much as they use it. For example, a Reclaimer using a Mud Rifle might experience incredible bursts of energy, only to have the weapon jam or explode in their hands after a few shots. Similarly, vehicles powered by Mud Engines have been known to surge forward uncontrollably or shut down mid-operation, trapping their operators in dangerous situations.
In the remote backcountry, legends tell of Mud Golems—elemental creatures believed to have been created accidentally through failed Mud Tech experiments. These beings, formed from the Etheric-charged mud, possess incredible strength and resistance to physical attacks, but their behavior is unpredictable. Some see them as mindless brutes, while others believe the mud itself is sentient, corrupted by the lingering effects of Etheric Power. While some factions have attempted to control these golems as weapons, their volatile nature makes this extremely dangerous, as the golems often turn on their creators.
Both the Mud Monarchs and the Conspiracy Architects view Mud Tech as an affront to the refined Etheric technologies they seek to control. The Monarchs see it as a threat to their dominance, fearing that widespread use of unstable Mud Tech could lead to further disasters and undermine their carefully guarded knowledge of Tartarian relics. The Forgotten Order, on the other hand, takes a more pragmatic approach. While they recognize Mud Tech’s instability, they view it as a necessary, if inferior, means for survival in areas where true Etheric Power is inaccessible.
Despite the risks, Mud Tech continues to evolve in the hands of factions desperate for any advantage in the ongoing struggle to reclaim Tartaria’s lost legacy. Those who wield it walk a fine line between harnessing the remnants of a fallen empire and unleashing the chaotic forces that once destroyed it.
- Architectural Sorcery:  
Tartarian architecture was more than just a triumph of engineering—it was a form of Architectural Sorcery. The Tartarians designed their buildings to channel and focus magical energy in ways that seamlessly blended with their technological advancements. Domes, arches, and geometric structures were specifically constructed to manipulate Etheric energy, serving as both shelters and powerful energy conduits.
The art of Architectural Sorcery allowed Tartarian engineers to not only build monumental structures but also imbue them with magical properties. Etheric energy flowed through these buildings, enhancing their strength, stability, and even self-repair capabilities. Some Tartarian buildings were rumored to have defensive properties, where the very walls could repel invaders or cast intruders into disorienting illusions. 
The Stone Builders, a religious and scientific order, still study the intricacies of Architectural Sorcery, believing that understanding the connection between structure and energy is the key to unlocking Tartaria’s full potential. However, recreating this ancient magic requires not only precise knowledge of Tartarian schematics but also access to functioning Etheric Power sources, which are rare and difficult to find. The Forgotten Order and Stone Builders are working to reverse-engineer these ancient methods, but the secrets of Architectural Sorcery remain elusive, buried beneath layers of mud and time.
- Mud Magic:  
A darker, more primitive form of energy manipulation exists in the form of Mud Magic. Survivors of the Mud Flood and those who dwell beneath the surface, like the Mud Dwellers, have learned to harness the residual energies left behind by the flood. By manipulating the strange properties of the Ether-charged mud, these survivors can alter their environment, create golems from the mud, and even heal wounds.
However, the use of Mud Magic comes at a great cost. Prolonged use warps the body and mind, leading to mutations, madness, or worse. Mud Magic practitioners often find themselves twisted by the very energies they seek to control. Their skin may harden and crack like the mud they command, or their minds may become consumed by the residual echoes of Tartaria’s downfall. Some practitioners develop the ability to shift the terrain itself, forming walls, barriers, or pits out of mud with a thought. Others summon Mud Golems, though these creations are difficult to control and often turn on their masters.
While some factions, such as the Servants of the Giants, revere Mud Magic as a divine gift from the Tartarian Giants, others fear its corrupting influence. The Forgotten Order considers it a dangerous offshoot of Etheric Power, while the Conspiracy Architects actively suppress its use, fearing the chaos it could bring if widely adopted. Mud Magic remains a mysterious and dangerous art, practiced only by those willing to risk the consequences of tampering with the raw forces left in the wake of Tartaria’s destruction.
7. Economy:
- Relic Trade:  
The Relic Trade is one of the most lucrative yet dangerous sectors in the underground economy. Tartarian relics—particularly those tied to Etheric Power—are highly sought after on the black market. These artifacts range from simple tools and trinkets to advanced energy conduits capable of tapping into the dormant Etheric networks hidden beneath the surface. Governments, private collectors, and factions such as the Reclaimers Guild are in constant competition to obtain these relics, each for their own purposes.
The Mud Monarchs maintain strict control over the most valuable relics, using their wealth and influence to keep the most powerful technologies out of public hands. They regularly employ mercenaries and Reclaimers to search for specific items, while simultaneously working to suppress any widespread knowledge of these artifacts. The Forgotten Order, by contrast, views the relics as keys to reviving Tartaria’s ancient technologies, and they frequently clash with the Monarchs over access to these treasures. 
Relics related to Etheric Power are the most valuable, capable of harnessing vast amounts of energy when properly restored. However, the dangers of mishandling these items are significant. Improperly activating a relic can result in localized disasters, from electrical surges to building collapses. Nonetheless, the risk is worth it to those who understand the true power these relics hold.
- Mud Resources:  
Beyond the relic trade, Mud Resources are another crucial and dangerous aspect of Tartaria's buried economy. The mud that encases Tartarian cities is rich with strange minerals and substances, many of which have been altered by centuries of exposure to Etheric energy. These resources can be harvested and used to create powerful but unstable technologies, including weapons, machinery, and even experimental energy sources.
The Reclaimers and certain factions within the Reclaimers Guild often specialize in extracting these resources from deep within the buried ruins, selling them to buyers on the black market or using them to craft Mud Tech. However, this extraction is perilous. The Etheric-charged mud has unpredictable properties, and entire mining crews have been lost to mudslides, energy surges, or mysterious disappearances.
Despite the risks, the rewards are immense. Certain minerals found within the mud can be used to enhance Etheric machines, while others possess unique properties such as superconductivity or energy storage. Some factions, like the Stone Builders, prize these materials for use in reconstructing Tartarian architecture, while others, like the Mud Monarchs, hoard them for their own covert experiments. The Mud Dwellers, who have lived beneath the surface for generations, are particularly adept at scavenging these resources, using them to survive and barter with the surface factions.
- City-State Barter Systems:  
Above-ground cities that were once part of Tartaria’s empire now trade in both modern goods and the remnants of Tartarian technology. In these city-states, a unique barter system has developed, particularly between factions that control access to Tartarian relics and knowledge. Cities like Paris, London, New York, Vienna, Istanbul, and Mexico City operate complex economies where old-world goods are traded alongside relics and knowledge.
Factions like the Forgotten Order, the Reclaimers Guild, and the Mud Dwellers often engage in bartering, exchanging not just relics but also valuable information about hidden Tartarian sites, access to dangerous locations, or services like excavation and relic restoration. While governments officially deny the existence of Tartaria’s hidden technologies, behind the scenes, many officials partake in this secret economy, using intermediaries to acquire the artifacts they covet.
For the Reclaimers, these city-states serve as hubs where they can sell the relics they’ve unearthed or exchange them for tools and equipment needed for further exploration. The Mud Dwellers, who rarely surface, use these cities as trading posts, exchanging underground resources and scavenged relics for food, medicine, and other essentials.
- Unknowing Masses Economy:  
For the vast majority of the Unknowing Masses, the economy is as it appears—based on typical goods and services similar to those found in modern society today. They buy and sell products such as food, clothing, electronics, and vehicles, completely unaware of the hidden economies that revolve around Tartarian relics and Mud Tech. 
In the everyday world, major cities function with familiar economic structures—banks, corporations, trade industries, and global markets thrive on the surface level. Many citizens unknowingly use infrastructure built atop Tartarian foundations, living in cities that were once grand Tartarian capitals. However, their lives are defined by the surface-level economy, and they remain blissfully ignorant of the powerful technologies lying dormant beneath their feet.
From a distance, the Unknowing Masses benefit indirectly from the presence of Tartarian relics, as the Mud Monarchs and certain global powers use these relics to advance their wealth and control. But for most, the economy is based on the exchange of modern goods, driven by the global markets they understand—oblivious to the black-market relic trade and the powerful forces shaping their world from the shadows.
8. Conflict:
- Reclamation vs. Suppression:
Before the Great Mud Flood in the mid-1500s, Tartaria was a flourishing global empire, unparalleled in its technological mastery of Etheric Power. Its cities spanned continents, and its influence was felt in every corner of the known world. Tartaria’s ruling class, the Tartarian Giants, maintained peace and prosperity through their advanced knowledge of architecture and energy manipulation. However, their ambition to push the limits of Etheric Power triggered an unforeseen cataclysm—the Great Mud Flood.
The flood buried much of the empire beneath layers of impenetrable mud, wiping out most of Tartaria's knowledge and technology. Those who survived either fled to the tops of their remaining cities, forming the basis of what would later become the Mud Monarchs, or became lost to history. As centuries passed, the Mud Monarchs worked to consolidate their power, suppressing all knowledge of Tartaria and using the remnants of its technology to maintain control over a new world order.
In the current age, the conflict between the Forgotten Order and the Mud Monarchs represents the crux of the struggle between Reclamation and Suppression. The Forgotten Order, a secret society formed by descendants of Tartarian engineers and scholars, believes that unlocking Tartaria’s ancient secrets—especially Etheric Power—is the key to humanity’s future. They seek to reclaim the lost cities, temples, and relics buried beneath the mud, aiming to revive Tartaria’s glory and restore the world to its former heights of technological advancement.
On the other side, the Mud Monarchs and their allies, the Conspiracy Architects, are determined to suppress any attempts to unearth Tartaria’s legacy. They believe that humanity is not ready for the power Tartaria wielded and fear that unleashing Etheric Power could trigger another disaster. These factions are locked in a covert war, battling for control of Tartarian relics, knowledge, and technology. The Reclaimers, a group of opportunistic treasure hunters, often find themselves caught in the crossfire, selling artifacts to both sides as they explore the buried ruins.
The constant push-and-pull between these factions defines the modern struggle. The Forgotten Order grows more desperate as they uncover more of Tartaria’s secrets, while the Mud Monarchs tighten their grip, using political and financial power to manipulate the world’s governments and prevent the resurgence of Tartarian technology.
- Tartarian Revival:
In the centuries following the Mud Flood, myths about the Tartarian Giants and their return began to circulate among certain religious sects. These stories gave birth to the Servants of the Giants, a fanatical faction that believes the Tartarian Giants will one day return and reclaim the world. They view the giants as gods and see themselves as the protectors of Tartaria’s legacy, performing strange rituals in the hope of awakening the dormant power within the ancient buildings.
The Servants of the Giants are not the only group that seeks Tartaria’s return. The Tartarian Revivalists, a more radical faction, take a far more aggressive approach. They believe that the only way to restore the lost empire is to bring about a new cataclysm—another Great Reset. Unlike the Servants, who await divine intervention, the Revivalists seek to actively trigger another global upheaval, whether by reactivating old Tartarian energy systems or by causing a geopolitical disaster that will destabilize the world and create a vacuum for Tartaria to rise again.
The Tartarian Revivalists clash not only with the Mud Monarchs and Conspiracy Architects, who fear their reckless ambitions, but also with the Forgotten Order, who see the Revivalists as too extreme. While the Forgotten Order seeks to carefully unlock Tartaria’s technology for the betterment of humanity, the Revivalists want to force a return to the old empire at any cost—even if it means causing another catastrophe.
The Revivalists have staged several high-profile attacks on key locations that house Tartarian relics, attempting to force the world’s hand. Meanwhile, the Servants of the Giants maintain a quieter but equally fervent approach, seeking to preserve the relics and temples of Tartaria for the return of their divine rulers. These two factions, though both focused on Tartaria’s resurgence, are often at odds due to their differing methods.
- Second Mud Flood:
In the modern era, whispers of a Second Mud Flood have begun to circulate. Some believe that reactivating Tartaria’s old energy systems—particularly the vast Etheric Power networks that once powered the empire—will trigger another flood. These concerns are not unfounded. The original Mud Flood was caused by a catastrophic overload of Etheric Power, and many fear that tampering with these ancient systems could destabilize the delicate balance that holds the world together.
Others, however, believe that the looming threat of a second flood is tied to natural forces, not human actions. Climate change, seismic activity, and other global environmental phenomena could lead to a repeat of the cataclysm that buried Tartaria. These natural disasters, combined with the destabilizing effects of Etheric Power, present a ticking clock—one that several factions are racing to beat.
The Forgotten Order believes that harnessing Tartaria’s power is the only way to prevent another disaster. By reactivating and controlling Etheric Power, they hope to stabilize the planet’s environment and avert a second Mud Flood. However, the Mud Monarchs argue that reactivating these ancient systems is precisely what could trigger the catastrophe they seek to avoid. The Conspiracy Architects are particularly focused on preventing the global public from discovering this potential disaster, fearing mass panic and a collapse of the existing world order.
Meanwhile, the Tartarian Revivalists see the potential second flood as a necessary event, a purging of the old world to make way for the new. They actively work to accelerate this disaster, believing that it will reset the world and allow them to rebuild Tartaria from the ground up. The Servants of the Giants, on the other hand, believe that the second flood is a divine event, foretold in their religious texts, and that it will herald the return of the Tartarian Giants to lead the world into a new golden age.
As the planet teeters on the brink of environmental and technological disaster, the race between these factions intensifies. Some seek to harness Tartaria’s old power to save humanity, while others aim to trigger a new catastrophe to reshape the world in their image. In the shadows, the Unknowing Masses continue to live their lives, unaware of the monumental struggle taking place beneath the surface, ignorant of the forces that may soon change their world forever.
Daily Life:
- Surface Dwellers:  
The Surface Dwellers make up the vast majority of the world’s population, living their lives completely unaware of the ancient Tartarian technology buried beneath them. These people live in bustling cities like Paris, London, New York, and Istanbul, where the remnants of Tartaria's empire form the foundations of their modern societies. To most, these cities are simply historical landmarks or marvels of classical architecture. The towering stone buildings, with their domes and spires, are admired for their beauty but are seen as relics of a forgotten era—not as the technological marvels they truly are.
Daily life for Surface Dwellers is similar to what we experience today, driven by the typical economy of modern goods and services. People go to work, shop, and engage in daily routines, blissfully unaware of the power struggles and hidden wars being fought beneath their feet. However, there are rumors—whispers of strange occurrences in the oldest buildings, where lights flicker without explanation or strange sounds echo through long-forgotten corridors. These stories, dismissed as ghost tales or urban legends, are small glimpses of the dormant Tartarian energy still humming through the architecture.
For the Unknowing Masses, life is dictated by what they can see and understand. The technology they use is basic compared to the Etheric Power still flowing through Tartarian structures. However, they unknowingly benefit from the covert influence of the Mud Monarchs, who use Tartarian technology to manipulate the global economy and political landscape. Despite this, the everyday person remains unaware of the true history and power hidden just beneath the surface.
- Underground Dwellers:  
Beneath the bustling cities of the Surface Dwellers lies an entirely different world—the world of the Underground Dwellers. These individuals, including the mutated Mud Dwellers and secret societies like the Forgotten Order and Servants of the Giants, live in the shadows of the old Tartarian empire. Their lives are shaped by the constant power struggles between factions, all vying for control of Tartaria’s lost technologies.
The Mud Dwellers have adapted to life in the dark, labyrinthine ruins buried beneath the mud. These tunnels and forgotten structures are filled with relics and fragments of ancient technology, much of it still active in unpredictable ways. The Mud Dwellers scavenge what they can, living off the remnants of Tartaria’s empire. They worship the Tartarian Giants as divine beings and see the relics they find as sacred objects. Their survival depends on their ability to navigate the dangerous terrain, avoiding ancient defenses like the Architectural Sentinels and other hidden dangers.
Meanwhile, secret societies like the Forgotten Order live a life dedicated to discovery and preservation. Their underground enclaves are filled with research labs, ancient libraries, and small-scale attempts to reactivate Tartarian technology. Members of the Forgotten Order lead lives of secrecy and scholarship, constantly searching for the lost temples and Etheric Power systems that could reshape the world. Their existence is marked by tension, as they are in constant conflict with the Mud Monarchs, who send agents to infiltrate and sabotage their operations.
Life underground is fraught with danger, but it also offers rewards for those brave enough to delve into Tartaria’s ruins. The ancient relics that lie buried here could hold the key to unimaginable power, but they come with the risk of unleashing forces no one fully understands.
- Reclaimers:  
The Reclaimers are wanderers, mercenaries, and adventurers who live on the edge of society, making a dangerous living scavenging the ruins of Tartaria. These individuals are some of the few who understand the true scale of what lies buried beneath the mud. They navigate through abandoned Tartarian cities and forgotten temples, searching for relics they can sell on the black market or trade to the highest bidder.
Life as a Reclaimer is dangerous and unpredictable. They face not only the ancient defenses still active within Tartarian ruins, such as the Architectural Sentinels, but also the hostile terrain itself. The mud that buried Tartaria has become infused with Etheric energy, making it both a source of power and a deadly hazard. Mudslides, sudden energy surges, and unpredictable environmental changes are common in the regions where Tartarian relics are buried.
Reclaimers also live in constant fear of being caught in the crossfire between factions. Both the Forgotten Order and the Mud Monarchs seek to control Tartaria’s relics, and Reclaimers often find themselves working as mercenaries for one faction or the other—or both, depending on who pays more. The Conspiracy Architects hunt them down, working to destroy any relics they uncover before they can be sold or used. On top of this, Reclaimers must contend with rival scavengers, roving gangs, and traps left by ancient Tartarians to protect their buried treasures.
Despite the risks, the rewards of being a Reclaimer are immense. Tartarian relics are highly prized on the black market, and a successful haul can make a Reclaimer rich—if they survive. Some Reclaimers dream of using the artifacts they find to gain power, while others simply see it as a way to make a living in a world where danger lurks beneath every footstep. Their lives are nomadic, moving between ruined cities, underground marketplaces, and secret factional outposts in search of their next big find.
10. Story Hooks:
1. The Awakening of Tartaria:  
A newly discovered Tartarian building is unearthed beneath the ruins of a forgotten city, its spires and domes gleaming beneath layers of mud. Its Etheric Power systems are still operational, and it could be the key to unlocking the full potential of Tartarian technology. However, multiple factions, including the Forgotten Order, Tartarian Revivalists, and Conspiracy Architects, converge on the site, each with their own motives. Players must decide whether to awaken the building's power, possibly triggering another Mud Flood, or destroy it to prevent further catastrophe. Along the way, they will face ancient traps, rival factions, and the last remaining Architectural Sentinel defending the ruins.
2. The Mud Monarch’s Conspiracy:  
The Mud Monarchs are planning a decisive strike to eliminate the Reclaimers Guild, a move that could shift the balance of power in the relic trade forever. Players are hired by a shadowy benefactor to infiltrate a secret Mud Monarch gathering and expose the conspiracy. Navigating between corrupt politicians, secret agents, and wealthy powerbrokers, the players will uncover that this plot extends far beyond the Reclaimers—it’s part of a larger plan to control the flow of Tartarian relics and manipulate global economies. Do the players sabotage the Monarchs’ plans, reveal the conspiracy, or play all sides to gain leverage in the relic market?
3. The Architect’s Return:  
Beneath a forgotten Tartarian city, players stumble upon a dormant Architectural Sentinel, a semi-conscious machine designed to rebuild the lost empire. Upon awakening, the Sentinel mistakes the players for Tartarian descendants and offers them Etheric knowledge and resources to restore the empire. However, activating these systems could destabilize the world and trigger another cataclysm. Players must choose whether to aid the Sentinel in resurrecting Tartaria or stop it before it brings about a global reset. Along the way, they will face resistance from the Conspiracy Architects, who want the Sentinel destroyed, and the Forgotten Order, who seek to control it.
4. The Relic Wars:  
A newly uncovered Tartarian temple in Central Asia contains a relic capable of reactivating Tartaria’s Etheric Power network, a discovery that could reshape the world. The players are caught in a race between the Forgotten Order, Tartarian Revivalists, and the Mud Monarchs to secure the artifact. Each faction offers alliances, wealth, or threats, depending on the players' actions. As they navigate ancient traps, rival relic hunters, and rogue factions, they must decide whether to hand over the relic, use it for themselves, or destroy it to prevent another Mud Flood.
5. The Lost Capital of the Grand Canyon:  
Deep within the restricted zones of the Grand Canyon lies a Tartarian capital, untouched for centuries. Legends say it holds vast reserves of Etheric Power and secrets of the Tartarian Giants. The players are hired to lead an expedition into the forbidden zone, but they soon discover they’re not alone. Tartarian Revivalists believe this city is the key to summoning the Giants, while Conspiracy Architects are mobilizing to stop anyone from reaching it. As players delve deeper, they encounter dangerous terrain, ancient defenses, and evidence of a second Mud Flood that could be triggered by the city’s energy systems. Will they survive long enough to uncover the truth?
6. The Return of the Giants:  
Reports of Tartarian Giant sightings are on the rise, with many previously attributed to Bigfoot or Yeti. The Servants of the Giants believe this is a sign of the Giants' return to reclaim the world. The players are hired to investigate one such sighting in the Siberian wilderness, only to discover a hidden Tartarian temple containing relics that can either summon or banish the Giants. As they confront rival factions, ancient magic, and forgotten technology, the players must decide whether to aid the Giants' return or prevent them from reshaping the world.
7. The Second Mud Flood Prophecy:  
A doomsday cult within the Tartarian Revivalists has uncovered an ancient prophecy foretelling a second Mud Flood that will cleanse the world and restore Tartaria to its former glory. The players are hired to investigate a series of strange natural disasters linked to the cult's activities. As they piece together the puzzle, they find that the cult is using Tartarian relics to manipulate the weather and trigger a chain reaction that could bury the world in mud once again. The players must stop the cult before they set the prophecy in motion, or decide if the world should be reset after all.
8. The Forgotten City of Ice:  
In the frozen wastelands of Antarctica, explorers discover a lost Tartarian city buried beneath the ice. This city, a once-glorious Tartarian capital, holds invaluable technology and relics. However, as players venture into the frozen depths, they find themselves in a deadly race with the Forgotten Order and the Mud Monarchs. Both factions seek the city’s secrets, but its ancient defenses are still active, making every step dangerous. As players delve deeper, they uncover relics that could rewrite history, and must decide whether to share their discovery or let it remain hidden beneath the ice.
9. The Etheric Grid:  
Players uncover evidence of a vast, hidden Etheric Power grid that once spanned the globe, connecting Tartarian temples and cities through invisible energy conduits. The Forgotten Order believes that by reactivating key nodes in this network, they can restore the world to its former prosperity. However, the Conspiracy Architects have long known about the grid and have suppressed knowledge of it for centuries. Players must travel to lost Tartarian sites to reactivate these nodes, while evading the Architects’ agents and uncovering the dangers of restoring the grid. Could reactivation lead to untold technological advances, or trigger another global disaster?
10. The Reclaimer's Dilemma:  
The Reclaimers Guild has stumbled upon the greatest haul in its history: an entire underground Tartarian city untouched by time. The Guild is ready to sell the relics on the black market, but before they can, multiple factions—including the Mud Monarchs and Forgotten Order—move to seize the city for themselves. Players are hired as mercenaries, tasked with defending the city from raiders, relic hunters, and faction agents. As the conflict escalates, the players must choose whether to side with the Guild, sell the relics to the highest bidder, or claim the city for themselves. Their actions could shift the balance of power in the hidden war over Tartaria’s legacy.

Here’s a step-by-step walkthrough for a turn in the Tartaria Prima TTRPG:
 1. Start of the Turn:
Each player completes their turn before moving to the next. Turns consist of Action Phases, Movement Phases, and Reaction Phases. Here's how a typical turn works:
 2. Roll Initiative:
At the beginning of combat, all players and enemies roll for Initiative using a d20, adding their Dexterity modifier or other bonuses based on their class (such as Reclaimers who might have an Initiative bonus).
- Initiative Example: Roll a d20 + Dexterity modifier.
Example: A Reclaimer with +2 Dexterity rolls a d20 and gets a 15. Their total Initiative is 17.
 3. Movement Phase:
On their turn, a player can move up to their Speed (typically 30 feet unless modified by race or abilities). Movement can happen before or after taking an action.
- Difficult Terrain: If the player is moving through difficult terrain (like Etheric-charged mud), their movement speed is halved.
Example: A player wants to move across 30 feet of normal terrain, which is their Speed. They move to a new position to gain a tactical advantage before attacking.
 4. Action Phase:
Players get 1 Action on their turn. This could be:
- Attack (with weapons or magic),
- Cast a spell (if they have magic),
- Use an item (like throwing a relic or activating Mud Tech),
- Take the Dodge action (to defend and make enemies roll attacks with disadvantage),
- Help another player, etc.
 Attack Example:
- The player declares an attack using their Etheric Blade. 
  - Roll d20 to determine if the attack hits. Compare the result to the target’s Armor Class (AC).
  - If the result is equal to or higher than the AC, the attack hits.
Example: The player rolls a d20 and adds their attack bonus (+3). They roll a 14, which with the +3 bonus becomes a 17. If the enemy's AC is 15, the attack hits.
 Damage Roll:
- Roll damage based on the weapon used. In this case, the Etheric Blade deals 1d8 slashing damage + 1d6 radiant damage (Etheric energy).
Example: The player rolls 1d8 for slashing damage and gets a 5. Then, they roll 1d6 for the Etheric energy and get a 3. Total damage = 8.
 5. Bonus Actions:
Some classes and races allow a Bonus Action. This could be:
- Using a special ability (like a Reclaimer’s grapple hook),
- Casting a minor spell,
- Activating a Mud Tech device, or
- Using off-hand weapons if they are dual-wielding.
Example: The Reclaimer uses their Magnetic Grappler as a bonus action to pull an object closer while attacking.
 6. Reaction Phase:
Reactions are actions that can be taken outside the player's turn, usually in response to something like:
- Opportunity Attacks (when an enemy leaves your melee range),
- Etheric Shield Activation (a reaction that boosts defense for 1 turn),
- Mud Magic defense (triggering a Mud Barrier when attacked).
Reactions can only be used once per round.
Example: The player uses Etheric Shield as a reaction after being attacked by an enemy to add +5 AC until the next turn, potentially dodging the incoming hit.
 7. Saving Throws:
Sometimes a player must make a Saving Throw to avoid effects like poison, being restrained by mud, or surviving a massive Etheric blast.
- Roll a d20 and add the appropriate ability modifier (like Constitution, Dexterity, etc.).
- The GM sets the DC (Difficulty Class) for the saving throw.
Example: The player must make a Dexterity Saving Throw (DC 15) to avoid being hit by a Mud Wave attack. They roll a d20 and add their Dexterity modifier (+2). The roll is 12, +2 = 14. They fail and take full damage.
 8. End of Turn:
Once the player completes their actions (movement, attack, bonus actions, reactions), the turn ends. The GM moves to the next player or enemy in the initiative order.
 Combat Example Walkthrough:
1. Reclaimer Turn:
- Initiative order comes around to the Reclaimer character.
- Movement: The Reclaimer moves 20 feet towards cover, keeping their distance from a Mud Golem.
2. Action Phase:
- Attack: The Reclaimer pulls out their Mud Rifle and takes a shot at the Mud Golem.
  - Rolls a d20 for the attack, gets a 16. Adding their attack bonus (+3), the total is 19, which hits the Golem’s AC of 14.
  - They roll damage: 2d6 lightning damage. The rolls are 4 and 5, dealing 9 damage to the Mud Golem.
3. Bonus Action:
- The Reclaimer uses their Magnetic Grappler to retrieve a nearby relic from the ruins.
4. Reaction Phase:
- Later in the round, the Mud Golem moves away from the Reclaimer, triggering an Opportunity Attack. The Reclaimer takes a swing with their Etheric Dagger, rolling a 15, which hits, and dealing 1d4 + 1d4 psychic damage.
End of Turn: After this, the GM proceeds to the next player in the initiative.
