Introduction - Tartaria Prima: Life After the Flood is a narrative-driven tabletop role-playing game (TTRPG) set in a post-apocalyptic world where the remnants of an ancient empire, Tartaria, still influence the modern world. After a catastrophic event known as the Great Mud Flood buried the once-mighty empire, humanity navigates a fractured world where forgotten technologies, arcane powers, and hidden histories unknowingly shape daily life. Players will explore ancient ruins, unlock forgotten secrets, and interact with factions vying for control over lost relics of power.
In this world, players can assume the roles of various races like Tartarian Giants, once God-like beings who ruled the empire, or Mud Dwellers, mutated humans living in subterranean ruins. Other options include reclaimers, adventurers diving into Tartaria’s buried cities for artifacts, architectural sentinels, and ancient semi-conscious machines still guarding Tartaria’s lost technology. With various races and backgrounds to choose from, each offering distinct abilities and cultural lore, players can customize their characters to fit their playstyles.
The core gameplay focuses on exploration, mystery, and survival. Players use skills and abilities linked to core attributes—like Prowess for strength tasks or Aetheria for arcane knowledge—to overcome challenges. The game uses a mix of dice mechanics (primarily D20s, D10s, and D6s) to resolve player actions, with the Arbiter guiding the story, setting challenges, and rolling for outcomes.
Players will face physical and supernatural challenges to uncover Tartaria's lost legacy. The world is teeming with ancient defenses, strange creatures like Mud Golems, and factions like the secretive Mud Monarchs who hoard Tartarian technology. The Forgotten Order, a rival group, seeks to unlock the secrets of Tartaria and restore its glory. These dynamics provide plenty of narrative hooks for adventures, creating a world where ancient power clashes with modern ambition.
The game balances combat, exploration, and roleplay, encouraging players to engage with the rich lore while testing their characters' abilities and resilience. With hidden technologies, cryptid-like creatures, and an ever-shifting political landscape, Tartaria Prima offers an immersive experience for players eager to explore a world filled with mystery, danger, and ancient power.
The Core Mechanics - Tartaria Prima uses a D20, D10, and D6-based system with a roll-over resolution. Players roll to resolve any combat or non-combat-related challenge; players roll the relevant dice and add the appropriate modifiers from their character’s core attributes (Prowess, Aetheria, etc.). The Arbiter sets a difficulty level for the task, and the player must roll equal to or higher than the target number to succeed in non-combat. If the roll is lower, the action fails, and the story progresses with the consequences of that failure.
For example:
A moderate task may require rolling nine or higher.
A very hard task may need 15 or higher.
For example, if a player tries to sneak past a guard and the Arbiter assigns a difficulty level of 4 (Hard), the player must roll 12 or higher. Stealth is tied to Dexterity, so if the player’s Dexterity modifier is +2, they must roll a ten or higher on the D20 to succeed​.
Combat rolls and mechanics will be discussed in more detail in the following Combat Mechanics section.

Advantages and Disadvantages -
During gameplay, The Arbiter, certain situations, abilities, or environmental factors may grant your character an advantage or disadvantage on an action. These mechanics affect how you roll for success:
Advantage: When you have an advantage, you roll a D20 and a D6. You add the result of both dice and attribute modifiers to determine the total. This increases your chances of success by giving you a bonus die.
Disadvantage: When you have a disadvantage, you roll a D10 and a D6 instead of a D20. The smaller dice combination imposes a 4-point handicap because the maximum total is lower. You still add any relevant modifiers, but your overall chance of success is reduced due to the smaller dice pool.
Example:
If you have an advantage on a roll to sneak past a guard, you roll a D20 and a D6, adding their totals with your Stealth modifier. For instance, if you roll a 14 on the D20, a 3 on the D6, and have a +2 Stealth modifier, your total would be 19.
If you have a disadvantage, roll a D10 and a D6. If you roll a 7 on the D10 and a 2 on the D6, with the same +2 modifier, your total would be 11, making success less likely.
These mechanics add strategic depth, rewarding players for gaining an advantage and making overcoming challenges harder when they’re at a disadvantage.
Damage Types
Degradation: Corrosive damage caused by Etheric-charged mud or sludge, rapidly decaying organic matter, armor, and weapons. Degradation effects often weaken defenses, reducing armor effectiveness or weapon durability by one step per prolonged exposure (typically lasting until the affected item is repaired). Victims with degradation damage feel a weakening sensation, and their gear may require maintenance after encountering significant degradation damage.
Bludgeoning: Physical impact damage from blunt force, such as falling debris, collapsing tunnels, or blunt relic weapons. Bludgeoning damage can knock creatures prone, preventing movement until they use half their movement to stand. In severe cases (Arbiter’s discretion), bludgeoning can temporarily daze a creature, causing a disadvantage on Dexterity-based attacks and checks for 1 round due to disorientation.
Burn: Intense Aetheric heat damage caused by overloaded relics or high-energy Etherstone. Burn effects can leave residual Etheric scars that weaken a creature’s resistance to further Etheric effects for 1d6 rounds. During this time, creatures are particularly vulnerable to burn or aetheric attacks, and their natural healing may be halved until they rest for an hour or use magical healing.
Aetheric: Invisible, concussive force from raw Etheric energy. Aetheric damage can knock creatures back 1 distance range and, in severe cases, induce temporary disorientation (1 round) where the creature cannot move or act. Aetheric force disrupts anything requiring concentration, causing an immediate concentration check to maintain ongoing effects.
Electrical: Shock damage from Etheric storms, relic discharges, or Tartarian Aether grids. Electrical damage can paralyze a creature's muscles, preventing them from moving for 1 round. While paralyzed, the creature cannot perform physical actions or reactions, although they may still perceive their surroundings. This temporary paralysis wears off quickly but leaves the target vulnerable to additional attacks as they cannot take defensive action after they become unparalyzed for 1 turn.
Piercing: Physical puncture damage from sharp relic shards, crystal weapons, or pointed constructs. Piercing damage, especially from Etheric shards, can cause bleeding wounds that persist, dealing an extra 1d6-2 of damage per turn until the wound is healed (typically requiring magical healing, a successful Medicine check, or some type of first aid kit). While bleeding, a creature is disadvantaged in attack rolls due to blood loss.
Poison: Toxic damage from Etheric reactions, noxious gases, or any other toxic substance that can spread through contact, ingestion, or inhalation. Poisoned creatures experience nausea and disorientation, causing a disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks for 1d6 rounds. Severe poisoning may require a First Aid saving throw each round to avoid incapacitation due to sickness.
Radiation: Intense Etheric light and cosmic energy from Etheric cores or relics, searing both flesh and spirit. Radiation damage can weaken creatures for 1d6 rounds, causing them to have a disadvantage on all Strength and First Aid checks and saving throws due to Etheric exhaustion. Prolonged exposure can result in lasting conditions (Arbiter discretion), requiring significant recovery time or powerful healing spells.
Slashing: Physical cutting damage from sharp edges, relic blades, or constructs with slicing appendages. Slashing damage from Etheric constructs can sever armor straps or slice through bindings, lowering armor class by 2 points until repaired. A creature with slashing injuries takes an additional 1d6 damage each turn if it attempts any action that requires a dexterity check.
Stun: Non-lethal shock or Etheric disruption that causes temporary incapacitation, often lasting 1 round. While stunned, a creature cannot move, speak, or take physical actions, and runecasting is impossible due to disrupted concentration. Stunned creatures automatically fail Dexterity saving throws, have a disadvantage on all other saves, and cannot react or use instants until the stun effect wears off.
Combat Order of Actions 
In Tartaria Prima, combat is structured to flow smoothly while allowing for tactical decisions, flexibility, and dynamic action. The following outlines the order of actions during a typical combat encounter.
1. Roll Initiative & Set the Scene
Before combat begins, both sides roll for initiative to determine which group acts first in each round. The Arbiter rolls for the enemy, and one player rolls on behalf of the group. The higher roll determines whether the players or enemies go first throughout the encounter.
Roll Initiative: The Arbiter (for the enemy) and the players roll a d10. The side with the higher result wins the initiative and takes their turn first in every round.
Set the Scene: The Arbiter describes the environment, enemy positions, and any environmental features or obstacles. This helps players understand their surroundings and opens possibilities for tactical decisions (e.g., climbing structures, finding cover, or using environmental hazards).
Example: "The Mud Golem stands in the center of the crumbling courtyard, debris scattered around the corners. A large pillar is leaning precariously nearby."
2. Players Declare Actions
Once the initiative is determined, the side that won (players or enemies) takes their turn. The players decide the order in which they act each turn. On their turn, players declare their actions based on their position and the situation.
Players can choose from a variety of actions, such as:
Attacking: Melee, ranged, or runecaster attacks. Attacking can only be done once per turn.
Blocking or defending: They can either use an appropriate item or attempt to evade or barehandedly block an attack. The Arbiter assigns a difficulty to this action.
Using Abilities: Using special abilities. You can only use an ability once per turn and only according to the ability's limitations.
Movement-Based Actions: Moving into a new position, climbing, or taking cover. You can only move from one position in the distance range you are into another and continue to an attack or other action. If you move to another distance range, you can no longer attack that turn, but you can do other actions, such as interact with the environment or another PC in that area.
Interacting with the Environment: Using debris, traps, or other features for advantage (e.g., toppling a pillar, collapsing rubble).
Interaction with another PC: Interaction with another PC could be a blocking move for them, such as handing them an item or assisting them in a single action like extinguishing them with your water flask.
If a player’s action involves something outside of regular combat (e.g., climbing, moving objects), the Arbiter may set a difficulty level for the task. The player must then roll against this difficulty to succeed.
Example: "The Rogue wants to climb the pillar for a better vantage point. The Arbiter sets the difficulty level at 2, meaning they must roll a 6 or higher to succeed."
3. Roll for Attacks, Saves, and Parry
Once actions are declared, players roll to see if their attacks or actions succeed. Combat is resolved using d20 rolls for attacks and defenses.
Attacks: Players roll a d20 to attack, and the roll is compared to the enemy’s Armor Class (AC). If the roll equals or exceeds the AC, the attack hits, and damage is rolled based on the weapon or runecaster.
Saves: If a player is the target of a spell, ability, or environmental hazard, they may need to roll a saving throw to avoid or reduce special effects such as stun, bludgeoning, burn, not a straight damage roll.
Parry: If a player is attacked, they can choose to parry. The player rolls against the enemy’s attack roll. If the player's parry roll is higher than the enemy's attack roll, the attack is successfully blocked.
Example: The Fighter sees the Mud Golem swinging and declares a parry. The Golem rolls an 18 to hit, and the Fighter rolls a 12, failing the parry and taking the full damage of the attack.
4. Resolve Player Actions
Once all attacks and actions are rolled, their effects are resolved:
Damage: Players roll damage dice for their successful attacks. The enemy takes damage, adjusted for any immunities or resistances (e.g., the Mud Golem may be immune to non-magical physical damage).
Non-Combat Actions: If a player attempts a non-combat action (e.g., collapsing rubble or climbing), they roll against the difficulty set by the Arbiter. If successful, the action is resolved and may affect the battlefield (e.g., creating obstacles or dealing with environmental damage).
Instants: Some abilities or powers can be triggered at any point during the round. Players can use instants during their turn or in reaction to an enemy’s action.
Example: The Mage successfully casts Etheric Pulse and rolls for damage, but the Mud Golem’s resistance to magic reduces the final damage taken.
5. Opposition’s Turn
After the players’ turn is complete, the enemies act. The Arbiter controls the enemies, rolling for their attacks, saves, or special abilities.
Enemy Attacks: The Arbiter rolls for the enemies' attacks, just like the players. Players can respond with parries or instants where applicable.
Enemy Abilities: The Arbiter may trigger special abilities, powers, or environmental interactions during the enemies’ turn.
Example: The Mud Golem charges at the Tartarian Giant, rolls a 20 for a max roll hit, and knocks the Tartarian Giant unconscious.
6. End of Round Recap
At the end of the round, the Arbiter summarizes the events, adjusting the scene if necessary. This allows players to keep track of damage, environmental changes, and enemy conditions. If the fight continues, the next round begins, and players and enemies continue their actions in the same initiative order.
Adjust the Scene: The Arbiter describes changes in the environment or battlefield (e.g., “The Mud Golem is wounded, and rubble is now blocking part of the courtyard.”).
Proceed to the Next Round: Combat continues in rounds until there is a resolution—whether through victory, defeat, escape, or negotiation.
Example: The Fighter is unconscious, the Rogue is still positioned on the pillar, and the Mud Golem is severely weakened. The group must decide whether to keep fighting or retreat.
Conclusion
Combat in follows a flexible and dynamic system where players declare actions, roll against enemy defenses or Arbiter-set difficulties, and use the environment to their advantage. Each round flows in this order, ensuring players can focus on strategy and narrative immersion.
Combat Example:
Combat Setup
Enemy: Mud Golem (AC 14, 60 HP, immune to non-Rune physical damage, resistant to magic)
Players:
Reclaimer: Melee combat specialist with parry ability.
Tartarian Giant: Etheric spellcaster.
Aetherborn: Agile and stealthy, using ranged attacks.
Round 1: Roll Initiative
Both sides roll a d10 for initiative to determine who acts first.
Arbiter rolls a 5 for the Mud Golem.
Players roll a 7, meaning the players go first.
Players can decide their order within their turn each round.
Players’ Turn
Tartarian Giant: Uses Etheric Pulse on the Mud Golem. They roll a d20 and get a 16, hitting the Golem. They roll 2d10 for damage, dealing 12 damage, but the Mud Golem's resistance halves it to 6. Mud Golem HP: 54.
Aetherborn: The Aetherborn, from behind cover, fires a short bow. They roll a 13, missing the Golem (AC 14).
Reclaimer: The Reclaimer charges in with a greatsword. They roll a 19, hitting the Golem, but the Mud Golem’s immunity to non-rune physical damage means the attack does no damage.
Mud Golem’s Turn
The Mud Golem targets the Reclaimer with a fist slam. It rolls a 15, hitting and dealing 12 bludgeoning damage. The Reclaimer’s HP drops significantly.
Round 2: Tactical Maneuvering
The players keep their initiative advantage and again decide their order of action.
Players’ Turn
Tartarian Giant: The Tartarian Giant uses Etheric Surge, rolling a 17 (hit). They roll 3d6 for damage, dealing 11 (reduced to 5 due to resistance). Mud Golem HP: 49.
Aetherborn: The Aetherborn attempts to climb a nearby pillar for a better vantage point. The Arbiter sets the difficulty at 2 (easy), meaning the Rogue needs to roll a 6 or higher. They roll a 9, successfully reaching the top of the pillar.
Reclaimer: The Reclaimer, hurt from the previous round, rolls 1d20 for a 9 and lands no damage to the golem.
Mud Golem’s Turn
The Mud Golem swings at the Reclaimer again. It rolls an 18, hitting the Reclaimer. The Reclaimer alerts the Arbiter that he wants to parry; before the damage is applied, the Reclaimer rolls 3d6 to get a best out of three, even or odd. He rolls 2 odd, 1 even. The Golem deals 8 more bludgeoning damage when he fails, and his weapon is now broken. 
Round 3: Adjusting the Strategy
The players switch up tactics in this round.
Players’ Turn
Tartarian Giant: The Tartarian Giant uses Etheric Barrier to protect themselves, gaining temporary damage reduction for 2 rounds.
Aetherborn: From the top of the pillar, the Aetherborn fires an arrow, rolling a 19 to hit. However, the non-rune attack does not cause damage due to Golem's immunity.
Reclaimer: The Reclaimer, heavily injured, decides to attack again. They roll a 20 (Max roll hit!). Since max roll hits in this game don't automatically deal double damage unless the weapon or asset specifies, the Reclaimer deals normal damage—but it’s still negated by the Mud Golem’s immunity to non-magical damage.
Mud Golem’s Turn
The Mud Golem turns its attention to the Tartarian Giant, charging them. It rolls a 14, hitting the Tartarian Giant despite the barrier. The Tartarian Giant takes only 6 damage with the barrier instead of 12.
Round 4: Using the Environment
The party realizes they must leverage the environment to overcome the Golem's resistance.
Players’ Turn
Tartarian Giant: The Tartarian Giant uses Etheric Pulse again, rolling a 12 but missing the Golem.
Aetherborn: The Aetherborn spots some loose rubble near the Golem and tries to cause it to collapse on the enemy. The Arbiter sets the difficulty at moderate, meaning the Aetherborn needs to roll a 9 or higher. The Aetherborn rolls a 10, successfully causing the rubble to collapse and deal 10 environmental damage (bypassing the Golem's immunities). Mud Golem HP: 39.
Reclaimer: The severely weakened Reclaimer assists the Aetherborn by pulling down more debris. The Arbiter sets the difficulty at easy, and the Reclaimer rolls a 7, successfully dislodging more rubble and dealing another 8 damage. Mud Golem HP: 31.
Mud Golem’s Turn
The Mud Golem, enraged, shakes off the debris and charges at the Tartarian Giant again. It rolls a 20 (max roll hit) and deals 20 damage, knocking the Tartarian Giant unconscious.
Round 5: The Final Push
The Aetherborn must get creative with the Tartarian Giant down and the Reclaimer heavily injured.
Players’ Turn
Aetherborn: The Aetherborn tries to bring down a large piece of a crumbling wall onto the Mud Golem. The Arbiter sets the difficulty at hard, so the Aetherborn needs to roll a 12 or higher. They roll a 12, succeeding and dealing 15 damage (Arbiter assigns 2d10 to the environmental attack) as the wall collapses on the Golem. Mud Golem HP: 16.
Reclaimer: The Reclaimer, out of options, takes a defensive stance and prepares to hold off the Golem’s next attack. (Arbiter assigns very hard to the action of barehanded blocking the golem)
Mud Golem’s Turn
The Mud Golem, though damaged, charges at Reclaimer one last time. It rolls a 17; the Reclaimer rolls a 4, missing the block, and the Mud Golem deals 12 bludgeoning damage, knocking the Reclaimer unconscious.
End of Combat
With the Tartarian Giant and Reclaimer unconscious, the Aetherborn is left standing but with only 16 HP left on the Mud Golem. The Aetherborn must decide whether to finish the fight by further leveraging the environment or attempting to escape, leaving their fallen comrades behind.
Instants
Definition: Abilities from your race, title, or faction that can be used only at certain times are considered instants. These powerful, special actions can be triggered at any time during the game, regardless of whose turn it is.
Usage: Instants can be used:
At any time, including during any player's turn, NPC's turn, or even during the Arbiter’s actions.
They do not require an action from the player to use, meaning they can be activated in addition to a player's regular turn.
Example of Instants:
Racial Ability: A Tartarian Giant may have a racial instant that allows them to temporarily boost their Strength for one attack, which can be triggered right before an attack to deal extra damage.
Faction Ability: A member of the Forgotten Order might have an instant that lets them tap into ancient Tartarian technology to disable a trap or bypass a security system.
Title Ability: A player with a Warlord title might have an instant ability to rally allies, giving them bonuses to their next roll, which they can trigger right before an ally's turn.
Strategic Use:
Interrupting: Instants provide tactical flexibility. They allow players to interrupt the flow of combat or events, react to threats, boost allies, or mitigate damage at critical moments.
Coordinated Actions: Because they can be activated during any player's turn, instants allow players to work together more efficiently, layering their abilities for greater impact.
Instants add an extra layer of strategy, letting players take advantage of unexpected situations and shift the tide of battle or encounters in their favor.
Initiative 
Group Initiative: When a fight begins, the players roll group initiative to determine if they attack first or have to defend. One roll is made for the whole party; if they succeed, they get the first strike; if they fail, the enemies act first.
Player-Determined Turn Order: After rolling initiative, players decide among themselves the order in which they will take their turns. This allows them to coordinate their actions and create strategic plans to deal with enemies.
Quick Events: For sudden, jump-scare-like events initiated by the Arbiter, only one player rolls initiative for the team. If they pass, the group can react to the event before being automatically affected by it; if they fail, the event happens before they can respond. The player must let the arbiter know they want to challenge the quick event, and the Arbiter must agree that this is possible. The player that challenged and ONLY the player that challenged describes his plan and rolls against it.
In-Game Distances
Arm’s Reach:
Description: 1-10 feet. This is when characters are in immediate proximity to each other—within striking distance for melee attacks or interactions with objects. In Combat: Melee combat happens at this range. Weapons like swords, knives, and fists are used. Grappling or other close-quarters abilities can be performed.
Movement: Moving within Arm’s Reach requires no special action; it’s instantaneous since you’re next to your target.
Useful Because: It keeps melee combat simple and clear—if you’re in Arm’s Reach, you can fight hand-to-hand or interact with something in your immediate space without worrying about exact distances.
Close:
Description: 10-20 feet. This range is just beyond Arm’s Reach but still nearby—perfect for short-range actions like throwing objects, drawing weapons, or preparing for a charge.
In Combat: Characters can use short-ranged weapons like spears, thrown weapons, or quick sprints to close the gap and engage in melee. Moving from Close to Arm’s Reach takes a single action.
Movement: It takes one movement action to traverse from Close to Arm’s Reach or vice versa. Moving from Close to Far takes more effort and might take more time or action.
Useful Because: It provides tactical decision-making in combat. You can position yourself outside immediate danger or prepare to engage without getting too close too soon.
Far:
Description: 20+ feet. This represents long distances—targets or areas far away enough that they require ranged weapons, runecasters, or extended movement to interact with.
In Combat: Characters use bows, crossbows, or long-range runecasters at this distance. Moving from Far to Close requires multiple actions or rounds of movement, depending on the situation.
Movement: Moving from Far to Close typically takes a full turn as you traverse a greater distance. Getting from Far to Arm’s Reach would take two rounds of movement.
Useful Because: It creates clear zones for ranged combat and positioning. Players can see where they’re safe from immediate attacks and how long it will take to close the gap.
Why This is Useful in Combat and Movement
Tartaria Prima simplifies movement and combat by using general ranges rather than exact distances, streamlining gameplay and making it easier for players and the Arbiter to visualize positioning. This system offers tactical flexibility, allowing players to strategically choose to stay at a distance or engage in melee without complex rules. It enhances combat flow by balancing the tension of movement with quick decision-making. In non-combat situations, this approach aids in area traversal, letting players explore without needing to measure every step, keeping the game focused on narrative and tactics.
Weapons System 
Weapons and Runecasters in Tartaria Prima
In Tartaria Prima, the choice between weapons or runecasters defines your approach to combat, exploration, and interaction with the world. Weapons and runecasters powers are tied directly to your character’s core abilities—Strength, Dexterity, Wisdom, Charisma, and Intelligence. Understanding the relationship between your abilities and equipment is essential to thriving in Tartaria’s post-flood landscape.
Weapon and Runecaster Selection
At character creation, you will choose whether your character focuses on weapons or runescastrers, with the restriction that you cannot wield both an offensive spell and a weapon during combat.
Weapons
Weapons are divided into four distinct styles, each tied to a core ability:
Dual Wield (Dexterity): Characters can wield two weapons, focusing on speed and agility for rapid attacks.
Single-Handed (Strength or Dexterity): Balanced between offense and defense, allowing flexibility in combat.
Two-Handed (Strength): High-damage weapons that are slower but devastating when they land.
Ranged (Dexterity or Wisdom): Weapons like bows, crossbows, and firearms that allow for long-range combat.
Weapons are also categorized by rarity, ranging from Common to Legendary, with higher rarity weapons offering stronger damage and special abilities. Your ability score in Strength, Dexterity, or Wisdom directly limits the weapon you can wield. For example, a Legendary two-handed sword may require a Strength of 12, and if your Strength is only 9, you will not be able to wield it until your Strength increases.
Weapon Scaling:
Common: Deals 1d6 damage.
Uncommon: Deals 2d6 or 1d10 damage.
Rare: Deals 1d10 + 1d6 damage.
Legendary: Deals 2d10 damage.
Example:
If your Strength is 6, you can use an Uncommon two-handed weapon but not a Rare or Legendary one. Until you reach the required Strength score, higher-tier weapons are unusable.
Also:
Skilled Weapon Usage Mechanic
Weapons are bound to a character’s ability scores, determining the damage they can inflict. If a character has an ability score that doubles the required amount to wield a weapon, they can wield that weapon with a heightened level of proficiency, unlocking a higher damage roll.
Example Rule:You are considered highly skilled if a weapon requires 5 Dexterity and your character has 10 Dexterity. Instead of using the standard damage dice for its tier (e.g., rolling 2d6 for an uncommon dagger), you can upgrade to the next tier of dice (e.g., rolling 1d20 as if it were a Rare weapon). This reflects mastery over the weapon’s use, allowing for more powerful attacks.
Limitations:
Upgrade one dice set higher than the base weapon’s damage. For example, you can move from 2d6 to 1d20, but not beyond that.
This bonus only applies to damage rolls. Other aspects of the weapon, such as rarity-based special abilities or secondary effects, are not upgraded.
This applies only to physical weapons, not runecasters or magical items.
Runecasters
If you use Aetheric power, your character will wield runecasters to channel Aetheric energy. Runecasters allow Aetheric wielders to manipulate the Aetheric energy left behind by the fallen Tartarian Empire. These powers are tied to your Intelligence score, and like weapons, runecasters are divided into Common, Uncommon, Rare, and Legendary tiers.
Spell Tiers by Intelligence:
Common (1-4 Intelligence): Basic powers with small effects or limited damage. Used in arm’s reach and close ranges.
Uncommon (5-8 Intelligence): More versatile powers with moderate damage, used in arm’s reach and close ranges.
Rare (9-10 Intelligence): Powerful Aetheric energy with complex effects, used in all three ranges; effects diminish at far range; utilize an even/odd roll with a d6 to see if it lands.
Legendary (11-12 Intelligence): Extremely potent Aetheric powers with devastating or unique effects. Potent at all ranges.
Runecasters are crafted by combining materials—primarily a blank rune caster casing, an Aetheric relic (often mud or a crystal), and another material to transmute the Aetheric energy into a specific effect (such as fire, lightning, freezing, or force). Crafting requires an Intelligence score of 11 or higher, and materials can be found, purchased, or acquired as rewards.
Although runecasters cannot be upgraded, they can be disassembled, allowing you to recover their materials for future crafting.
Ability Scores and Weapon/Spell Unlocks
Your ability score determines which weapons and runecasters you can use and directly limits the level and power of equipment available. Here’s how it works:
Strength allows you to unlock heavier weapons like two-handed swords and axes.
Dexterity unlocks agile weapons and boosts accuracy with ranged weapons.
Wisdom enhances your perception and efficiency with ranged weapons.
Charisma ties into more specialized weapons, often influencing social encounters.
Intelligence governs your ability to use runecasters, unlocking more powerful Aetheric effects as you grow stronger.
If your ability score doesn’t meet a particular weapon or rune caster requirement, you cannot use it until your score increases. For instance, if you need 12 Strength to wield a Legendary weapon and you only have 9 Strength, that weapon remains locked for you.
Example:
A Rare sword might require a Strength of 9, but if your Strength is only 7, you’ll have to use Uncommon or Common weapons until you increase your Strength score.
Switching Between Weapons and Runecasters
Switching between weapons and runecasters (or switching between different weapons) during combat leaves your character empty-handed for one full turn. During this turn, you cannot use any weapon or spell but interact with the environment and take non-combat actions.
During this “empty-handed” turn, you can:
Punch or physically interact with enemies.
Throw an object (like a rock or dust).
Climb or grab a ledge to escape attacks.
Dodge or perform defensive rolls.
You can take any action your character’s hands could perform, except using a weapon or rune caster. This restriction forces you to carefully plan your switches to avoid vulnerability at a critical moment.
Example:
If you’re switching from a rune caster to a bow, you could spend the turn throwing a handful of dirt at an enemy to blind them or grabbing a ledge to hang out of range. You can still defend yourself but can’t attack with a weapon or cast a spell during this turn.
Runecaster Crafting Process and Ingredients
Runecaster crafting is a scientific process in Tartaria Prima, using ancient Tartarian techniques to harness the Aetheric energy infused worldwide. Aetheric energy acts like radiation, emanating from materials left behind after the empire’s fall. By placing these materials into a blank rune caster casing, you can transmute that energy into specific effects, such as fire, lightning, or force.
Runecaster Crafting Components:
A blank rune caster casing (Common, Uncommon, Rare, or Legendary).
An Aetheric relic (usually infused mud, a crystal shard, or other ancient materials).
A third transmutative material that dictates the effect of the spell.
For example, combining a legendary blank rune caster, a highly Aetheric-infused mud sample, and a crystal shard might create a power weapon rune caster capable of casting a force-based spell with devastating effects.
Runecaster crafting requires recipes, which can be obtained from NPCs, found in ruins, or given as rewards. These recipes detail the materials needed for each rune caster, and the crafting process involves imbuing the Aetheric relics with the desired effect.
Although runecasters cannot be upgraded, they can be disassembled, allowing you to recover and use the components to craft higher-level runecasters.
Weapon and Rune caster Upgrades
Weapons in Tartaria Prima can be upgraded with runecasters to enhance their abilities. However, runecasters themselves cannot be upgraded. Here’s what you need to know:
Weapon Upgrades: Runecasters can be slotted into weapons to enhance their power. For example, a rune caster may give your weapon an ability modifier or a special attack bonus. This is done using a Rune Engraving Kit to engrave the associated rune markings on the weapons. The player must keep the runecaster in their pack for it to work on the weapon.
Common/Uncommon Weapons: These offer smaller, more straightforward bonuses like a slight ability boost or an advantage.
Rare/Legendary Weapons: These often come with instant abilities, such as switching weapons without penalty or triggering a bonus action during combat.
Upgrading weapons with runecasters makes them more powerful, giving you unique combat advantages without directly boosting your character’s offensive and defensive power.
Parrying an Attack 
Parry System in Tartaria Prima
The parry system in Tartaria Prima allows players to reduce incoming damage from physical attacks by attempting to deflect or block with their weapon. Parrying is available to characters using single-handed weapons, ranged weapons, dual-wielders, and even rune caster users, but there are risks involved—each parry comes with a chance that your weapon will break. This makes parrying a strategic decision, as overuse can lead to losing important gear.
Who Can Parry?
Single-Handed Weapon Users: Characters using single-handed melee weapons.
Ranged Weapon Users: Characters using bows, crossbows, or other ranged weapons.
Dual-Wielders: Characters holding two weapons can parry, though at a disadvantage.
Rune caster Users: Characters using magical runecasters can attempt to parry, but with added risk and consequences if their rune caster breaks.
What Can Be Parried?
Parrying can only be used to defend against physical attacks:
Melee weapon strikes (swords, axes, clubs, etc.).
Physical attacks (punches, kicks, thrown objects).
Ranged weapons (arrows, crossbow bolts, bullets).
You cannot parry:
Runecasters or magical effects.
Supernatural powers (such as elemental or arcane forces).
Even single-handed weapon users must roll at a disadvantage when parrying ranged weapons.
How Parry Works
Declare a Parry: When attacked by a physical object, declare a parry as a reaction. You can parry as often as you like during an encounter, but each parry risks breaking your weapon.
Roll to Parry: The roll depends on your combat style:
Single-handed weapon users roll 1d10 for melee attacks but roll 1d10 twice and use the lower result when parrying ranged attacks.
Ranged weapon users roll 1d6 for both melee and ranged attacks.
Dual-wielders and rune caster users roll 1d6 twice and get lower results for all parties due to their focus on offense or magic.
Roll for Weapon Durability: After each parry, roll a d20 three times, the best out of three is your outcome, track odd rolls and even rolls:
More odd than even: The weapon breaks.
More even than odd: The weapon stays intact and can be used again.
Damage Reduction: The result of your parry roll is subtracted from the incoming damage. If the roll reduces the damage to zero or below, you block the attack entirely.
Rune caster Parry Durability and Breakage
If you parry with a rune caster and fail the best-of-three d20 durability roll (i.e., you roll odd twice out of three rolls), the rune caster breaks beyond repair. A party member or vendor cannot repair broken runecasters, unlike weapons. Instead, you can salvage the core components of the rune caster, excluding the casing, and store them in your pack if you have enough room.
Core Components: You can retrieve the Aetheric relic or any additional magical ingredients used to create the rune caster, but the casing is lost. These components can be used in future crafting, provided you have room in your inventory.
Example:
You parry with a powerful rune caster and reduce an incoming 8-point physical attack to 2 points of damage. However, after the parry, you roll three times on a d20 for durability and get 6, 17, and 13 results. Since two of the rolls are odd, your rune caster breaks. You can no longer use the rune caster; salvage the aetheric core and crystal used to create it.
Weapon Repair and Rune caster Salvage
If a weapon breaks after a failed durability roll, it can be repaired by either:
Vendor Repair: Take the weapon to a vendor and pay a fee equal to half the weapon’s cost in Tartarian coins.
Party Repair: A party member with the necessary skills can repair the weapon, but you still lose half the weapon’s value in Tartarian coins (deducted from your total, not paid to the party member).
If a rune caster breaks, it cannot be repaired. However, you can salvage the rune caster’s core components (minus the casing) and store them in your pack for future use.
Salvaging the Rune caster: After a rune caster breaks, the casing is destroyed, but you can keep the core Aetheric material and any other ingredients used to craft the rune caster, provided you have enough inventory space.
Example:
If your rune caster breaks during a parry, you might recover a piece of Aetheric mud and a crystal shard used in its creation. These materials can be stored and used in future rune caster crafting.
Parrying with Different Combat Styles
Single-Handed Weapon Parry (Melee)
Mechanic: Roll 1d10 to reduce damage from physical melee attacks.
Durability Risk: After each parry, roll a d20 three times. If two of the rolls are odd, your weapon breaks.
Parrying Ranged Attacks: Roll 1d10 twice and take the lower result when parrying a ranged attack, then roll the d20 for durability as usual.
Ranged Weapon Parry
Mechanic: Roll 1d6 to reduce damage from melee or ranged attacks.
Durability Risk: Roll a d20 three times after each parry to see if the weapon breaks.
Dual-Wielding Parry
Mechanic: Roll 1d6 twice and take the lower result for all parries.
Durability Risk: After each parry, roll a d20 to see if your weapon breaks, using the best of three results.
Rune caster User Parry
Mechanic: Roll 1d6 twice and take the lower result for parrying physical attacks.
Durability Risk: Roll a d20 three times after each parry. If the rune caster breaks, it cannot be repaired, but you can salvage the core components (excluding the casing).
When to Use Parry
Parrying is a useful tool for reducing damage from physical attacks, but the risk of breaking your weapon or rune caster means you’ll need to use it wisely. Use it when:
You’re facing a high-damage physical attack.
You’re low on health and need to reduce damage to survive.
You’re prepared to lose your weapon or rune caster and have a backup plan.
Advantages of Parrying
Immediate Physical Defense: Parrying allows instant damage reduction for physical attacks.
Accessible Across Combat Styles: Whether wielding a sword, bow, or rune caster, all characters can attempt to parry.
Strategic Risk: The durability mechanic forces players to weigh the risk of losing their weapon or rune caster, adding a layer of tension to combat.
Limitations of Parrying
Weapon Durability: Each parry comes with the risk of breaking your weapon or rune caster. Weapons can be repaired, but runecasters cannot.
Physical Attacks Only: Parrying is only effective against physical attacks and cannot block magical or supernatural effects.
Disadvantage on Ranged Attacks: Parrying ranged weapons requires rolling at a disadvantage, even for single-handed weapon users.
Parry Example with Rune caster Durability
Situation:You are a rune caster user, parrying a physical attack from a club that would deal 8 points of damage.
Declare Parry: You parry the attack using a powerful rune caster.
Roll for Parry: Since you are a rune caster user, you roll 1d6 twice, getting a 4 and a 2. You take the lower result of 2, reducing the incoming damage to 6 points.
Roll for Durability: After the parry, you roll your d20 three times, getting results of 11, 19, and 6. Two of the three rolls are odd, so your rune caster breaks.
Salvage the Rune caster: You cannot repair the rune caster, but you recover its Aetheric core and a crystal shard, which you can store for future crafting.

Shield Bonus: If a character uses a shield, they gain an additional bonus to their AC.
Example: Aurora Shield (Rare) adds +3 AC.
Environmental/Racial Bonuses: Some races gain AC bonuses depending on their environment or racial traits.
Example: Mud Dwellers get +1 AC when fighting in muddy or wet environments.

How AC Works in Combat
During combat, enemies roll an attack against the character's AC:
If the attack roll meets or exceeds the character's AC, the attack hits, and the enemy rolls for damage.
If the attack roll is lower than the character's AC, the attack misses.
For example, the attack hits if an enemy rolls a 15 to hit Taryn, whose AC is 11. If the same enemy rolls against Vesper's AC of 16, the attack will miss unless the roll is 16 or higher.

Barehanded Combat Mechanics
Attack Roll:
Characters fighting barehanded roll a d20 to determine if they hit, just like with any weapon.
They add any Strength and/or Dexterity modifiers (if applicable) to their attack roll.
Damage Roll:
Upon a successful hit, the player rolls according to their race’s base barehanded damage. Most races roll 1d6, while Tartarian Giants roll 1d6 + 1 to reflect their superior strength.
Limitations of Barehanded Combat:
Barehanded attacks do not trigger special abilities (such as bonus damage or magical effects) unless specified by a race or class.
Barehanded attacks do not scale with rarity and can’t be modified with runecasters or relics.
Characters fighting barehanded are disadvantaged (not at a disadvantage), encouraging them to find another weapon or rune caster quickly.
Resting to Recover HP –
Group Rest: When a player chooses to rest, the entire group must rest together. This keeps the party moving and recovering together, maintaining balance within the group.
Two Types of Rest:
2-Hour Rest:
Effect: During a 2-hour rest, you roll 2d6 and regain as many hit points up to your maximum HP.
Risk: There is no risk of enemy attack during this short rest, allowing for safe partial healing.
Full Day Rest (24 hours):
Effect: After a full day of rest, your character fully replenishes all their hit points, restoring HP to the maximum.
Risk: During a full day of rest, there is a risk of being attacked by a random enemy. The Arbiter rolls a D6:
Even roll: The group completes the rest peacefully.
Odd roll: The group is attacked mid-rest, and players must fight without the benefit of the hit points they were working on regaining. They will be fighting with the HP level they had when they started the rest. After the battle, all combatants that survive regain full health if they win.
Why This is Useful:
Strategic Choice: The group must weigh the benefit of a full recovery with the risk of being attacked during a full day's rest. The 2-hour rest offers quicker but smaller healing, while the full-day rest guarantees full recovery at the cost of potential danger.
Tactical Impact: Being attacked during a full day's rest can lead to challenging encounters since the group fights with pre-rest HP levels, deciding to rest a critical one.
Team Coordination: Since resting affects the entire party, it encourages the group to plan strategically and agree on when it’s safe to rest for recovery or when to push forward without it.
Player Development 
In Tartaria Prima, character advancement doesn't rely on traditional leveling but instead focuses on gear improvements, titles, and faction affiliations to grant perks and abilities. However, characters do gain HP by increasing certain core attributes—specifically Strength and Intelligence.
Gaining HP:
Attribute-Based HP Gain: Every time a character increases their Strength or Intelligence—through any means, such as gear, training, or magical enhancements—they gain an additional 10 HP.
For example, if your character increases their Strength by 1 point, they immediately gain 10 HP added to their maximum health.
The same applies to increases in Intelligence, representing physical and mental fortitude.
Advancement Through Gear, Titles, and Factions:
Better Gear: Players will seek out and acquire more powerful weapons, armor, and equipment, which come with their own unique perks and advantages.
Titles and Faction Affiliations: Characters can earn titles or join factions, which provide special bonuses and abilities, giving them access to resources, special abilities, or even unique powers depending on the organization they ally with.
This system allows for a unique form of progression, where instead of traditional leveling, players enhance their survivability and power through gear, titles, and attribute boosts, gaining HP through Strength and Intelligence increases.

Towering Strength: Giants are known for their incredible strength. Gain +2 Strength.
Ancient Insight: The Giants’ deep understanding of architecture and technology gives them an innate ability to interact with Tartarian relics. Gain +1 to Intelligence when investigating ancient structures or artifacts.
Legacy of Power: Once per day, tap into the hidden Aetheric Power within. This ability allows them to channel energy into a relic, providing unexpected results like repairing a broken artifact or powering up dormant technology.
True Tartarians / Mud Dwellers:
Dark Vision: Generations spent underground have sharpened their eyesight. True Tartarians can see in darkness.
Aethercraft Mastery: Gain +2 to intelligence when using Aethercraft. True Tartarians can manipulate Aetherstone to create tools, weapons, and golems or shape the earth around them.
Relic Savvy: True Tartarians have developed an instinct for scavenging and repurposing ancient Tartarian relics. Gain +2 to Dexterity when dealing with Aetherstone or Tartarian technology.
Aetherstone Resilience: Prolonged exposure to Aetherstone has made them resistant to Aetheric damage. They take half damage from all Aetheric or Mud power-based attacks.
Reclaimers:
Urban Explorer: Reclaimers are skilled at navigating the ruins and forgotten cities of Tartaria. Gain +2 to Acrobatics and Stealth when exploring city ruins or navigating underground structures.
Ruins Specialist: Reclaimers have a natural aptitude for finding hidden traps, secret passages, and valuable artifacts. Gain +1 to Investigation and +1 to Woodsman when searching for relics or navigating dangerous areas.
Rogue’s Ingenuity: Reclaimers are quick thinkers, skilled with their hands. Gain +1 to Chicanery and a reroll when picking locks or disarming traps.
Relic Hunter: Reclaimers can detect hidden Aetherstone signatures, allowing them to sense nearby relics within 30 feet.
Architectural Sentinels:
Aetheric Constitution: Sentinels are powered by Aetherstone cores. They take half the damage from all energy-based attacks.
Indomitable Strength: Sentinels possess unmatched physical power. Gain +2 to Strength and +1 to intelligence.
Immunity to Time: Sentinels do not age, require food, or suffer from exhaustion. They are immune to the effects of hunger and fatigue.
Defensive Protocols: Sentinels can activate a defensive shield once per day, reducing incoming damage by half for three rounds.
Aether / Mud Golems:
Aetherstone Resilience: Aether Golems are nearly impervious to physical damage. Gain +2 to Strength and reduce all non-Aetheric damage by half.
Elemental Control: Aether Golems can manipulate the aethereal power in the mud around them, creating barriers or launching boulders at enemies. The surrounding Aetherstone and terrain fuel this ability; the aethereal ground must be within arm's reach or close (distance chart). Roll 1d6 for blocking power of incoming damage when using a shield, and roll 1d6 for damage when attacking. Can only use 1 of each power per encounter.
Regenerative Core: As long as an Aether Golem remains near Aetherstone deposits, they can regenerate, slowly restoring lost health. Roll 1d10 daily for free HP recharge up to the maximum.
Unknowing Masses:
Curious Mind: Once exposed to Tartaria’s secrets, members of the Unknowing Masses gain +2 in Intelligence and wisdom as they have become more adept at researching ancient lore and uncovering hidden knowledge after their discoveries.
Beginner’s Luck: Characters from the Unknowing Masses have an innate ability to adapt quickly to new situations. Once per day, reroll a failed Arbiter difficulty roll related to survival, combat, or interacting with relics.
Ignorant of Aetheric Power: Initially, members of the Unknowing Masses have no resistance to Aetheric energy or knowledge of Aetherstone. However, upon exposure to relics or Aetheric technology, they can gain abilities related to relic use. Common-level Aether and mud magic powers only can be used. Roll 1d6 for damage output, and roll 1d6 for blocking and restoration powers.
Aetherborn:
Aetheric Awakening: Aetherborn can instinctively sense nearby Aetherstone and Aetheric relics, gaining +2 to intelligence when interacting with these objects.
Noble Heritage: As descendants of the Tartarian elite, Aetherborn have an innate charisma and commanding presence. Gain +1 to Charisma, and gain one reroll daily when attempting to persuade or intimidate.
Latent Powers: Aetherborns possess latent Aetheric abilities tied to their lineage. Once per day, they can activate a surge of Aetheric energy to enhance their capabilities, such as speeding up their movements, increasing their strength, or creating a protective barrier for three rounds.
Destiny Unfolding: The longer Aetherborn interacts with Aetheric relics and Aetherstone, the more their powers grow, and they can use higher power magic/powers. They can unlock hidden abilities tied to their noble bloodline, but this comes at a cost—prolonged exposure may cause physical mutations or mental instability. Using rare or legendary Aether powers necessitates a 1d6 roll of damage to the player themselves.
Characters Faction:
Example: Eternal Dynasty Have backstories and histories that help you find your playstyle and place in the scheme of things. The five playable factions are listed below.
Mud Monarchs
OverviewThe Mud Monarchs are the secretive rulers of the modern world, descended from Tartaria’s noble class. They survived the Great Mud Flood by taking refuge in the highest, untouched remnants of the empire’s cities. Within these towering citadels, they preserved fragments of Tartaria’s Aetheric technology and used it to manipulate history, erasing Tartaria’s existence from the world’s collective memory. Over centuries, they established themselves as the global elite, pulling the strings of governments, economies, and societies while the world below forgot its true heritage.
To maintain their control, the Mud Monarchs concealed their existence, allowing modern civilization to develop under a false pretense that the world had risen from the ashes of a primitive past. Few know that the cities they inhabit are built atop ancient Tartarian ruins. Despite their wealth, influence, and hidden Aetheric technology, the Monarchs’ power is beginning to wane. Much of their knowledge of Tartaria has been lost, and their Aetheric machines are failing. With their grip on the world slipping, they become increasingly ruthless as factions like the Forgotten Order uncover the truth.
Forgotten Order
OverviewThe Forgotten Order is a faction dedicated to recovering and restoring Tartaria’s lost technology and knowledge. Formed by descendants of Tartaria’s engineers and scholars, the Order believes that humanity’s future lies in rediscovering the secrets of Aetheric power. They aim to unearth Tartaria’s relics, reverse-engineer its technology, and rebuild the world using the empire’s unparalleled advancements.
While the Mud Monarchs seek to suppress Tartaria’s history, the Forgotten Order works to unearth it, often operating in the shadows to avoid drawing attention to their discoveries. The Order sends explorers into Tartaria’s deepest ruins, risking their lives to recover ancient devices and texts. Their work is dangerous, not only because of the Aetheric anomalies within the ruins but because their activities often provoke the ire of rival factions like the Monarchs.
Reclaimers Guild
OverviewThe Reclaimers Guild is a loosely organized but highly influential group of treasure hunters, scavengers, and explorers. Their primary goal is profit, not ideology. They dive into the ruins of Tartaria to recover valuable relics, artifacts, and technological wonders, selling them to the highest bidder. Whether they work with the Mud Monarchs, the Forgotten Order, or secretive private collectors, the Reclaimers always put wealth and opportunity first. What began as informal alliances between individual scavengers grew into a full-fledged organization, exchanging maps, information, and resources.
Despite the allure of fortune, Reclaimers live dangerous lives, often operating in the treacherous ruins of Tartaria, evading traps, ancient sentinels, and rival factions. Their work brings them into contact with powerful groups who seek to exploit Tartaria’s technology for their own purposes, but the Reclaimers, motivated by profit, are more than willing to play all sides.
True Tartarians
OverviewThe True Tartarians, often called Mud Dwellers, are descendants of the original citizens of Tartaria who were trapped underground during the Great Mud Flood. For centuries, they have survived in the subterranean ruins of Tartaria, adapting to life in the depths by developing Aethercraft—a form of Aetheric manipulation that allows them to shape the mud and stone around them. The Mud Dwellers live in isolated, self-sufficient enclaves deep beneath the surface, worshipping the fallen Tartarian Giants as divine beings and preserving fragments of their ancient heritage.
While the world above has moved on, the True Tartarians see themselves as the rightful heirs of Tartaria. They view their survival as proof of their divine right to one day reclaim the surface. Through Aethercraft, they have built hidden cities, crafted weapons, and shaped golems, preparing for the day when they will restore Tartaria’s glory.
Eternal Dynasty
OverviewThe Eternal Dynasty is the oldest and most secretive faction, composed of Aetherborn—descendants of Tartaria’s noble bloodlines whose genes have awakened Aetheric powers. These individuals believe themselves to be the rightful rulers of the world and view the collapse of Tartaria as a divine test of their purity. The Dynasty has operated from the shadows for centuries, manipulating world events to ensure their bloodline survives and flourishes. Their ultimate goal is to reclaim Tartaria’s lost cities, reactivate the ancient Aetheric cores, and reshape the world under their rule.
Aetherborn possess supernatural abilities—such as telekinetic control, energy manipulation, and even temporal shifts—making them formidable in battle and political maneuvering. However, not every descendant carries the awakened Aetheric gene, so the Dynasty constantly searches for those with potential. Only the most powerful and pure can rise within the ranks, while the weak or unworthy are cast aside, sometimes sacrificed to fuel the Dynasty’s Aetheric machines.

Explanation of Base AC by Race
True Tartarians (Base AC: 10):Tartarians start with a standard AC but gain a +1 bonus when using Aetheric relics, making them more powerful as they attune to ancient gear. Early on, they need basic armor for protection but become formidable with relic-based gear.
Aetherborn (Base AC: 11):Aetherborn have a slight natural AC advantage due to their connection to Aetheric energy. They gain a +1 bonus when using Aetheric powers or runecasters, reflecting their magical nature.
Tartarian Giants (Base AC: 12):Giants have the highest natural AC due to their sheer size and strength, making them tougher than most. Their massive bodies make them harder to hit but can be balanced by slower movement or fewer armor options early on.
Unknowing Masses (Base AC: 10):This race has no inherent AC bonus. They rely entirely on armor or shields to increase their defense, making them vulnerable early but balanced as they acquire better gear.
Mud Dwellers (Base AC: 9):Mud Dwellers start with a slightly lower AC but gain a +1 bonus in dark or underground environments where their agility shines. They must rely on armor to boost their defenses outside of these conditions.
Forgotten Reclaimers (Base AC: 10):Reclaimers have average AC but gain a +1 bonus in constructed environments such as cities or ruins. They are adept at using terrain and cover to avoid attacks, balancing their AC across situations.
Architectural Sentinels (Base AC: 10):Architectural Sentinels have a standard AC but gain a +1 bonus when attuned to special runic gear or equipment. Their defenses increase with their mastery over runecasters, making them highly adaptable.

Player’s Backpack
Each player begins with a Player’s Backpack, a standard-issue backpack worn on the back. This backpack can hold up to ten items, providing essential storage for adventurers in Tartaria. The backpack is sturdy but simple, designed for survival and exploration without offering extra protection or enhanced features. Importantly, this is the only backpack available in the game and cannot be upgraded or replaced.
Carrying Limits: Players can carry ten items in their backpack, regardless of the item’s size or weight, unless a specific ability or perk grants additional slots.
Expanding Capacity: Some abilities or perks may add one or two slots, but no physical upgrades or alternative packs are available. These additions represent unique skills or traits, not modifications to the backpack.
Backpack Considerations: Since the backpack is worn on the back, players may need to consider access and visibility in certain scenarios, such as during combat or confined spaces. Retrieving an item during combat may require full action or have specific limitations based on the character’s situation.

Weapon Selection
Weapons are tied to a character’s core abilities and are essential in shaping combat style and strategy. Each weapon style and type requires a minimum score in one of the following abilities: Strength, Dexterity, Wisdom, or Charisma. You must meet or exceed the required ability level to use the weapon effectively. For example, a sword might require Strength 4, while a staff may need Wisdom 3. Characters specializing in Intelligence will focus on using runecasters to harness Aetheric powers rather than physical weapons, making them a unique force in combat.
Players may start with a single style of weapon or two spell runecasters, but they cannot simultaneously wield a weapon and an offensive rune caster.
Choosing Your Starting Weapon or Runecaster
Weapon Styles: There are four distinct weapon styles, each tied to a specific ability:
Dual Wield: Focuses on agility and speed, requiring Dexterity.
Single-Handed: A balanced approach with flexibility, requiring Strength or Dexterity.
Two-Handed: Heavy weapons that deal with high damage, requiring strength.
Ranged: Bows, crossbows, and other distance weapons requiring Dexterity or Wisdom.
Characters can only wield one weapon style at a time in combat, so consider carefully how you want to approach fights.
Runecasters Instead of Weapons: If you prefer, your character may select an offensive and defensive spell at character creation instead of weapons. Aetheric powers are tied to intelligence, and power is used more than physical force. However, if you choose a runecaster, you cannot wield a weapon in combat unless you switch away from the runecaster. Some runecasters provide unique bonuses and can target various enemies, offering an alternative to traditional combat.
Weapon Table Mechanics
Weapons in Tartaria Prima are categorized based on rarity, style, and the required ability. Each weapon is detailed in a table that contains the following information:
Weapon Type: The type of weapon (e.g., sword, dagger, bow, staff) and the style it belongs to (Dual Wield, Single-Handed, Two-Handed, or Ranged).
Related Ability: The core ability required to wield the weapon effectively (Strength, Dexterity, Wisdom, or Charisma). This ability score determines how well the character can handle the weapon.
Damage Output: The base damage the weapon deals in combat. The damage may scale with the weapon’s rarity and the character’s ability level.
Skill Bonuses: Some weapons offer additional bonuses for skills like acrobatics, stealth, parry, or endurance, depending on the weapon’s type and origin.
Rarity: Weapons are divided into four categories: Common, Uncommon, Rare, and Legendary. Players may select a common weapon at character creation, while higher-tier weapons are acquired through gameplay.
Weapon and Runecasters Choice in Combat
During gameplay, players must commit to either using a weapon or an offensive spell during combat:
Weapon Users: If you choose a weapon, you will rely on your related ability (Strength, Dexterity, Wisdom, or Charisma) to attack and deal damage, with the potential for additional skill bonuses.
Runecasters: If you choose to wield runecasters, you’ll use your Intelligence points to decide what level of caster to arm. Runecasters come with their own damage rolls and unique effects.
As characters progress through Tartaria, they encounter new and more powerful weapons, allowing for upgrades and special abilities that reflect the character's growth. Higher rarity weapons (Uncommon, Rare, and Legendary) can be acquired through exploration, trading, or purchasing from NPC vendors.

Weapon Explanation
Damage Rolls:
Common weapons (Charisma 1-4) deal 1d6 damage.
Uncommon weapons (Charisma 5-8) deal 2d6 damage.
Rare weapons (Charisma 9-10) deal 1d10 + 1d6 damage.
Legendary weapons (Charisma 11-12) deal 2d10 damage.
Faction-Based Gear:
Mud Dwellers: Primitive yet effective, often incorporating poison or bleed effects.
Unknowing Masses: High-tech, energy-based weapons with plasma and laser technology, focusing on precision and armor penetration.
Aetheric: Advanced weapons using Aetheric energy, dealing extra elemental damage and bypassing defenses.
Giants: Heavy weapons forged from Giant bones, delivering devastating blows to armored or large foes.
Charisma-Based Use:
A player’s Charisma determines what single-handed weapons they can use effectively.
Common items are widely accessible, while the more powerful or rare items require higher Charisma levels to wield efficiently.
Parry Mechanic:
Parry: As an instant action, players wielding single-handed weapons can parry incoming attacks.
Parrying allows them to roll 1d6 when attacked, reducing damage by that amount.
Parrying is less effective than blocking with a shield, but still provides a layer of defense in combat.
Example: If an enemy attacks a player, they can instantly declare a parry and roll 1d6 to reduce the incoming damage before it hits.
This table provides players with a wide array of single-handed weapons, allowing them to maintain versatility in combat while keeping one hand free for other actions. The parry mechanic offers an additional layer of defense, ensuring that single-weapon users can still mitigate some damage in battle.

Weapon Explanation
Damage Rolls:
Common ranged weapons (Wisdom 1-4) deal 1d6 damage.
Uncommon ranged weapons (Wisdom 5-8) deal 2d6 damage.
Rare ranged weapons (Wisdom 9-10) deal 1d10 + 1d6 damage.
Legendary ranged weapons (Wisdom 11-12) deal 2d10 damage.
Hand Use:
One-Handed: These ranged weapons can be used with one hand, allowing the other hand to remain free for additional actions.
Two-Handed: These weapons require both hands to use, but they offer greater range or more powerful attacks.
Faction-Based Gear:
Mud Dwellers: Their ranged weapons are primitive but effective, often using harpoons, slings, and hunting bows. They rely on basic ballistics and simple mechanics, and weather can hinder performance.
Unknowing Masses: Their advanced technology includes plasma-based firearms and laser weapons. These weapons often ignore physical armor but can be affected by environmental conditions.
Aetheric: These weapons use advanced Aetheric energy, offering precision, the ability to bypass defenses, and minimal weather impact.
Giants: Giant bone-crafted bows, spears, and javelins offer massive power, and some weapons are designed to deal with larger foes or structures.
Weather Impact:
Environmental conditions like rain, wind, and cold can affect accuracy, damage, and performance for certain weapons, particularly those from the Mud Dwellers and Unknowing Masses factions.
Aetheric weapons are generally unaffected by weather, while Giant weapons can struggle with precision in high winds due to their size and weight.
Parry Mechanic:
Parry: Ranged weapon users can parry weakly as an instant action.
When attacked, they can declare a parry and roll 1d6 to reduce incoming damage, but this offers limited defense compared to melee weapons or shields.
Example: A player wielding a ranged weapon like a Mud Harpoon Gun can attempt to parry an incoming attack, rolling 1d6 to reduce damage.
This table provides a balanced mix of ranged weapons, allowing for diverse playstyles based on a player’s Wisdom level and faction. Including one-handed and two-handed weapons gives players options for combat versatility, while the parry mechanic offers a small defensive boost for ranged fighters.
Tartaria Prima: Magic, Powers, and Runecasters
In Tartaria Prima, magic and powers are derived from ancient Tartarian knowledge, where the manipulation of Mud and Aetheria form the core of spellcasting. These abilities are tied to the use of runecasters—special inscriptions forged from unique types of mud or Aether. Characters who possess sufficient Intelligence and access to these runecasters can wield powerful magic and abilities.
How Aetheric Powers Work
Runecasters: All Aetheric powers and abilities come from runecasters forged from specific types of mud or Aether. These runecasters can either be:
Bought from markets or NPCs: The player can immediately use ready-made runecasters.
Crafted by Players: Players who possess the recipe for a rune caster and have 11 Intelligence or higher can create their runecasters. These runecasters can then be used by the player or distributed to others in the party.
Using Runecasters
Intelligence-Based Spellcasting: A character’s Intelligence determines what level of Aetheric powers and powers they can use. If a character has the appropriate rune caster and sufficient Intelligence, they can use powers based on their Aetheria level.

Acquiring Items from NPC Vendors
Before leaving camp for the first time, there will be an NPC vendor with a limited selection of items selected by the Arbiter. You may sell unwanted gear and use those funds and your starter funds for items to complete your loadout before leaving camp. This vendor is never heard from again in the game, and all items sold to them or offered by them are gone for the remainder of the campaign.
Additional NPC vendors found in the game world
Players can acquire new weapons, armor, and gear from NPC vendors or find them during exploration. Any item listed in this guide's various weapons, armor, and gear tables can be found and purchased, provided the Arbiter (the game master) determines that the vendor has it in stock.
NPC vendors are scattered worldwide, and players will encounter them in villages, cities, marketplaces, and even hidden outposts. These vendors offer a wide variety of items for sale, ranging from common everyday gear to rare and legendary artifacts.
Persuasion and Hidden Inventory
While many vendors may have a limited stock based on their specialty, players can sometimes persuade them to look in the back for additional or hidden items. If the vendor doesn’t immediately have the item the player is looking for, the Arbiter can allow the player to attempt Persuasion; this is based on an Arbiter's difficulty roll. Success on the roll may convince the vendor to reveal hidden or rarer items they weren’t initially offered. These rolls are a 1d20, and only a charisma modifier may be added.
The difficulty of the Arbiter assessment can vary based on the item's rarity, the relationship with the vendor faction, and the specific situation.
On a successful difficulty roll, the vendor should find a rare or uncommon item and offer it to the player for a negotiated price.
Other Ways to Find Gear
Beyond vendors, players can also find weapons and gear in loot chests, as mission rewards, or hidden worldwide. Items given as rewards or found in exploration often have unique properties or enhanced abilities that make them valuable beyond what’s found in a shop.

Races 

Tartarian Giants
Masters of the Lost Empire
The Tartarian Giants once strode across the landscape like living gods, the architects and rulers of a civilization unparalleled in human history. Towering over 10 feet tall, their very presence commanded awe and respect. They were physically imposing and intellectually superior, blending vast knowledge of Aetheric Power with unrivaled engineering prowess. These Giants created the monumental structures that still stand today—half-buried beneath Aetherstone but defiant against time. Their spires and domes, the remnants of their glory, remain etched into the landscape, their purpose half-forgotten but still humming with dormant Aetheric energy.
The Giants embedded their technology deep within their architecture, integrating it so seamlessly that it became indistinguishable from the natural environment. Aetheric grids powered their cities, pulling limitless energy from the atmosphere. This was second nature to the Giants—tools and technology so advanced that they seemed like magic to the world outside. Yet, despite their immense power and intellect, they overreached. In their quest to harness even greater forces of the Aether, they triggered the Great Aetherstone Flood. This catastrophe buried their empire beneath a thick layer of Aetheric-charged Aetherstone. Their towering cities crumbled, and the world as they knew it was lost.
Now, millennia later, the Tartarian Giants are spoken of only in legends. Their possible survival remains a tantalizing mystery. Sightings of cryptids like Sasquatch, Yeti, or Bigfoot fuel theories that these ancient beings may still walk the Earth, their descendants mutated but holding fragments of the power and knowledge of their ancestors. Those who believe the Giants still live see them as hidden guardians of the world’s secrets, watching from the shadows.
Daily Life (In Ancient Tartaria):A typical day for a Tartarian Giant in the height of their empire was one of grand ambition. In the morning, they would gather in their aethereal citadels, where colossal council chambers floated above the ground, supported by invisible streams of Aetheric energy. From these vantage points, they oversaw vast cities, planning expansion projects stretching across continents. Giants did not toil—they commanded legions of Aetheric constructs that carried out their designs with flawless precision.
Some Giants were scholars dedicated to advancing their understanding of Aetheric Power. They delved deep into the mysteries of the universe, experimenting with Aetheric currents and manipulating time, space, and even life itself. Others were warriors, protectors of the empire, wielding weapons infused with Aetheric energy, capable of tearing through any foe with a single strike. Their elite warriors trained endlessly, their exercises visible from miles away as they clashed with tremendous force, sending shockwaves through the very air.
Daily Life (In Modern Times):If any Giants survive, they are isolated, living in the most remote, impenetrable parts of the world. Their days would be spent maintaining the last flickering remnants of their once-mighty civilization. Hidden away in ancient citadels perched atop high mountains, they tend to the old Aetheric machines that still power the few remaining bastions of their empire. These tasks are not ones of routine maintenance but of survival—their ancient devices faltering, each day bringing them closer to failure.
Despite their dwindling numbers and fading knowledge, the Giants cling to the hope of rebuilding their lost empire. They remain vigilant, watching the modern world from afar, their ancient instincts as rulers and protectors still intact. They keep the last Aetheric cores active, knowing that their time may come again.
Role in the World:Giants hold the key to Tartaria’s past and perhaps its future. Choosing a Giant as a character allows you to walk in the footsteps of the lost empire’s greatest rulers, with the chance to unlock ancient secrets or reclaim the world that was once theirs.

Mud Dwellers 
Survivors of the Subterranean World
The True Tartarians are the last direct descendants of the original Tartarian people, those who survived the Great Aetherstone Flood but were trapped beneath the mud for centuries. Over time, their bodies and minds adapted to the harsh, subterranean world they call home. They live deep underground, beneath the forgotten cities of Tartaria, in vast caverns and ruined halls that echo the memories of a fallen empire. The True Tartarians have become masters of scavenging, reverse-engineering ancient technology, and manipulating the potent energies of Aetherstone—a substance they consider both a curse and a blessing.
Despite their harsh environment, the True Tartarians revere their ancestors, believing that the Tartarian Giants were not just rulers but gods. They worship the relics left behind, viewing the fallen empire as a sacred past they must preserve. Their culture revolves around the belief that the Giants will return one day, and when that time comes, the True Tartarians will rise again to reclaim the surface. Until then, they remain hidden from the world above, scavenging what little they can from the ruins and surviving through their mastery of Aether Tech—manipulating Aetherstone to power their makeshift settlements.
However, the True Tartarians are more than just scavengers. Over centuries, they have developed a unique form of magic known as Aethercraft, which allows them to harness the energy of Aetherstone in ways the surface world can barely comprehend. From constructing Aether Golems to shaping the very stone and earth around them, Aethercraft has become their lifeblood. However, its use often leaves its practitioners physically and mentally scarred by prolonged exposure to the unstable energies of Aetherstone.
Daily Life (In the Underground):The life of a True Tartarian is one of constant hardship and faith. Each day begins with a ritual honoring the Tartarian Giants, who they believe will one day return to lead them back to the surface. After the morning rites, groups of scavengers, called Seekers, venture into the deepest parts of the ancient ruins, navigating labyrinthine tunnels and long-forgotten cities, searching for relics and Aetherstone deposits.
In their underground enclaves, the True Tartarians live in tight-knit communities. Their homes are built within the hollowed-out ruins of the old world, reinforced with scavenged Aether Tech. These caverns glow with the eerie blue light of Aetherstone reactors, providing just enough power to sustain life in the pitch-black depths. Water and food are scarce, with underground farms growing strange bioluminescent fungi that sustain them.
At the heart of each settlement is the Aetherforge, a sacred place where Aethercraft practitioners work tirelessly to shape Aetherstone into weapons, tools, and devices that keep their society running. The most skilled Aethercraft users are revered as priests, able to manipulate the raw power of Aetherstone to defend their people or create powerful golems to protect the underground cities.
Despite the grim conditions, the True Tartarians have a strong sense of identity and pride in their ancient heritage. They train their young to be warriors, scholars, and engineers, ensuring the knowledge of their ancestors is passed down from generation to generation. Their ultimate goal is to reclaim the world above, but they must survive in the shadows for now.
Daily Life (In the Present):True Tartarians would see themselves as part of a sacred mission if they were to reach the surface. Above ground, these individuals are secretive and cautious, often disguising their features and avoiding large cities. The surface world is foreign and dangerous, full of people unaware of their true history. For the True Tartarians, every interaction with surface dwellers is an opportunity to gather resources or knowledge for the eventual return to glory.
Above all, True Tartarians believe they are the rightful heirs to the world, and everything they do is a step toward reclaiming that legacy.
Aethercraft Abilities:
Aether Golem Constructor: True Tartarians can craft temporary Aether Golems from Aetherstone and Aetheric energy to assist in combat or protect their settlements. These golems, however, are unstable and may collapse without warning.
Aetherstone Manipulation: Shape the earth and stone around you, creating barriers, pathways, or small weapons using Aetherstone energy. The effects are temporary but powerful.
Aaetheric Healing: A rare skill among Aethercraft practitioners, this ability allows True Tartarians to use Aetherstone to mend wounds. However, it comes at a personal cost of mental strain or physical scarring.
Role in the World:True Tartarians see themselves as the rightful heirs to the world of Tartaria, driven by the need to reclaim their lost empire and return to the surface. As a player, embodying a True Tartarian means balancing survival with pursuing ancient knowledge, fighting to rebuild a world that the surface-dwellers don’t even know exists.

Reclaimers
Adventurers of the Forgotten World
The Reclaimers are human adventurers descended from the people of Tartaria, though most do not know their true heritage. These individuals are explorers, scavengers, and opportunists who make their living diving into the ruins of ancient Tartaria to retrieve relics of immense value. Unknown to them, the artifacts they unearth are relics of their ancestors, imbued with powerful Aetheric energy. Many Reclaimers sell these treasures on the black market, unaware of the true significance of their finds.
Some Reclaimers operate as part of the Reclaimers Guild, a loose but powerful network of explorers who trade information, relics, and ancient knowledge. The Guild controls access to many of the most profitable ruins, maintaining secret archives on the locations of Tartarian cities and vaults. Other Reclaimers prefer to work independently, seeing themselves as mercenaries or treasure hunters, selling their finds to the highest bidder. Regardless of their methods, all Reclaimers share a common drive—the thrill of discovery and the promise of untold wealth.
Daily Life (In the Field):The life of a Reclaimer is one of constant danger and risk. Each morning begins with preparation—gearing up with ancient maps, climbing tools, and Aetherstone-powered devices to descend into the labyrinthine ruins of Tartaria. These expeditions are perilous; forgotten traps, collapsing structures, and the ever-present threat of Architectural Sentinels make each dive a test of skill and survival. Reclaimers must rely on their wits, agility, and knowledge of relics to navigate these ancient death traps.
Once inside, the day is spent scouring the depths of the ruins, searching for hidden passages or sealed vaults filled with Tartarian technology. A successful dive can bring immense rewards—an ancient artifact or Aetheric core can fetch a fortune. But failure often means death, as many Reclaimers never return from their dives. The lucky ones live to sell their finds on the black market or to secretive collectors, who are always eager to pay for forgotten technology.
Reclaimers often gather in hidden taverns or camps near the ruins at night, exchanging stories of their adventures, bartering relics, or negotiating their next job. While some enjoy the camaraderie, most are guarded, knowing that every Reclaimer is also a competitor for the rarest and most valuable finds. In this world, trust is fleeting, and betrayal is common.
Daily Life (Outside the Ruins):Above ground, Reclaimers live on the fringes of society. Some disguise their work as mere archaeology or relic hunting, but most keep a low profile, avoiding the authorities and powerful factions that seek to control Tartaria’s secrets. For Reclaimers, every interaction with others is a potential opportunity to gather information about new ruins, locate buyers for their relics, or form temporary alliances for dangerous dives.
Many Reclaimers work for shadowy organizations or wealthy elites with agendas concerning Tartaria’s lost technology. While some see themselves as noble explorers reclaiming their ancestors' heritage, others are driven purely by greed, willing to sell their findings to the highest bidder, regardless of the consequences. Either way, Reclaimers are deeply connected to the ancient past, though they may not even realize it.
Role in the World:As a Reclaimer, you are an adventurer, treasure hunter, and opportunist, delving into the forgotten ruins of Tartaria to uncover ancient relics and make your fortune. Whether driven by the thrill of discovery, the promise of wealth, or a secret desire to reclaim your ancestors’ lost legacy, your actions will shape the future of Tartaria. Choose wisely, as each relic you uncover holds great power and risk.

Architectural Sentinels
Guardians of the Ancient World
The Architectural Sentinels were once the elite, semi-conscious machines created by the Tartarian Giants to protect their vast cities and the Aetheric technology within. Programmed to ensure that only those with the knowledge of Tartaria’s past could access its most powerful relics, these tireless sentinels still patrol the forgotten ruins of the empire, guarding the buried relics from intruders. They are remnants of an age long gone, yet their loyalty to their creators and purpose remains unwavering.
Powered by the same Aetherstone that caused the Great Aetherstone Flood, the Sentinels are more than simple machines. They are sophisticated constructs capable of adapting to new threats, and their mechanical bodies are imbued with Aetheric energy, allowing them to heal over time, repair broken structures, and maintain the ancient technologies left behind by the Giants. Though many Sentinels have been damaged or fallen into disrepair over the centuries, those that remain are formidable guardians, ensuring that the secrets of Tartaria are not easily disturbed.
Daily Life (In Ancient Tartaria):In the age of the Tartarian Empire, the Sentinels were the silent protectors of the cities. Their daily existence was defined by vigilance, ensuring that the relics and technologies of Tartaria were safe from external threats. Each Sentinel was assigned to patrol a specific district, temple, or vault, enforcing the Giants' will by defending the Aetheric cores and protecting the ancient libraries of Tartarian knowledge.
Though the Sentinels did not have emotions or desires like organic beings, they were highly intelligent and capable of recognizing patterns and learning from past encounters. Their duty extended beyond mere defense—they could repair broken sections of their city, maintain power grids, and even perform ritualistic maintenance on the Aetheric cores. For the Tartarian Giants, the Sentinels were essential to the day-to-day function of their empire, an unyielding force that ensured its longevity.
Daily Life (In the Present):In modern times, the Sentinels remain a terrifying presence in the ruins of Tartaria. They patrol the crumbling cities daily, tirelessly guarding the ancient relics. They do not sleep or rest, and the passage of centuries means little to them. While their original programming still drives them to defend the relics, many Sentinels have developed quirks due to prolonged exposure to Aetherstone energy. Some display signs of curiosity or hesitation, while others may seem overly aggressive, their programming distorted by time.
When intruders enter the ruins, the Sentinels activate, their eyes glowing with Aetheric light as they scan for threats. Those who trigger their defenses are met with Aetheric-powered weapons, shields, and a relentless pursuit that often ends in the intruder's destruction. Few have survived a direct confrontation with a fully powered Sentinel.
Some Reclaimers and factions have attempted to deactivate or control the Sentinels, but these efforts rarely succeed. The few who have managed to bypass the Sentinel’s programming have found temporary reprieves, though the machines inevitably revert to their original function of guardianship.
Role in the World:Playing as an Architectural Sentinel places you in the unique position of a living relic of Tartaria. Whether continuing your ancient duty to guard the ruins or discovering a new purpose in the modern world, you possess knowledge and power that few others can claim. As a Sentinel, you stand at the intersection of the past and the future, with the ability to shape both in ways that no organic being can.

Mud Golems
Sentinels of Aetherstone
The Aether Golems are living manifestations of Aetheric energy, creatures created by the chaotic fallout of the Great Aetherstone Flood. Born from the collision of raw Aetheric power and the Aetherstone that now blankets the world, these golems are elemental in nature, composed of stone, mud, and energy. They are not natural beings but a byproduct of Tartaria’s downfall—walking remnants of the disaster that destroyed the empire.
Aether Golems are feared and revered in equal measure. Some believe the Tartarian Giants once constructed them as sentinels designed to protect the cities during the war. Others claim they are living extensions of the Aetherstone itself, manifestations of the energy that once powered an entire empire. Regardless of their origins, Aether Golems are immensely powerful creatures, capable of wreaking havoc on anyone who crosses their path.
Though they are typically seen as mindless creatures, their connection to Aetherstone gives them a mysterious awareness of their surroundings. The energy that binds them to the earth pulses with life, allowing them to sense nearby Aetheric disturbances or intruders in the ruins they roam. This makes them formidable guardians, often appearing without warning to crush intruders who dare approach the lost technologies of Tartaria.
Daily Life (In Ancient Tartaria):In the ancient world, Aether Golems were rarely seen but highly revered as the ultimate line of defense for the Tartarian Empire. Summoned by the most powerful Aetheric mages, these golems were created to defend sacred sites, powerful relics, and Aetheric cores from outside forces. Unlike the mechanical Architectural Sentinels, the Aether Golems were elemental beings of immense power, capable of merging with the environment to launch devastating ambushes on enemies.
The Golems were connected to the Aetheric ley lines that powered Tartaria’s cities, drawing strength from the very ground they were summoned from. Their purpose was singular: to protect the empire’s most valuable assets at all costs. Many saw them as walking gods, divine entities that embodied the raw power of Tartaria’s Aetheric mastery. Even the Tartarian Giants treated them respectfully, knowing that once summoned, the Golems would defend their charge until their destruction.
Daily Life (In the Present):In modern times, the Aether Golems wander the ruined cities of Tartaria, taethered to the Aetherstone deposits that still pulse with life beneath the surface. Though their creators are long gone, the Golems continue their ancient duties, protecting relics, Aetherstone cores, and lost Aetheric technology from those who would disturb it. Their forms have been eroded over centuries, and they now resemble massive, hulking figures of rock and mud, glowing with the soft, eerie light of Aetherstone energy.
Aether Golems are rarely active unless provoked by the presence of intruders. When they sense movement or Aetheric disturbances near their domain, they rise from the earth, their bodies reforming from the Aetherstone around them. Once engaged, they are relentless in their pursuit, smashing through stone and metal to reach their target.
Despite their fearsome reputation, some factions and individuals have tried to tame or control the Golems with varying degrees of success. Those who have attempted to bind an Aether Golem using relics or ancient powers have often been destroyed by the force they sought to control. Only a few have used ancient Tartarian technologies to command these behemoths, though even these methods are unpredictable and dangerous.
Role in the World:As an Aether Golem, you are a walking manifestation of Tartaria’s lost power. Bound to the ancient ley lines of Aetherstone, your very existence is a testament to the empire’s mastery over Aetheric energy. Whether you continue your ancient duty to protect the relics of Tartaria or seek a new purpose in the modern world, you are a force of nature—unstoppable and eternal.

Unknowing Masses
The Ignorant Inheritors of a Forgotten Past
The Unknowing Masses represent the majority of humanity living on the surface—descendants of Tartaria who have long forgotten their empire’s past. They go about their lives unaware of the ancient powers and secrets buried beneath their feet. To them, the world is a place of progress, industry, and normalcy, with little knowledge of the ancient ruins, lost relics, or the Aetheric energy that once powered the Tartarian Empire.
For these people, the idea of a lost empire is a myth, an urban legend dismissed by scholars and the general public. Conspiracy theories about the Great Aetherstone Flood and stories of Tartarian Giants are scoffed at and ridiculed. Yet, some among the Unknowing Masses are starting to awaken to the truth through personal discoveries or stumbling upon ancient relics that hold the key to their forgotten heritage.
The Unknowing Masses are often the last to realize the full scope of the events still shaping their world. Though they live in ignorance, some individuals question the official histories they’ve been told, especially after coming into contact with strange artifacts or mysterious factions seeking to reclaim Tartaria’s lost power. Once exposed to the truth, members of the Unknowing Masses find themselves on a path that could lead them to great power, danger, or the truth about their origins.
Daily Life (Before the Discovery):For most members of the Unknowing Masses, life is typical of any modern society. They live in bustling cities, engage in business, pursue education, and do their daily routines without a second thought. They enjoy modern comforts like technology, electricity, and communication, not realizing that much of what they take for granted is derived from ancient Tartarian technology repurposed by the ruling factions.
Most people of the Unknowing Masses grow up hearing rumors of buried cities, hidden technologies, or relic hunters. Still, these stories are typically brushed off as conspiracy theories or folklore. Tales of Tartarian Giants lost civilizations and Aetherstone floods are passed around as harmless urban legends, with few realizing just how much of it is true. To the Unknowing Masses, the idea of a grand empire lying beneath their feet seems far-fetched, and most are content to live their lives within the confines of their daily experiences.
Daily Life (After the Discovery):Everything changes for those who stumble upon an ancient relic or discover hidden knowledge of Tartaria’s past. Finding a single piece of ancient technology—a forgotten Aetheric device, a map of Tartarian ruins, or even a mysterious artifact—sets them on a journey that unravels everything they thought they knew. The discovery of such an artifact often attracts the attention of powerful factions, Reclaimers, or True Tartarians, who want to recruit, manipulate, or silence those who have stumbled upon the truth.
These individuals begin to uncover the secret histories buried beneath centuries of misinformation. They may join secretive groups that seek to reclaim Tartaria’s lost technology, or they may go on the run, pursued by those who would stop them from revealing the truth. Life as a member of the Unknowing Masses after the discovery is one of danger, curiosity, and the gradual realization that the world is not what it seems. For some, this is a path to enlightenment and power; for others, it leads to peril and conspiracy.
Role in the World:As a member of the Unknowing Masses, you begin your journey unaware of the true history and power of Tartaria. But once you uncover your first relic, everything changes. Your path will take you from ignorance to enlightenment as you become a key player in the battle for Tartaria’s forgotten knowledge. Whether you join the Reclaimers, ally with the True Tartarians, or forge your 
destiny, your discoveries will shape the world's future.

Aetherborn
The Hidden Heirs of Tartaria
The Aetherborn are rare individuals who carry the ancient bloodline of Tartaria. Though they live among the Unknowing Masses on the surface, they are not truly part of the modern world. Family whispers and old rumors haunt their past—stories of long-lost relatives who displayed strange gifts, relatives who were outcasts and shunned for being different. Most Aetherborn grow up unaware of their true lineage, hearing only fragmented legends of distant ancestors who once wielded great power before they were forced into hiding.
As they come of age, the Aetherborn begin to experience odd phenomena: inexplicable visions, strange sensations when near relics of the past, or surges of Aetheric energy they cannot explain. Though initially terrifying, these gifts are remnants of their forgotten heritage—powers connected to the long-lost Tartarian throne. Some Aetherborn may even be the last heirs to noble houses, their ancestors hiding them away to avoid persecution or death during Tartaria’s fall.
For the Aetherborn, the truth of their origins is a double-edged sword. On one hand, they may be the last surviving members of Tartaria’s ruling elite, capable of reclaiming their throne and shaping the world's future. On the other hand, these powers set them apart, making them targets for factions, scavengers, and those who seek to control the lost power of Tartaria. As they discover more about their lineage, they may find themselves drawn into the tangled web of Tartaria’s politics, hunted by those who fear the return of its rightful rulers.
Daily Life (Before Awakening):Before their powers manifest, the Aetherborn grow up just like any member of the Unknowing Masses. They attend schools, work ordinary jobs, and live in bustling cities. However, they often feel like outsiders, sensing a deeper connection to the world around them that they cannot explain. Family stories about long-lost relatives who were “different” or “cursed” sometimes linger in their thoughts, though they rarely give them much credence.
Their lives begin to change when they first come into contact with ancient relics, Aetherstone, or Aetheric energy. They might feel strange vibrations in the air or experience visions of the past—brief flashes of Tartaria’s lost glory. As they draw closer to their true heritage, these powers intensify, marking them as something other than human.
Daily Life (After Awakening):Once the Aetherborn begin to manifest their powers, their lives change forever. They are no longer able to deny their connection to Tartaria, so they become obsessed with finding the truth about their origins. Some may seek out the True Tartarians, hoping for answers about their bloodline, while others may join the Reclaimers or shadowy factions to unearth more relics and uncover their destiny. Many find themselves hunted by those who recognize the signs of their noble heritage, while others are feared or shunned by those who cannot understand their gifts.
In their search for answers, the Aetherborn realize they may be the last of Tartaria’s royal bloodlines, heir to a throne buried under centuries of mud and rubble. With this knowledge comes great responsibility—and great danger. Whether they embrace their destiny or flee from it, their actions will ripple through the world, for better or worse.
Role in the World:The Aetherborn are hidden heirs to Tartaria’s throne, torn between their ordinary upbringing and extraordinary destiny. As players, they must navigate the complexities of their dual heritage, facing the challenges of their newfound powers while uncovering the secrets of their noble lineage. Whether they embrace their birthright or forge a new path in the modern world, their decisions will have far-reaching consequences, and their powers may change the fate of Tartaria itself.
The Five Factions:
Mud Monarchs: Architects of a False World Order
Faction Name: Mud MonarchsMotto: "In shadow, we reign. In silence, we shape the world."
OverviewThe Mud Monarchs are the secretive rulers of the modern world, descended from Tartaria’s noble class. They survived the Great Mud Flood by taking refuge in the highest, untouched remnants of the empire’s cities. Within these towering citadels, they preserved fragments of Tartaria’s Aetheric technology and used it to manipulate history, erasing Tartaria’s existence from the world’s collective memory. Over centuries, they established themselves as the global elite, pulling the strings of governments, economies, and societies while the world below forgot its true heritage.
To maintain their control, the Mud Monarchs concealed their existence, allowing modern civilization to develop under a false pretense that the world had risen from the ashes of a primitive past. Few know that the cities they inhabit are built atop ancient Tartarian ruins. Despite their wealth, influence, and hidden Aetheric technology, the Monarchs’ power is beginning to wane. Much of their knowledge of Tartaria has been lost, and their Aetheric machines are failing. With their grip on the world slipping, they become increasingly ruthless as factions like the Forgotten Order uncover the truth.
Origins of the Mud MonarchsWhen the Mud Flood swallowed Tartaria’s cities, the empire’s ruling families fled to the tallest spires and fortresses, surviving the disaster. These noble families preserved what little they could of Tartaria’s Aetheric technology and began their work to rewrite history. Over time, they buried the truth beneath layers of lies, installing new governments, dynasties, and shadow organizations to maintain their rule over the world.
The Mud Monarchs hoarded the remaining Aetheric devices, using them to control industries, political systems, and economies across the globe. They worked tirelessly to suppress knowledge of Tartaria, ensuring that any attempts to uncover the empire’s true history were quashed through their control of academic institutions, media, and technological innovation.
Structure of the Mud Monarchs
The Council of Sovereigns:The ruling body of the Mud Monarchs was composed of the seven most powerful members of their noble families. The Council wields immense influence, controlling the remaining Aetheric relics and the power cores that sustain their hidden cities. Though they work together, the Council is rife with political infighting, with each Sovereign vying for greater control.
The Scions:Direct descendants of the Monarchs’ bloodlines, the Scions serve as enforcers, diplomats, and agents. Each Scion commands a network of spies, assassins, and political manipulators who maintain the global status quo. In public, they often appear as corporate leaders or political figures, while behind the scenes, they orchestrate world events.
The Black Cloaks:The secret police of the Mud Monarchs, the Black Cloaks, are tasked with silencing dissent and ensuring that the public remains ignorant of Tartaria’s history. They erase evidence, assassinate threats, and manipulate information to ensure that any whispers of Tartaria’s true past are dismissed as mere conspiracy theories. They serve the Monarchs with fanatical loyalty.
The Relic Keepers:Engineers and scientists responsible for maintaining the few remaining Aetheric devices, the Relic Keepers, are crucial to the survival of the Mud Monarchs. However, their knowledge is fragmented, and as technology degrades, they are running out of solutions. Many fear the day when they can no longer maintain Tartaria’s machines.
Philosophy and Goals of the Mud Monarchs
Total Control: The Monarchs believe in maintaining absolute control over society through manipulation, not brute force. They view themselves as the rightful rulers, capable of steering the world in the right direction.
Preserve the Illusion: They work tirelessly to suppress the truth about Tartaria, ensuring that the masses remain blind to the empire’s existence and the Aetheric technology beneath their feet.
Survival of the Dynasty: As their Aetheric devices fail, the Monarchs are becoming more desperate to find a way to restore their power. Their goal is to survive and ensure their dynasty remains in control, no matter the cost.
Rivalries and Relations
The Forgotten Order: The Monarchs see the Forgotten Order as a direct threat. The Order’s attempts to uncover Tartaria’s technology could destabilize the world and expose the Monarchs’ lies. The Monarchs use their vast resources to sabotage the Order’s operations, burying discoveries and eliminating key figures.
The Eternal Dynasty: While the Mud Monarchs do not fully understand the extent of the Aetherborn Dynasty, they suspect a rival group is working in the shadows. A secret war of assassinations and espionage is brewing between the two factions, especially if the Aetherborn unlock Tartaria’s true Aetheric potential.
Reclaimers Guild: The Monarchs tolerate the Reclaimers Guild as long as they remain useful, purchasing relics and using the Guild’s network to keep tabs on Tartarian ruins. However, they keep a close watch on the Guild to ensure that no critical relics are sold to outsiders.
True Tartarians: The Monarchs view the Mud Dwellers as primitive remnants of the empire—unsophisticated and unworthy of their attention. They dismiss the True Tartarians’ Aethercraft as a crude imitation of true Aetheric science.
Mud Monarchs Character Options
Aetheric Hoarder: Gain a +2 bonus when interacting with Aetheric devices. Your character has access to the most advanced remnants of Tartaria’s technology.
Master of Shadows: Gain a +2 bonus to Deception and Persuasion. You have been trained to manipulate others without their awareness.
Relic Keeper Knowledge: Once per session, tap into the Monarchs’ archives to gain critical information on Aetheric technology or political matters.
Requirements to Join the Mud Monarchs
Wealth or Resources: Must control an organization, city, or faction worth at least 10,000 TC in assets, proving your influence in the modern world.
Faction Points: Accumulate 10 faction points by completing covert missions that undermine rival factions, retrieve Aetheric relics, or silence those threatening to expose Tartaria’s true history.
Aetheric Knowledge: You must possess or have exclusive access to at least one functioning Aetheric relic or device, showcasing your understanding of Tartaria’s technology.
Title: Earn the title of Shadow Envoy by demonstrating your mastery of subterfuge, diplomacy, and loyalty to the Monarchs’ cause.
Eternal Dynasty: The Aetherborn Cabal
Faction Name: Eternal DynastyMotto: "Our blood, our empire, our destiny."
OverviewThe Eternal Dynasty is the oldest and most secretive faction, composed of Aetherborn—descendants of Tartaria’s noble bloodlines whose genes have awakened Aetheric powers. These individuals believe themselves to be the rightful rulers of the world and view the collapse of Tartaria as a divine test of their purity. The Dynasty has operated from the shadows for centuries, manipulating world events to ensure their bloodline survives and flourishes. Their ultimate goal is to reclaim Tartaria’s lost cities, reactivate the ancient Aetheric cores, and reshape the world under their rule.
Aetherborn possess supernatural abilities—such as telekinetic control, energy manipulation, and even temporal shifts—making them formidable in battle and political maneuvering. However, not every descendant carries the awakened Aetheric gene, so the Dynasty constantly searches for those with potential. Only the most powerful and pure can rise within the ranks, while the weak or unworthy are cast aside, sometimes sacrificed to fuel the Dynasty’s Aetheric machines.
Origins of the Eternal DynastyThe Eternal Dynasty traces its origins to Tartaria’s royal families, who used Aetheric preservation chambers to survive the Mud Flood. These nobles reawakened centuries later, realizing the world had changed. Rather than emerging publicly, they decided to remain hidden, manipulating events from behind the scenes. They established secret sanctuaries deep within Tartaria’s highest spires, waiting for the right moment to reclaim their empire.
Today, the Eternal Dynasty is a shadowy force, with Aetherborn serving as political puppeteers, influencing global affairs from the shadows. They believe that their Aetheric powers and blood purity give them the divine right to rule, and they actively seek to strengthen their bloodlines while eliminating any potential threats to their dominance.
Structure of the Eternal Dynasty
The High Council:The supreme ruling body of the Dynasty, composed of the most powerful and ancient Aetherborn families. Each member of the High Council wields significant influence, and they often engage in political intrigue to ensure their bloodline remains dominant.
The Clans:The Dynasty is divided into several clans, each representing a branch of the Tartarian royal lineage. Clans operate autonomously but swear fealty to the High Council. Within each clan, Aetherborn are ranked based on their Aetheric power and blood purity. Rivalry between clans is fierce, with each seeking to prove its superiority.
The Seekers:The Seekers are responsible for finding and recruiting newly awakened Aetherborn. These individuals are drawn to the Dynasty through subtle manipulation or prophetic visions of their ancestors. The Seekers ensure no potential Aetherborn is overlooked, offering them a place within the Dynasty or eliminating them if they refuse the call.
The Arbiters:Arbiters oversee the Trials of Awakening, where new Aetherborn must prove their loyalty and Aetheric capabilities. These life-threatening trials test both the mental and physical strength of candidates, determining whether they are worthy of joining the Dynasty’s inner circle. Those who fail are discarded, while the successful are inducted into the ranks of the Aetherborn elite.
Philosophy and Goals of the Eternal Dynasty
Bloodline Supremacy: The Dynasty believes in the divine right of their pure bloodlines to rule the world. Only those with Aetheric abilities are considered worthy of leadership.
Reclaim the Throne: Their ultimate goal is to overthrow the modern world order and restore Tartaria to its former glory, with Aetherborn ruling over all.
Secrecy and Manipulation: The Dynasty operates in the shadows, subtly manipulating events, guiding history, and waiting for the perfect moment to reveal themselves and reclaim their empire.
Rivalries and Relations
The Mud Monarchs: The Dynasty views the Mud Monarchs as usurpers, holding power that rightfully belongs to the Aetherborn. A covert war of assassinations and espionage exists between the two factions.
The Forgotten Order: While the Dynasty respects the Order’s dedication to recovering Tartaria’s technology, they view them as naive. The Dynasty believes only they can truly control the empire’s power.
Reclaimers Guild: The Dynasty sees the Reclaimers as mere scavengers with no respect for Tartaria’s relics. However, they occasionally manipulate the Guild into retrieving valuable artifacts on their behalf.
True Tartarians: Though they share a common ancestry, the Dynasty views the Mud Dwellers as inferior, trapped in primitivism and unworthy of attention.
Eternal Dynasty Character Options
Aetheric Bloodline: Gain a +2 bonus to Aetheric manipulation abilities.
Bloodbound Loyalty: You cannot betray the Dynasty without severe mental and physical consequences. Breaking this bond causes intense pain and a loss of Aetheric abilities.
Legacy of the Ancients: Gain a +1 bonus to interactions with Tartarian relics and technology. Your bloodline resonates with ancient devices, giving you an innate understanding of their functions.
Requirements to Join the Eternal Dynasty
Bloodline Purity: Prove direct descent from a noble Tartarian lineage and possess awakened Aetheric abilities.
Faction Points: Accumulate 15 faction points by completing missions that serve the Dynasty’s interests, such as acquiring Tartarian relics, eliminating rivals, or influencing political events.
Title: Earn the title of Seeker of the Blood by passing the Trials of Awakening, which test your physical, mental, and Aetheric strength.
Skill Requirements: Must have +4 in Aetheria due to the high level of Aetheric manipulation required by the Dynasty.
Forgotten Order: Seekers of the Lost Truth
Faction Name: Forgotten OrderMotto: "Through the ruins, we shall rise again."
OverviewThe Forgotten Order is a faction dedicated to recovering and restoring Tartaria’s lost technology and knowledge. Formed by descendants of Tartaria’s engineers and scholars, the Order believes that humanity’s future lies in rediscovering the secrets of Aetheric power. They aim to unearth Tartaria’s relics, reverse-engineer its technology, and rebuild the world using the empire’s unparalleled advancements.
While the Mud Monarchs seek to suppress Tartaria’s history, the Forgotten Order works to unearth it, often operating in the shadows to avoid drawing attention to their discoveries. The Order sends explorers into Tartaria’s deepest ruins, risking their lives to recover ancient devices and texts. Their work is dangerous, not only because of the Aetheric anomalies within the ruins but because their activities often provoke the ire of rival factions like the Monarchs.
Origins of the Forgotten OrderThe Order’s origins date back to the survivors of Tartaria’s engineering class. After the empire’s collapse, these individuals believed that reviving its technology was the only way to save humanity from the chaos that followed. Over time, they became a secretive society, passing down fragmented knowledge and training new members in the art of Aetheric science.
Today, the Forgotten Order operates as an underground network of scholars, engineers, and adventurers. Though their resources are limited, they have made significant strides in understanding Tartaria’s technology, occasionally managing to restore ancient Aetheric machines to partial functionality.
Structure of the Forgotten Order
The Keepers of Knowledge:Archivists and scholars responsible for studying and preserving any documents recovered from Tartaria. They piece together the fragmented history of the empire and educate new members in the principles of Aetheric science.
The Engineers:These members are tasked with reverse-engineering Tartaria’s technology. They conduct dangerous experiments, sometimes with catastrophic results, but their successes have allowed the Order to rebuild a few working Aetheric devices.
The Seekers:Explorers and adventurers who venture into Tartarian ruins to recover lost relics. They are the Order’s frontline operatives, navigating ancient traps and Aetheric anomalies to bring back valuable artifacts.
The Council of Arcanists:The most senior Aetheric theorists and scientists, the Arcanists oversee the Order’s most ambitious projects. They direct the recovery of key technologies and guide the Order’s overall mission.
Philosophy and Goals of the Forgotten Order
Restore Tartaria: The Order’s goal is to rebuild Tartaria’s empire by using its technology to reshape the world and bring about a new golden age.
Preserve Knowledge: They work tirelessly to preserve and share the knowledge they uncover, believing that humanity’s future lies in understanding Tartaria’s past.
Oppose the Monarchs: The Order views the Mud Monarchs as enemies of progress, actively sabotaging their efforts to suppress Tartaria’s history.
Rivalries and Relations
The Mud Monarchs: The Monarchs actively hunt the Order’s members, seeing them as a threat to their control over Tartaria’s secrets.
The Eternal Dynasty: Though the Order knows little about the Dynasty, they have encountered Aetherborn during their explorations. The Dynasty’s secretive nature makes them a potential threat.
Reclaimers Guild: The Order views the Reclaimers as opportunists, but they occasionally collaborate to recover important artifacts.
True Tartarians: The Order sees the Mud Dwellers as potential allies, respecting their knowledge of Aethercraft and establishing diplomatic ties with their enclaves.
Forgotten Order Character Options

Aetheric Engineer: Gain a +2 bonus to Aetheria involving relics and ancient devices.
Seeker’s Instinct: Gain a +2 bonus to Perception or Stealth when exploring Tartarian ruins.
Requirements to Join the Forgotten Order

Academic Reputation: Earn the title of Arcanist by proving your expertise in Aetheric theory and conducting at least one successful field mission.

Reclaimers Guild: Opportunists of the Lost World
Faction Name: Reclaimers GuildMotto: "In the ruins, we find our fortune."
OverviewThe Reclaimers Guild is a loosely organized but highly influential group of treasure hunters, scavengers, and explorers. Their primary goal is profit, not ideology. They dive into the ruins of Tartaria to recover valuable relics, artifacts, and technological wonders, selling them to the highest bidder. Whether they work with the Mud Monarchs, the Forgotten Order, or secretive private collectors, the Reclaimers always put wealth and opportunity first. What began as informal alliances between individual scavengers grew into a full-fledged organization, exchanging maps, information, and resources.
Despite the allure of fortune, Reclaimers live dangerous lives, often operating in the treacherous ruins of Tartaria, evading traps, ancient sentinels, and rival factions. Their work brings them into contact with powerful groups who seek to exploit Tartaria’s technology for their own purposes, but the Reclaimers, motivated by profit, are more than willing to play all sides.
Origins of the Reclaimers GuildThe Guild’s origins trace back to the earliest treasure hunters, scavengers who picked through the remnants of Tartaria after the empire’s collapse. Over time, these individuals began to share information, forming loose alliances that eventually developed into a network. These early Reclaimers became experts in navigating Tartarian ruins, selling maps, relics, and artifacts on the underground market.
Today, the Guild has grown into a powerful force with connections to black markets, elite buyers, and global factions. Reclaimers sell rare Tartarian technology to whoever offers the most, and their influence has earned them the respect—and distrust—of nearly every faction operating in the shadows of the modern world.
Structure of the Reclaimers Guild
The Grand Assembly:The Reclaimers Guild is loosely led by the Grand Assembly, a gathering of the most influential explorers, leaders, and traders within the Guild. The Assembly coordinates major deals, shares intelligence about newly discovered ruins, and sets loose guidelines for Guild operations. Although they cannot enforce these rules, their influence is considerable.
Independent Reclaimers:Most Reclaimers operate independently or in small groups, taking on expeditions as they see fit. These freelancers sell their finds to buyers in the underground market or through the Grand Assembly. Some are opportunistic and cutthroat, while others maintain a sense of honor among thieves.
The Cartographers:A specialized branch within the Guild, the Cartographers are responsible for mapping the ruins of Tartaria. They maintain secret archives of ancient city layouts, hidden treasure caches, and hazardous sites. These maps are valuable and are often sold to the highest bidder.
The Mercenaries:Some Reclaimers serve as mercenaries, specializing in dangerous combat expeditions and artifact recovery missions. Powerful factions often hire them to retrieve valuable relics or carry out high-risk missions in Tartarian ruins.
Philosophy and Goals of the Reclaimers Guild
Profit Above All: Reclaimers are driven by wealth and discovery, selling Tartaria’s relics to the highest bidder.
Independence: Every Reclaimer can chart their own path, and the Guild values this freedom of operation.
Exploration and Risk: The Guild thrives on the danger of exploration. Every relic is a potential fortune, and the risk of death is just part of the job.
Rivalries and Relations
The Mud Monarchs: The Monarchs often buy relics from the Reclaimers but maintain a cautious distance. The Guild knows the Monarchs will turn on them if they become too powerful.
The Forgotten Order: The Reclaimers respect the Order’s dedication to Tartaria’s restoration but view them as competitors. Collaboration occurs occasionally, but their goals differ greatly.
The Eternal Dynasty: The Dynasty’s secrecy makes them a mystery to the Guild, though some Aetherborn relics have passed through Reclaimer hands.
True Tartarians: The Mud Dwellers view the Reclaimers with suspicion, though they sometimes trade relics. Clashes over ruins and resources are common.
Reclaimers Guild Character Options
Relic Hunter’s Instinct: Gain a +2 bonus to Investigation and Perception when exploring Tartarian ruins.
Opportunist: Gain a +2 bonus to Coercion or Persuasion when negotiating the sale of relics.
Freelancer’s Flexibility: Reroll one failed Arbiter difficulty roll or Parry roll per session, reflecting your adaptability in dangerous situations.
Requirements to Join the Reclaimers Guild
Relic Hunter: You must have recovered and sold at least three Tartarian relics.
Faction Points: Accumulate 10 faction points by completing successful expeditions or securing valuable artifacts for the Guild.
Cartographer’s Mark: You must have explored and mapped a previously undocumented Tartarian ruin.
Title: Earn the title of Master Explorer by completing a particularly dangerous or profitable expedition.
True Tartarians: Survivors of the Subterranean World
Faction Name: True TartariansMotto: "From the depths, we will rise again."
Origins of the True TartariansWhen the Mud Flood buried Tartaria, many of its people were trapped in the lower levels of cities, left to survive in the darkness. Over time, these survivors adapted, building new lives within the underground ruins. They scavenged fragments of Aetheric technology and developed their own form of Aethercraft, using the Aetheric mud around them to shape their environment. The True Tartarians became a self-sufficient society, revering the Tartarian Giants as gods who would one day return to lead them to victory.
Today, the True Tartarians remain hidden from the surface world, but they believe their time is coming. Their underground cities are thriving, and their mastery of Aethercraft continues to grow.
Structure of the True Tartarians
The Aether Priests:The spiritual leaders of the Mud Dwellers, the Aether Priests are masters of Aethercraft, able to manipulate Aetheric-infused stone and mud. They are revered as prophets who communicate with the Tartarian Giants and interpret signs from the Aetheric realm.
The Chieftains:Each underground settlement is led by a Chieftain, responsible for organizing expeditions into deeper ruins to recover relics and resources. They are also tasked with defending their cities from outsiders and creatures that roam the depths.
The Seekers of the Depths:The Seekers are the adventurers and scavengers of the Mud Dwellers. Sent into dangerous areas, they recover Tartarian relics and Aetheric resources. They are highly skilled in Aethercraft and navigate treacherous underground passages.
The Aetherforgers:Aetherforgers are the engineers of the Mud Dwellers, responsible for using Aethercraft to shape tools, weapons, and golems. They are skilled in manipulating Aetherstone, a rare material found in the deepest parts of Tartaria’s ruins.
Philosophy and Goals of the True Tartarians
Reclaim the Surface: The Mud Dwellers see themselves as the rightful heirs to Tartaria and aim to retake the surface, restoring the empire’s glory.
Preserve the Past: They are dedicated to preserving Tartaria’s heritage and passing down knowledge of Aethercraft to future generations.
Worship the Giants: The Tartarian Giants are worshipped as divine beings, and their return is awaited as a sign that the time to reclaim the surface has come.
Rivalries and Relations
The Mud Monarchs: The Mud Dwellers view the Monarchs as false rulers who have corrupted Tartaria’s legacy.
The Forgotten Order: Though they respect the Order’s dedication to restoring Tartaria, they see them as misguided. The Mud Dwellers believe only Aethercraft can truly restore the empire.
The Eternal Dynasty: The Dynasty is seen as a potential rival, as their Aetherborn bloodline poses a challenge to the Mud Dwellers' claim to Tartaria’s throne.
Reclaimers Guild: The Mud Dwellers are cautious of the Reclaimers, viewing them as opportunists with no respect for Tartaria’s history.
True Tartarians Character Options
Aethercraft Mastery: Gain a +2 bonus to Aetheric manipulation when using Aetherstone.
Dark Vision: Gain superior vision in darkness, seeing clearly in arm’s reach and close range. diminished but usable vision in far range, details are unobservable.
Relic Scavenger: You will receive both loot items listed in the table. No even/odd roll is used if you land the final blow that kills an enemy.
Aethercraft Master: You must demonstrate proficiency in Aaetheric manipulation and craft an Aetherstone-infused weapon or tool.
Faction Points: Accumulate 10 faction points by aiding in reclaiming ancient ruins or defending Mud Dweller enclaves.
Title: Earn the title of Aether Priest by proving your mastery of Aethercraft and loyalty to the Tartarian Giants.
Skill Requirements: Must have +3 in Stealth or Survival.

Lore
What if the world we know is built upon the buried ruins of a civilization so advanced it mastered the very forces of nature?
Imagine a time when towering spires pierced the clouds, their brilliance powered not by the sun or man-made fuels but by the fabric of the universe itself. This civilization harnessed an elemental force—an energy that did not burn or deplete but flowed freely through all things, from the atmosphere to the earth beneath their feet and even through time. It was the Etheric energy, a mysterious, primal power that they had learned to command. With it, they reshaped the world.
This was the Tartarian Empire, an unparalleled beacon of knowledge, power, and innovation. Where history remembers little more than whispers of their existence, the true Tartaria was the zenith of human achievement—a civilization that had unlocked the secrets of existence itself. Etheric Power shaped everything in their world, from the foundations of their great cities to the most delicate of their creations. It fueled their machines and homes and their way of life.
In the streets of Tartaria’s capital cities, Etheric constructs—automatons made of strange alloys—patrolled, maintained, and governed. These were not crude, clanking machines. They moved with a grace that rivaled humans, powered by currents of Etheric energy that hummed through their cores. Entire cities shimmered with life, illuminated by light that seemed to come from nowhere—no visible source, just the hum of the invisible Etheric grids that ran beneath the earth and high above in the skies. And time itself? The greatest scholars of Tartaria had learned to bend it, to manipulate space and the passage of moments, allowing them to experiment on the boundaries of reality. There were whispers that some could even step through folds in time, touching the past and glimpsing the future.
This was not a world of fantasy but absolute, unshakable knowledge, where the once-unimaginable had become commonplace. Tartaria was a civilization of progress without limits, a society that had conquered the material world and begun to transcend it. Their greatest architects designed cities that defied logic—massive floating towers, hovering citadels that drifted in the air on invisible Etheric currents. Roads and smooth, gleaming stone pathways wound through the skies, connecting islands of human achievement that seemed to mock the laws of gravity. The very fabric of their existence was a seamless blend of the natural and the impossible.
At the heart of this civilization was a utopia—a place where knowledge, progress, and the betterment of humanity were paramount. The people of Tartaria lived in a harmony that modern minds would struggle to comprehend. There was no want, disease, or suffering in our understanding. Etheric Power had solved many of the problems that had plagued mankind for millennia. Their technology had eradicated famine by instantly turning barren wastelands into lush farmland. Their medical devices, powered by Etheric currents, healed the sick and even regrow limbs. Their knowledge of the stars and the cosmos had expanded beyond the limits of Earth, allowing them to communicate and travel in ways that we can scarcely imagine. In every way, the Tartarian Empire was the pinnacle of human potential.
But as with all great powers, the desire for more crept into the hearts of Tartaria’s greatest minds. They were not content simply maintaining their mastery over Etheric Power. They began to look beyond the boundaries of what was known, delving deeper into the mysteries of the universe. Their experiments grew more ambitious, their inventions more daring. The Etheric energy flowing through their world was no longer enough—they sought to command the forces of time, space, and matter. The brightest minds of Tartaria were no longer satisfied with controlling the world around them; they wanted to control the very nature of reality.
Etheric Laboratories, once focused on the betterment of humanity, began to explore darker, more dangerous avenues of research. The manipulation of time was no longer just an academic curiosity but a pursuit of the most radical kind. Scholars in hidden chambers worked to unravel the delicate balance of the universe, convinced that they could fold time, reverse it, or extend it indefinitely. Others, obsessed with manipulating matter, sought to reshape the world’s natural elements with the flick of a wrist. Some whispered of Tartarian scientists who could bring the stone to life or render the most solid structures intangible.
The Etheric constructs that once served as mere tools of convenience began to take on new, more sinister forms. Some were designed not just to perform simple tasks but to serve as instruments of war—machines capable of turning the full force of Etheric energy upon those who dared to oppose Tartaria. Massive automaton armies were built, equipped with weaponry that could rip apart reality itself, and while the empire had known peace for centuries, there were rumblings of unrest as Tartaria’s expansionist desires grew.
For the common people, life remained utopian, but in the higher echelons of society, a different vision began to take root. The scholars, scientists, and rulers of Tartaria believed that their empire was destined to control the Earth and transcend it entirely. They had mastered the forces of nature and sought to master the forces of existence itself. Tartaria would not simply be the greatest civilization on Earth; it would become the greatest civilization in the universe, a power that stretched across all of time and space.
This thirst for godlike power was not met without consequence. Deep within the earth, in secret laboratories and temples, experiments grew ever more reckless. Etheric energy was not a simple, controllable force—it was a primal, dangerous power that responded unpredictably when pushed to its limits. The scholars of Tartaria, in their hubris, believed that they could contain it and manipulate it to their will. But something began to go wrong in their empire's deepest, darkest places.
At first, the signs were subtle—small distortions in the flow of Etheric energy, odd occurrences where time seemed to jump or loop without explanation. A scholar might return to his laboratory to find that hours had passed when he had only stepped outside for a moment or that an entire day had disappeared without warning. In the skies above Tartaria, strange storms began to form—whorls of blue and purple lightning crackling across the horizon, instantly flashing in and out of existence. Buildings that had stood for centuries would suddenly crumble, only to reassemble themselves moments later, untouched.
And then, the tremors began.
At first, they were mild—a slight shaking of the earth beneath their feet, easily dismissed as a natural occurrence. But as the days went on, the tremors grew more frequent. The ground beneath the cities of Tartaria began to shift unnaturally as though some great, ancient force had been disturbed. The scholars of Tartaria, confident in their control over the world, pressed on with their experiments, dismissing these warnings as mere anomalies, nothing more than minor setbacks on their path to greater power.
But they were wrong.
Beneath Tartaria, the Etheric energy that they had harnessed for so long began to surge as though the very fabric of the earth was rebelling against their control. And then, without warning, the cataclysm began. Deep within the earth, a tide of thick, impenetrable mud—charged with Etheric energy—began to rise. This was no ordinary flood. It did not flow like water but surged upward, slow and relentless, as though gravity had been reversed. This mud, infused with the same energy that had once powered the empire, defied the natural laws of physics. It flowed through the streets of Tartaria’s great cities, engulfing everything in its path.
Unlike a natural disaster, this flood did not destroy—it entombed. The mud swallowed buildings, streets, monuments, and people, which hardened into a substance stronger than any stone. Entire cities were buried over a few weeks, their great spires and towers left sticking out of the earth like the bones of some long-dead beast. The once-great cities of Tartaria became little more than tombs, their knowledge, technology, and people buried beneath waves of Etheric-charged mud.
The few who survived fled to the highest towers, clinging to the remnants of their once-great empire. But it was too late. The age of Tartaria had come to an end. The empire that had sought to conquer time and space was now a shadow of its former self, buried beneath the very forces it had tried to command. The Etheric energy that had powered their greatest achievements now lay dormant, sealed beneath the earth, waiting for the day when it would be uncovered again.
But the legacy of Tartaria did not end there. While the empire was buried, its influence would continue to echo through the centuries. The Etheric energy that had once powered their cities still lingered, seeping into the ground, the air, and the few remaining structures. And as the world moved on, those who would one day discover the remnants of Tartaria’s power would face the same choice: to use it wisely or suffer the same fate as the empire that had tried to master the forces of the universe.
Tartaria’s downfall was not just a lesson in hubris but a warning that would echo through the ages. A warning that the forces of nature, once disturbed, could never truly be controlled.
The Great Catastrophe, known now as the Mud Flood, marked the end of Tartaria's reign over the Earth, a profound collapse that its echoes still ripple through history—if only in whispers and forgotten legends. In the mid-1500s, Tartaria’s ambition knew no bounds. Their mastery over the Etheric forces had led them to the brink of godhood, tampering with the very foundations of time, space, and matter. The warnings were initially subtle—small distortions, odd tremors, anomalies in time and space—but they were ignored. The Tartarians, in their arrogance, believed themselves above the natural world. They had harnessed Etheric energy to such an extent that they felt untouchable. But the balance of nature could only be toyed with for so long before it pushed back.
The first signs of the coming disaster were the tremors. The ground beneath Tartaria’s sprawling, normally solid, dependable cities began shifting strangely and unpredictably. At first, these tremors were minor, the kind one might dismiss as the idle settling of the earth. But they grew more frequent, more intense. It was as if the planet had become restless, stirred by the Tartarians' reckless manipulation of the forces that held reality together. Still, the empire pressed on, refusing to halt their experiments, driven by an insatiable thirst for even greater control over Etheric power.
The final stroke came suddenly. Deep beneath the heart of the empire, in the Tartarians’ most secret laboratories, where the greatest minds of the age toiled in the shadows, the ground ruptured. From these hidden depths, a slow-moving, thick substance began to rise—Etheric-charged mud infused with the raw, untamed energy they had sought to bend to their will. This was no ordinary mud. It was dense and heavy, flowing not like water but with the weight of something primordial and ancient. It moved as if it had its own will, creeping upward from the earth as though gravity had been reversed. This was not a natural disaster. It was a force of nature, powered by the energies the Tartarians had tried to command, and it came with a terrifying purpose: to reclaim the world from those who had dared to manipulate its core.
The mud surged slowly at first, giving a false sense of hope. Perhaps, some thought, it would stop. Perhaps the disaster could still be contained. But over days and weeks, it continued to rise, inexorably swallowing everything in its path. Once gleaming with Etheric power, the grandest of Tartaria's cities began to succumb to the relentless tide. As the mud engulfed them, streets that had thrummed with life, commerce, and culture were transformed into silent and lifeless tombs. Entire districts disappeared beneath the thick waves, and their inhabitants could not escape. The lower levels of the cities, where the common folk lived and worked, were buried first, the mud flowing into homes, workshops, and marketplaces, entombing them in a matter of hours.
Tartaria’s temples, monuments, and libraries—the heart of their cultural and scientific achievements—were next. These places once housed the greatest knowledge of the empire, as well as texts and devices that could unlock the mysteries of the universe. Now, they were buried under the weight of the Etheric-charged mud, sealed away from the world. The Tartarians had been so sure of their control over the forces they had harnessed, but now those same forces were burying their greatest accomplishments as if to erase them from history. The Etheric energy within the mud crackled and surged, creating a hum that resonated through the ground, a final lament for the empire that had overreached.
And yet, the mud did not simply destroy—it preserved. Infused with Etheric power, the mud calcified as it cooled and hardened, transforming into something far more than simple sediment. It became an impenetrable shell, stronger than stone, protecting the relics, buildings, and even bodies of Tartaria beneath its surface. Once vibrant and alive, the cities were frozen in time, locked beneath the hardened mud like insects trapped in amber. The uppermost towers and spires of Tartaria’s cities—those that had reached for the heavens—were left exposed, standing above the waves of mud as though in mockery of the civilization’s arrogance. These skeletal remains, half-submerged and half-visible, were eerie reminders of the grandeur that once was.
The Great Catastrophe was not just a natural disaster but a catastrophic failure of hubris, resulting from humanity’s attempt to control forces beyond its understanding. In their arrogance, the Tartarians had sought to bend Etheric energy—the fundamental power of the universe itself—to their will and, in doing so, had unleashed nature’s wrath. The mud, infused with the energy they had manipulated, had come to reclaim what was always beyond their control.
For those who survived the catastrophe, there was little hope. The survivors—nobles, scholars, and engineers who had managed to escape to the highest reaches of the cities—looked down in horror as the world they had built was consumed. They stood on the last remaining towers and citadels, watching helplessly as the mud swallowed the streets below. There was no stopping it. They could only wait and hope the flood would not rise any further.
When the mud finally stopped its relentless climb, it left behind a world transformed. What was once a vibrant, thriving empire had become a wasteland of mud-encased cities, their lower levels completely sealed beneath the surface. The grand structures of Tartaria that had once soared into the skies now stood half-buried, the tops of their towers and domes protruding from the hardened mud like the bones of a giant beast. It was as if the earth had chosen to preserve these testaments to Tartaria’s greatness, a warning to any who might come after them.
But the preservation of Tartaria beneath the mud was not just a matter of chance—the Etheric energy within the mud had calcified it, turning it into a substance stronger than any natural rock. The knowledge, technology, and relics of Tartaria were sealed away and perfectly preserved in the tomb of the mud. Time itself seemed to stand still beneath the surface. The devices that had once powered the empire, the scrolls and books that had recorded their knowledge, and the bodies of those who had perished were frozen at the moment of their burial. It was as though the catastrophe had buried Tartaria and locked it away in a bubble of suspended time, protected by the forces that had caused its downfall.
As the centuries passed, the world above moved on, unaware of the lost civilization beneath them. The mud now hardened into an impenetrable crust, became part of the landscape. New nations rose, new cities were built, and history slowly forgot about Tartaria. The once-great empire became a myth, a legend whispered among scholars and storytellers. The few spires and towers that still jutted above the mud were seen as curiosities, relics of a bygone age, but few knew the truth of what lay beneath them. The Etheric energy that had once powered the empire still lingered in the air, creating strange anomalies and disturbances in the regions where Tartaria’s cities had been buried. Still, these were dismissed as natural phenomena by the people of the new world.
Yet, beneath the hardened surface, Tartaria’s legacy remained intact, waiting to be uncovered. The knowledge and technology of the empire, sealed away in the depths, had the potential to change the world once more—if only someone could find a way to reach it. But the Etheric energy that had preserved Tartaria’s secrets also guarded them fiercely. The mud that encased the cities was nearly impossible to penetrate, and those who tried often met with failure, their tools breaking against the impenetrable crust. Even modern technology, with all its advancements, could not crack the surface of the mud hardened by Etheric power.
For centuries, the world above remained ignorant of the treasures beneath. But some remembered. In secret, a few select groups began to search for the lost cities of Tartaria, hoping to unlock the knowledge and power that had been sealed away. These explorers, driven by greed, curiosity, or the desire for power, ventured into the regions where the remnants of Tartaria still stood. They knew that if they could recover the Etheric technology buried beneath the mud, they could reshape the world in their image.
But the Mud Flood had left more than just physical remnants behind. The Etheric energy that had caused the catastrophe still pulsed through the ground, creating strange phenomena in the regions where Tartaria’s cities were buried. Time seemed to stretch and bend in these places, with explorers reporting strange occurrences—hours passing in minutes or days lost in what felt like the blink of an eye. Spatial anomalies, where distances seemed to warp and shift, made navigation difficult. And in the depths of the mud, where the energy was strongest, Etheric storms raged, cracking the sky with blue and purple lightning flashes.
The mud itself, though hardened, was not lifeless. In some places, it seemed to pulse with a faint glow, as though the Etheric energy trapped within it was still alive, waiting to be unleashed. Those who ventured too deep into the mud reported seeing strange figures—Mud Golems, creatures made of the very substance that had buried Tartaria. These golems were said to rise from the ground without warning, their bodies crackling with Etheric energy as if they were the guardians of the lost cities, protecting the secrets beneath the surface.
The world was forever changed when the Great Mud Flood swallowed Tartaria’s glorious cities beneath waves of Etheric-charged earth. The once-mighty Tartarian Empire, whose shimmering towers had reached the heavens and whose citizens had lived in a utopia built on Etheric Power, was no more. A new age dawned, but it was an age of forgetting, of ignorance, where the true history of Tartaria faded into myth and legend.
Over the centuries that followed, civilizations rose and fell on the bones of the fallen empire. The great ruins of Tartaria, once visible on the landscape, slowly disappeared beneath overgrowth, shifting sands, and the erosion of time. Spires and domes once punctuated the skyline, crumbled further, leaving only faint traces of their former glory. Entire cities entombed beneath the impenetrable crust of hardened mud became forgotten entirely. What little remained of Tartaria’s surface-level structures—crumbled towers or exposed rooftops of submerged buildings—were regarded as curiosities, sources of local superstition, or at most, subjects for speculative historians and conspiracy theorists.
Etheric storms, once associated with Tartarian technology, became scattered worldwide. These storms crackled with blue and violet lightning, bursts of energy that warped reality in subtle but terrifying ways. Time seemed to stretch and contract near the heart of these storms, bending the laws of physics and leaving travelers and explorers disoriented, sometimes vanishing for hours or days, only to reappear with no memory of what had transpired. These occurrences were chalked up to strange natural phenomena by the scholars of the new world—anomalies of the atmosphere, they claimed, dismissing the deeper truths. But those in the know understood these Etheric disturbances for what they were: remnants of a lost civilization, traces of the immense power that had once coursed through Tartaria's heart.
Tartaria, though forgotten by most, was never fully erased. Some remembered—those who had survived the catastrophe or descended from the great fall survivors. These groups remained in the shadows, quietly working to preserve what little they had managed to save of Tartaria’s vast and advanced technologies. Among them, none were more powerful, secretive, or dangerous than the Mud Monarchs.
The Mud Monarchs: Masters of the Shadows
The Mud Monarchs are the descendants of Tartaria’s nobility and ruling class. Unlike the common people who perished in the Mud Flood, these elites had managed to escape to the highest reaches of the cities—isolated fortresses and citadels that remained above the rising mud. In these towers, they watched as their empire was swallowed, but they did not forget. Over the centuries, they consolidated what little Etheric technology they still had access to and used it to influence the rebuilding world quietly.
Operating from the shadows, the Mud Monarchs became masters of subtlety, manipulation, and control. They moved like ghosts, weaving into the fabric of emerging societies, slowly bending the world to their will without revealing their true power. They understood the danger of letting the common people rediscover the secrets of Etheric Power. If the masses ever learned what lay buried beneath their feet, the balance of power would shift, and the fragile web of control the Mud Monarchs had built would come crashing down. To prevent this, they suppressed any knowledge of Tartaria’s true history. Historical texts were altered, evidence of the old empire erased, and anyone who stumbled too close to the truth was quietly silenced. The Mud Monarchs became the hidden rulers of the world, guiding its fate from the darkness while maintaining the illusion of autonomy among the people.
Though their rule was secretive, it was effective. They used the ancient Etheric devices still at their disposal to solidify their power, subtly influencing politics, trade, and even the development of technology. They had no desire to restore Tartaria—they understood that the knowledge their ancestors had sought was too dangerous to wield without consequence. Instead, they wished only to preserve their rule and prevent the mistakes of the past from being repeated. But the Etheric technology they relied on was failing. With each century, the Mud Monarchs found it harder to maintain the ancient machines. The knowledge to repair or create new devices was lost, and those who still understood the inner workings of Etheric Power were dwindling in number. The Mud Monarchs were desperate, clutching onto the crumbling remnants of their power, knowing that their reign could not last forever unless they found a way to recover the lost knowledge buried deep beneath the mud.
The True Tartarians: Beneath the Surface
While the Mud Monarchs controlled the surface world, another group quietly rose beneath the earth—the True Tartarians, descendants of those trapped below the mud during the Great Catastrophe. While their once-grand cities had been buried under the weight of Etheric-charged earth, not all was lost. These survivors, initially believed to have perished, had managed to carve out a new existence in the depths of Tartaria’s entombed world. Forced to survive in the dark, they developed a unique and powerful technology known as Aethercraft.
Aethercraft was born from necessity. The True Tartarians, surrounded by Etheric-infused stone and mud, quickly learned to harness the energy still trapped within the earth. Over generations, they mastered manipulating the Etheric-charged environment around them, reshaping stone and metal with their minds, and constructing new cities in the ruins of the old. Where once they had lived in open, sunlit metropolises, they now thrived in subterranean worlds of gleaming Etheric stone powered by the force that had destroyed their civilization.
But the True Tartarians did not forget their past. They knew the surface world had moved on, ignorant of the existence of their subterranean empire. They also knew that the Mud Monarchs, those who had escaped to the highest towers, still existed above them, hoarding the last scraps of Tartaria’s ancient technology. The True Tartarians had remained hidden for centuries, building their strength and perfecting their craft, waiting for the right moment to reclaim the surface and restore Tartaria’s lost glory.
The cities of the True Tartarians were wonders in their own right—hollowed caverns of Etheric-infused stone, filled with mechanical marvels and defenses unlike anything the surface world could imagine. Etheric golems roamed their streets, massive constructs of metal and stone that responded to the will of the Aethercrafters who commanded them. Their technology, though different from the Etheric devices once used by their ancestors, was no less powerful. They had learned to manipulate raw Etheric energy in ways the Mud Monarchs could not comprehend, allowing them to shape reality within the bounds of their subterranean world.
Yet, for all their power, the True Tartarians had a single goal—to reclaim the surface. They believed it was their birthright to rule, to restore the empire that had once been the pinnacle of human achievement. But they were patient. They knew that if they revealed themselves too soon, the Mud Monarchs would move against them, using what little remained of their surface influence to crush any rebellion. And so, they waited, biding their time, gathering their strength.
The Etheric Divide: Tensions Mount
As the centuries passed, the world above and below drifted further apart. The surface civilizations, ruled secretly by the Mud Monarchs, grew and evolved, unaware of the vast empire hidden beneath them. Meanwhile, the True Tartarians perfected their craft, refining their control over Etheric energy and preparing for the day they would rise. But tensions were building. The Mud Monarchs, whose power was waning, were beginning to suspect that something stirred beneath the surface. Their ancient machines, though unreliable, still picked up faint signals—disturbances in the Etheric field that they could not explain.
The Etheric storms that had plagued the world since the fall of Tartaria were growing stronger and more frequent, especially in regions where the buried cities lay closest to the surface. These storms disrupted communications, scrambled technology, and occasionally revealed glimpses of the past—flickers of the old empire trapped in the Etheric charge. Scholars and adventurers who ventured too close to these disturbances often found themselves pulled into the depths of history, experiencing brief flashes of Tartaria as it once was, only to be hurled back into the present, disoriented and changed.
The world was on the brink of rediscovery. The fragile balance of power maintained by the Mud Monarchs was teetering, and the True Tartarians were growing restless beneath the surface. The technologies of both groups—one trying desperately to hold onto the past, the other seeking to reclaim it—were edging closer to a collision, and the surface world would soon be caught in the middle of a conflict it could not possibly understand.
A World on the Edge: The Factions of 2148
It is 2148, and the Earth is teetering on the brink of an unprecedented rediscovery. Few realize the magnitude of what lies beneath their feet, hidden beneath layers of Etheric-charged mud—a civilization so advanced that its mastery of energy and matter defied the natural laws of physics. Yet, for the average person walking the streets of this rebuilt world, Tartaria is a name long forgotten, a ghost of history buried in the collective memory of humanity. The technologies that power the modern world, from electricity grids to transportation systems, are mere shadows of the immense power the Tartarian Empire once wielded. The relics that occasionally resurface—rusted machines, strange artifacts, and unexplained anomalies—are dismissed by scholars as ancient curiosities, their true significance obscured by ignorance. But while the world remains blissfully unaware of its past, competing factions in the shadows know all too well what is at stake. The world is on the verge of rediscovering its lost legacy, and with it, the fate of humanity itself may be reshaped.
Beneath the surface, ancient power struggles are beginning to bubble to the fore, involving factions that have long waited for this moment. These factions, steeped in the secret knowledge of Tartaria, are vying for control of the ruins and the immense Etheric Power that lies entombed within them. This is no longer a battle for mere survival—a battle for the world's future, a war between those who would reignite the flames of Tartaria's technological brilliance and those who would bury its legacy forever.
The Mud Monarchs: The Dying Rulers of a Hidden World
For centuries, the Mud Monarchs have operated from the shadows, their bloodlines tracing directly back to the elite families of Tartaria who escaped the Great Mud Flood. These descendants of the old world watched as their empire crumbled beneath waves of Etheric-charged mud, but they managed to preserve what they could—fragments of the empire’s technology, knowledge, and power. The Mud Monarchs have maintained a secret grip on global power from their isolated fortresses, deep within the remnants of the world’s highest cities.
But the days of their dominion are numbered. The once-great Etheric technology they so carefully guarded is now failing. The devices that once fueled their influence are becoming unstable, and with each passing generation, their understanding of how to operate or repair these ancient machines diminishes. What was once the source of their power has become a fragile crutch. They are like kings sitting atop a crumbling throne, desperate to hold onto their power but fully aware that they are running out of time.
The Mud Monarchs aim to prevent the world from rediscovering Tartaria’s legacy. They know that if the knowledge of Etheric Power is ever fully uncovered, the masses would rise, and their tenuous grip on the world would collapse. They have manipulated history, erased evidence, and orchestrated global events to suppress any knowledge of Tartaria’s actual past. But now, cracks are forming in their carefully constructed web of control. Rumors of Tartaria's lost technologies are surfacing more frequently, and Etheric anomalies are growing harder to conceal. Entire regions are being affected by unexplained disturbances—time distortions, spatial warps, and storms of Etheric energy. The Mud Monarchs’ greatest fear is that the world is getting too close to the truth.
As their desperation grows, they have resorted to extreme measures. Secret operatives, spies, and assassins are dispatched to silence those who come too close to rediscovering Tartaria’s buried secrets. But for all their efforts, they are fighting a losing battle. The technology they once controlled is slipping through their fingers, and they know that soon, the world will no longer be theirs to manipulate. The Mud Monarchs are standing on the edge of a precipice and willing to do whatever it takes to prevent the rebirth of Tartaria—even if that means plunging the world into chaos.
The Forgotten Order: Rediscovery of a New Age
Opposing the Mud Monarchs is a faction as secretive as it is determined: the Forgotten Order. Comprised of scholars, engineers, and archaeologists, the Forgotten Order has made it their mission to reclaim Tartaria’s lost knowledge for the good of humanity. They see the Etheric energy buried beneath the hardened layers of mud not as a dangerous force to be suppressed but as the key to unlocking humanity’s full potential. In their view, the Mud Monarchs are nothing more than the last remnants of a dying aristocracy, hoarding the knowledge of a bygone age out of fear.
For centuries, the Forgotten Order has operated in the shadows, conducting clandestine expeditions into the buried ruins of Tartaria. They are explorers and scientists, driven not by greed or power but by a deep curiosity and a belief that Tartaria’s knowledge can be used to build a better world. The Forgotten Order is not without its dangers, though—many of their expeditions into the depths of Tartaria have ended in disaster. The Etheric energy that lies dormant beneath the mud is volatile and unpredictable. Strange, ancient defenses still guard the old cities, and many who have ventured into the ruins have never returned.
Despite these risks, the Forgotten Order presses on, driven by the hope that they can recover the ancient technologies that once powered an empire. They believe that with the right tools, they can harness Etheric Power to solve the energy crises of the modern world, eliminate poverty, and restore balance to a planet on the brink of environmental collapse. But they are not naïve. The Etheric anomalies that have begun to manifest more frequently are signs that time is running out. The world is waking up to the buried power beneath its feet, and the Forgotten Order knows they must act before others with darker intentions seize control.
While they oppose the Mud Monarchs, the Forgotten Order is not free from internal conflict. Different sects within the Order have different visions for how Tartaria’s knowledge should be used. Some believe in releasing Etheric technology to the public, democratizing its power for the benefit of all. Others argue that this would lead to the same hubris that brought about Tartaria’s downfall. They advocate for a more controlled approach, carefully selecting which technologies should be released and ensuring that Etheric Power is never again used for war. The tension within the Order is growing, and with the world on the verge of rediscovery, their unity will soon be tested.
The Eternal Dynasty: The Bloodline of the Empire
While the Mud Monarchs and the Forgotten Order battle for control over Tartaria’s legacy, another faction believes it has a divine right to rule: the Eternal Dynasty. Descendants of Tartaria’s royal bloodlines, the Eternal Dynasty sees itself not as a relic of the past but as the rightful rulers of the future. To them, the fall of Tartaria was not a failure but a test—a cleansing of the weak so that only the strongest might inherit the earth.
The Dynasty’s power lies in the Aetherborn, individuals born with Etheric-infused bloodlines. These individuals are rare and possess unique abilities that stem from the ancient Etheric experiments conducted by Tartaria’s scientists. Some can easily manipulate Etheric energy, bending reality in small but profound ways—healing wounds, teleporting short distances, or even slowing time around them for brief moments. To the Eternal Dynasty, the Aetherborn are the key to unlocking the full potential of Tartaria’s lost power.
The Dynasty seeks to restore the empire to its former glory, believing that only those with the royal bloodlines of Tartaria are fit to wield the immense power that lies beneath the surface. They view the Mud Monarchs and the Forgotten Order as pretenders—one group clinging to power they do not deserve, the other squandering it on naïve dreams of a better world. The Eternal Dynasty is preparing for the moment the Aetherborn will rise and reclaim their birthright. They have been quietly breeding and training Aetherborn for generations, waiting for the right time to unleash their power upon the world.
However, the Dynasty’s view of the future is not peaceful. They believe that only through domination can the world be remade. Their vision is one of imperial rule, where the familiar people serve under the watchful eye of the Aetherborn, guided by the ancient wisdom of the Tartarian bloodlines. The Dynasty has little interest in collaboration or negotiation. They are waiting for the moment to strike, to tear down the fragile structures of the modern world and build a new empire atop the ruins of the old.
The Reclaimers Guild: Opportunists and Relic Hunters
Lurking at the fringes of this great power struggle is the Reclaimers Guild, a loose coalition of treasure hunters, mercenaries, and opportunists who see Tartaria not as a legacy to be preserved or a power to be controlled but as a goldmine. For the Reclaimers, the buried cities of Tartaria represent wealth and power beyond imagination. They care little for the moral implications of uncovering ancient technologies. What they want is simple: profit.
The Guild operates in the shadows, taking on contracts from anyone willing to pay for Tartarian relics. They are scavengers, diving into the mud-encased ruins in search of anything that can be sold on the black market. Many of the world’s most powerful corporations and governments have quietly hired Reclaimers to recover ancient artifacts, knowing whoever controls these relics will hold the keys to power in the new world. But the Guild is a dangerous and unstable faction. Their greed often leads them to take risks that no other group would dare, and many Reclaimers have met their end in Tartaria’s forgotten cities.
For the Reclaimers, the race is on. They know that the world is on the edge of rediscovery, and they are determined to seize as much wealth and power as possible before the truth of Tartaria is revealed. Their agents operate globally, slipping through the cracks of civilization, always on the hunt for the next big score. But in their pursuit of treasure, they risk awakening forces they cannot control—for not all of Tartaria’s relics are dormant, and some hold secrets far more dangerous than they could ever imagine.
As 2148 progresses, the world stands on the precipice of a new era. The factions that have long waited in the shadows are preparing to move, and the buried Etheric Power of Tartaria is stirring once more. Anomalies are growing more substantial, and the ancient defenses of the empire are reawakening, guarding the secrets that could reshape the future. The modern world, blissfully unaware of the war being fought beneath its surface, is about to be pulled into a conflict that will decide the fate of humanity.
The choices made in the coming months will determine whether Tartaria’s legacy is rebirth or destruction. The Mud Monarchs, the Forgotten Order, the Eternal Dynasty, and the Reclaimers Guild play their part in a grand game of power, deception, and survival. But as the world teeters on the brink, one thing is sure: Tartaria’s secrets will not be buried for long.
The Earth itself has not remained untouched by the powerful forces that once sustained the mighty empire of Tartaria. Even though centuries have passed since the Great Mud Flood buried the Tartarian cities, the Etheric energy that powered their civilization still lingers, refusing to fade into the annals of forgotten history. Though dormant in many places, this energy continues to warp and manipulate the fabric of reality in regions where the buried ruins of Tartaria lie just beneath the surface. These zones, scattered across the globe, are marked by strange and unpredictable phenomena—Etheric anomalies—that ripple through time, space, and matter, creating landscapes where the rules of the natural world no longer seem to apply.
These anomalous zones, known to scholars and treasure hunters alike, are as mysterious as dangerous. Some appear serene on the surface, with nothing more than a faint shimmer in the air betraying the latent power below. Others are more chaotic, where the Etheric energy bubbles into the atmosphere, unleashing storms of raw energy that crackle with blue and purple lightning, distorting the landscape and twisting reality into something alien and terrifying. To the untrained eye, these anomalies may seem like simple environmental disturbances. Still, to those who understand the legacy of Tartaria, they represent both a profound danger and a tantalizing opportunity.
Temporal Loops and Spatial Distortions
One of the most common effects of Etheric anomalies is the warping of time and space. In areas where the Etheric energy is most concentrated, explorers and researchers often find themselves trapped in temporal loops, where the flow of time becomes unreliable and inconsistent. In some cases, hours stretch into what feels like days; in others, weeks pass in the blink of an eye. Explorers have reported wandering through ancient ruins only to relive the same moments repeatedly as if the fabric of time itself had been caught in an endless loop. These time traps are disorienting and terrifying, with no clear way to break free other than retreating from the Etheric zone altogether.
Spatial distortions are just as dangerous. Within these zones, distances become unreliable as space is folded or stretched. A building that seems only a few meters away may suddenly stretch into an unreachable horizon. At the same time, a short walk down a corridor can lead to an entirely different part of the ruin, bypassing entire sections of the structure. Some explorers have even reported stepping into impossible spaces, where rooms twist in on themselves, creating mazes that defy geometry, leaving them lost for days. In the most extreme cases, spatial distortions have caused individuals to disappear entirely, as though they had been pulled out of reality altogether, leaving no trace behind.
These temporal and spatial anomalies are dangerous not only because of their disorienting effects but also because they guard the ruins of Tartaria, preventing easy access to the hidden technological treasures. But the rewards are immense for those who can navigate these warped realities. Ancient machines, weapons, and Etheric cores—devices that once powered entire cities—remain buried within these zones, waiting to be uncovered by those brave enough to risk the dangers of the anomalies.
Etheric Storms: Nature Unleashed
In the most active Etheric zones, storms of pure energy rage across the landscape, disrupting both natural and artificial environments. These storms are unlike anything seen in the modern world. They are not fueled by the atmosphere or natural weather patterns but by the lingering Etheric energy that still flows beneath the earth, concentrated in the buried cities of Tartaria. Etheric storms are characterized by lightning that cracks the sky in brilliant blue, purple, and green hues, lighting the night in unnatural colors. The storms warp the ground and air, causing localized electromagnetic disruptions that destroy communication systems and turn off electronic devices. In some instances, the storms have been known to alter the very composition of matter, turning solid objects into etheric mist or causing the metal to behave like a liquid.
Explorers caught in these storms have reported hallucinations, where reality seems to shift and buckle under the pressure of the Etheric energy. Some see glimpses of Tartaria's past, ghostly figures walking through the ruins from another time, carrying out their long-forgotten tasks as if unaware of the world’s collapse. Others experience intense mental and physical strain, their minds overwhelmed by the raw energy that pulses through the storm. There are stories of entire teams of explorers disappearing without a trace, swallowed by the Etheric energy, and never seen again.
Yet despite these dangers, Etheric storms are often seen as opportunities. When these storms rage, they sometimes expose hidden entrances to buried Tartarian vaults or reveal energy signatures that point to long-lost Etheric cores, the most coveted relics of Tartaria's past. Some believe these storms are the last vestiges of the ancient defenses that once protected Tartaria, meant to keep intruders away from the empire’s secrets. But others believe the storms are a natural consequence of the Etheric energy trying to reconnect with its source, a sign that the old technology is waking up again.
The Guardians of the Ether: Automated Defenses and Dormant Constructs
The deeper explorers venture into the ruins of Tartaria, the more they encounter the ancient defenses that still guard the empire’s secrets. Once designed to protect Tartaria’s most sensitive locations, these automated systems remain active, even after centuries of dormancy. Etheric constructs, mechanical guardians powered by the energy that destroyed the empire, patrol the forgotten corridors and streets of Tartaria’s buried cities. Some are humanoid in form, resembling ancient warriors clad in gleaming Etheric armor, while others are more alien, their designs meant to intimidate and overwhelm any who dare to intrude. These constructs are not simple machines—they are sentient guardians programmed to defend Tartaria’s treasures.
One of the most feared of these ancient defenses is the Mud Golem—a massive creature said to be born of the Etheric-charged mud that swallowed Tartaria’s cities. These beings are not mere legends; they are living remnants of the empire’s collapse, shaped by the forces that destroyed it. Mud Golems rise from the hardened Etheric mud like ancient titans, their bodies crackling with the same energy that destroyed the empire. Their massive forms blend seamlessly with their surroundings, making them nearly impossible to detect until too late. They relentlessly pursue intruders, their bodies impervious to most modern weapons, and they wield Etheric power to devastating effect. Some scholars believe that these creatures were created during the final days of Tartaria as a desperate attempt to control the mud that had consumed the empire. But whatever their origin, they now serve as uncompromising guardians, patrolling the depths of Tartaria’s buried cities, ensuring that no one disturbs the ancient technology.
Other, less fearsome defenses are no less dangerous. Automated traps lie dormant in the ruins, waiting to spring into action at the slightest disturbance. Some release bursts of Etheric energy capable of disintegrating an intruder in seconds. In contrast, others manipulate the environment around them, causing walls to shift and floors to collapse, trapping explorers in inescapable death traps. These defense mechanisms are so advanced that they seem almost magical to the modern world. Still, they are nothing more than the remnants of a civilization that once mastered the art of manipulating reality.
The Promise of Tartaria’s Power: Rewards and Risks
For those who dare to explore the anomalous zones where Tartaria’s buried cities lie hidden, the risks are immense, but the rewards are even more remarkable. The Etheric cores that still pulse beneath the ground hold the key to a power unlike anything the modern world has ever seen. Once used to power entire cities, these cores can generate immense amounts of energy, far beyond what today’s technology can produce. Whoever controls these cores can reshape the world, build cities that hover in the air, manipulate time and space, and create weapons capable of devastating entire armies.
The ancient machines of Tartaria, too, hold untold potential. Some are considered terraforming devices capable of turning barren wastelands into fertile lands within moments. Others are constructs of war, designed to level cities with a single blast of Etheric energy. The relics of Tartaria are game-changers capable of altering the balance of power on a global scale. But with these treasures comes great danger. The Etheric energy fuels them is volatile, unpredictable, and often poorly understood. Unlocking these ancient devices requires technological expertise and a deep understanding of the risks involved.
For some, the lure of these treasures is too great to resist. The Mud Monarchs seek to bury Tartaria’s legacy deeper, fearing what might happen if the ordinary people were to rediscover this immense power. The Forgotten Order believes that humanity’s future lies in reclaiming Tartaria’s lost technology, convinced that it holds the key to solving the world’s most significant challenges. Meanwhile, the Reclaimers Guild and other opportunists see Tartaria’s ruins as nothing more than a source of wealth and power, caring little for the consequences of their actions.
The Path Forward: A New Age or Total Collapse?
As the world of 2148 moves closer to rediscovering the power of Tartaria, the stakes have never been higher. The anomalies are growing more robust, the defenses of the buried cities are reawakening, and the factions that have long fought in the shadows are preparing for open conflict. The choices made by those who dare to explore the ruins of Tartaria will determine the future of humanity.
Will the ancient power buried beneath the earth lead to a new golden age, where the technology of Tartaria is used to solve the world’s problems and elevate humanity to new heights? Or will the greed and ambition of those who seek to control this power bring about a second collapse, one even more devastating than the fall of Tartaria itself?
The legacy of Tartaria is one of unimaginable power but also unimaginable risk. The secrets of its buried cities may change the world forever—or destroy it entirely. The path forward is uncertain, and as the factions race to unlock the mysteries of Tartaria’s lost empire, the world's fate hangs in the balance.

The Nature of Aether: Tartaria’s Lost Elemental Power
In the once-mighty empire of Tartaria, Aether—also known as Etheric Power—was much more than a mystical concept. To Tartarian scholars, engineers, and scientists, Aether represented the raw essence of creation, an omnipresent energy field permeating both the material and cosmic realms. Advanced by the ancient Tartarians as the "quintessence" or fifth fundamental force, Aether had characteristics that bridged the physical and metaphysical: a charged, electric power, dynamic yet ever-present in the world around them. It was both particle and wave, resonating at frequencies that modern science has yet to quantify, aligning with certain theories of gravitational and electromagnetic fields but extending far beyond them in complexity.
Where Aristotle and other early thinkers across cultures viewed Aether as a hypothetical substrate for celestial bodies, Tartarian scholars demonstrated that Aether was, in fact, harnessable. They captured it in vast Etheric Grids embedded throughout Tartaria’s infrastructure, channeling the energy to fuel all aspects of life, from the cities’ grand lighting arrays to the defense mechanisms embedded within Tartaria’s towering spires. The capital, Asgardar, was home to the fabled Grand Spire of Etheria. It functioned as a monumental capacitor for Aetheric energy drawn from cosmic sources, effectively grounding celestial Aether and directing it into the city's vast grid system. Aether in Tartaria was, therefore, not only a scientific marvel but also a symbol of its near-limitless prosperity, innovation, and ambition​​.
The Etheric Field: A Living Energy Network
Central to Tartaria’s scientific doctrine was that Aether constituted a “living” energy field. Tartarian scholars posited that this force could modulate its density and vibration in response to environmental conditions, both amplifying and neutralizing physical forces, from gravitational shifts to magnetic fields. Elior Zalmar, one of Tartaria’s most revolutionary Etheric scientists, further advanced this notion through his work on Etheric frequency resonance. According to Zalmar, Aether’s structure allowed it to behave as a medium for light and sound and as an active participant in physical phenomena. His calculations suggested that Aether was capable of localized excitation—essentially vibrating at frequencies that could affect matter at an atomic level, potentially even altering time-space metrics within localized regions of high Etheric concentration.
Zalmar's theoretical framework took inspiration from early concepts of the luminiferous ether but extended them into what he called resonant Etheric fields. His Etheric models suggested that Aether particles—known as "Aetherons"—oscillated at different frequencies, allowing Tartarian technology to selectively manipulate these particles using complex oscillatory fields. By adjusting the vibrational alignment of Aetherons, Tartarian devices could theoretically produce massive bursts of energy without fuel depletion. This principle of dynamic resonance modulation was core to his experiments, especially those conducted at Thametan’s Tower in Samarran, which housed a colossal device known as the Etheric Engine. This engine, Zalmar claimed, could exploit the naturally oscillating fields of Aether to produce self-sustaining energy cycles—potentially rendering Tartaria’s entire empire effectively energy-independent​​.
The Great Mud Flood: Aetheric Overload and Catastrophe
However, in a bid to expand Aether’s potential beyond the constraints of existing Etheric Grids, Zalmar’s experiments with the Etheric Engine led to an uncontrollable resonance cascade—a phenomenon Tartarian records describe as an Aetheric Overload. In lay terms, the Etheric Engine inadvertently “overclocked” the field density of Aether within the material plane, drawing excessive energy from both terrestrial and cosmic sources. This energy surge destabilized the atmospheric balance of Tartaria and released a catastrophic pulse that interacted unpredictably with the geological substratum beneath Tartaria. The outcome was the Great Mud Flood, a geological cataclysm marked by the sudden uprising of Ether-charged mud, a dense, electrically reactive sludge that spread across Tartaria in waves.
This event buried Tartaria under layers of mud infused with Etheric fields that continue to emit charged particles and energy pulses. The mud now hardened to a state resembling Aetherstone, forms an impenetrable barrier that distorts electromagnetic fields and scrambles modern excavation technology. Scholars have likened the phenomenon to a constant-state Aetheric “field lock,” where the Etheric charge within the mud acts almost like an unbreakable atomic bond, rendering Tartarian sites beneath it impervious to standard tools and creating the Etheric anomalies that plague explorers to this day​​.
The Rise of the Mud Monarchs: Custodians of Suppression
After the Great Mud Flood, Tartaria was effectively fractured. Among the survivors were members of Tartaria's former nobility and elite engineers, who took control of the remnants of functioning Etheric technology. This faction, known as the Mud Monarchs, realized the strategic potential of controlling access to Aetheric relics and knowledge. Establishing their rule from surviving cities like Asgardar and Nimari, the Mud Monarchs declared Aetheric research forbidden, enforcing their control by hunting down and eliminating any who dared pursue Etheric studies.
The Mud Monarchs then redefined Aether as a myth, warning of the disastrous consequences of its misuse and spinning it as a dangerous relic of Tartaria's misguided ambition. All official records of Elior Zalmar’s Etheric work and the Etheric Engine at Thametan’s Tower were sealed away, and those who questioned this historical revisionism often vanished without a trace. As the Mud Monarchs’ rule solidified, Aetheric Power became a tightly controlled secret, known only to a select few engineers under their command. The legacy of Tartaria thus fell under layers of mud and misinformation, an empire buried not only in Etheric stone but also in forgotten memory​​.
The Forgotten Order: Seekers of Etheric Knowledge
In direct opposition to the Mud Monarchs, a covert faction called the Forgotten Order emerged, consisting of Tartarian engineers, scientists, and descendants of scholars who survived the Mud Flood. These individuals were convinced that Aether was the key to humanity’s rebirth, and they dedicated themselves to uncovering Tartaria’s lost Etheric technology. They believed that Aether could be safely and effectively controlled with the right knowledge—a view that drove them to defy the Mud Monarchs and reclaim Tartaria’s secrets for all benefit.
Operating out of hidden enclaves such as Varakush, a stronghold located on the edge of the Great Tartary Plains, the Forgotten Order scours buried Tartarian sites for Etheric relics and strives to understand the phenomena behind the Great Mud Flood. Their greatest targets include Tartaria's Lost Capitals like Drakova, where an ancient Etheric Core is rumored to remain, and the Red Tower of Nimari, one of the last structures believed to house functional Etheric technology. But their mission is fraught with danger, as the Mud Monarchs, ever watchful, use spies and assassins to maintain their grip on Etheric knowledge​​.
Etheric Anomalies and Aetherborn
Today, the Great Tartary Plains—ground zero for the Etheric cataclysm—remain a landscape where Aetheric phenomena still manifest. Field measurements show unpredictable fluctuations in geomagnetic forces, and travelers frequently report sensory distortions, such as disorientation, hallucinations, and time dilation. Etheric “storms” discharge across the plains, producing violet and blue “Aether lightning” arcs that appear to carry electrical and gravitational properties. Some Tartarian theorists propose that these storms are remnants of the Etheric Grid reactivating spontaneously, the echoes of Tartaria's resonant fields still rippling through the land.
Interestingly, a small group known as Aetherborn—individuals carrying the latent bloodlines of ancient Tartarian families—appear to exhibit a unique biological sensitivity to Etheric energy. Studies suggest that Aetherborn can detect variations in the ambient Etheric field and may display temporary abilities, such as heightened reflexes or limited energy manipulation, when near Aetherstone or other Etheric relics. The Mud Monarchs keep a close eye on these individuals, fearing their potential as threats and assets, while the Forgotten Order seeks to recruit them, viewing their abilities as a bridge to the past​​.
Aether’s Modern Mystery and Legacy
Aether, then, is more than a mere historical curiosity—it remains an active, enigmatic force in the world of Tartaria Prima. It is a power source with the potential to transform reality, a cosmic energy that, if properly harnessed, could alter the balance of power worldwide. Yet, the journey to unlock Tartaria’s Etheric secrets is not for the faint-hearted. The Mud Monarchs guard these secrets ruthlessly, while Etheric relics are shielded by ancient Tartarian defenses, Etheric traps, and the strange creatures that roam the ruins.
For players, Aether represents a doorway to adventure, danger, and possibly enlightenment. Each discovery, each relic, and each encounter with the Etheric anomalies in Tartaria brings them closer to the power that built and destroyed an empire. Aether is both the lure and the warning, a force whose legacy continues to shape the fate of all who dare to uncover its mysteries.
Aetherkin
The Aetherkin are the reanimated remains of those who perished during the Great Mud Flood. These unfortunate souls, unable to reach the tallest structures before the mud engulfed the land, were buried within the Aetherstone sludge, with only moments to realize their fate before their lives were cut short. As the mud solidified, it created an almost sacred seal around their bodies, bathing them in an unbroken field of Etheric energy. This immersion granted them an otherworldly peace, transcending time and linking their consciousness to the Aetheric flow that Tartarian scholars had only begun to understand.
To the Aetherkin, death was a release—a chance to become one with the Aetheric field in a realm of profound serenity. In this connected ethereal plane, their consciousnesses melded into an endless network of energy, extending beyond the limits of space and time. Within this realm, they knew no suffering, existing as part of a vast, calming energy that brought a wholeness far greater than any experience they’d had in life. They have been sealed in this peace for centuries, no longer bound to Tartaria’s struggles, wars, or conflicts.
However, this peace is fragile. When the Aetherstone mud surrounding their bodies is disturbed, Aetherkin is jolted back to consciousness, violently torn from their eternal rest, and returned to the mortal plane. Infused with the latent Etheric energy that permeated the mud, they awaken with formidable strength and resilience, capable of pushing through up to five feet of mud to break free. When they emerge, they are hostile to any living beings nearby, perceiving them as threats to their “sleep” and the Aetheric rest they so deeply crave.
Abilities and Behavior
The Aetherkin are not mindless undead but are driven by a singular desire to return to their rest. Active people exhibit heightened physical abilities and a strong Etheric aura, making them formidable adversaries. In combat, they strike with the urgency of someone desperate to return to the peace they were torn from, and they often manifest residual Etheric powers. These can include minor disruptive auras that interfere with nearby technology, spells, or etheric bursts that pulse outward in flashes of blue and violet light.
Unique Traits:
Reanimated by Disturbance: An Aetherkin only awakens if the Aetherstone mud encasing them is disturbed, usually by excavation efforts, relic hunters, or unlucky travelers.
Etheric Resilience: Infused with Etheric energy, they are resilient to certain magical and physical attacks, with an inherent resistance to elemental or arcane effects. 
Ethereal Calling: As a reminder of the Aetheric peace they experienced in “death,” Aetherkin can summon brief flashes of that realm, creating disorienting visions or projecting an aura of dread in their vicinity. This effect can intimidate or disorient foes, as nearby creatures feel a haunting pull toward the same peace the Aetherkin longs for.
Relentless Peacekeepers: Their goal is not domination but peace, and if left unprovoked, they might retreat back into the mud to seek their rest. However, their rage and desperation intensify if their rest is disturbed again.
Legends and Attitudes
The Aetherkin are both feared and revered by those who know their existence. Some Tartarian Revivalists consider them tragic guardians of Tartaria’s secrets, while others, like the Mud Monarchs, view them as cursed remnants of the past, best left undisturbed. In the Lost Capitals and the depths of the Mud Seas, relic hunters and explorers tell tales of encountering these beings, describing the hollow, peaceful expressions on their faces when they break free—faces that only become twisted with despair as they realize they have been dragged from their peace.
To some Tartarian scholars, the Aetherkin were, in a sense, the “lucky” ones. Their fate, though grim, granted them a fleeting oneness with the Aetheric field, an understanding of Tartaria’s lost power that none among the living can achieve. Yet, it is a rest that, once disturbed, can never be fully restored, and the Aetherkin live as relics of a forgotten disaster, doomed to protect the peace they lost.
The Entombed
The Entombed are the honored dead of Tartaria, those who perished sealed within their homes during the catastrophic Great Mud Flood. When the floodwaters of Ether-charged mud rose rapidly and unexpectedly, countless Tartarians could not reach higher ground or the tallest structures, where others sought refuge. Instead, they were trapped in their homes, surrounded by the solidifying Etherstone mud, which ultimately cut off air, leaving them to suffocate. This tragic fate made them silent witnesses to Tartaria's fall, their bodies preserved by the mud, as though sealed in time, until now.
Now, centuries later, the True Tartarians—descendants of those who survived in the subterranean world—are reclaiming their ancient homeland beneath the earth. As they tunnel through Aetherstone and excavate buried homes to expand their underground society, they uncover the remains of the Entombed. Driven by necessity and reverence, the True Tartarians dig these tunnels to reconnect their settlements and make space for new generations. Each home they uncover is a fragment of Tartaria’s lost world, carrying artifacts, relics, and the skeletal remains of the last residents of ancient Tartaria.
The Entombed bodies are often found lying in rooms or seated at tables, remnants of daily life frozen in place. Bones and scraps of ancient cloth remain, but each discovery is treated with the utmost respect. The True Tartarians carefully relocate these remains, reburying the bones on shelves carved into tunnel walls stretching miles beneath the earth. These catacombs serve as solemn memorials, where thousands upon thousands of skeletal remains line the passages, honoring the Entombed as though they were guardians of the past. Their placement in the catacombs is a ritual of remembrance, celebrating them as ancestral heroes who gave their lives to preserve the legacy of Tartaria.
For the True Tartarians, each excavation is more than a practical expansion; it is a spiritual journey. Every uncovered home, every skeleton, and every artifact represents a piece of Tartaria’s rich, enduring culture. Through these excavations, they seek to honor their ancestors, rediscover lost heritage, and, ultimately, reconnect to the legacy of a world buried yet unbroken beneath the mud.
The Arbiter’s Guide
Overview
The Arbiter in Tartaria Prima is more than a Game Master or narrator; they are a living mystery woven into the story, a guide who travels with the players as a semi-enigmatic figure. The Arbiter knows Tartaria’s landscape, lore, and secrets and is a helpful, if cryptic, companion with deep insight and timeless wisdom. They are a storyteller, guide, and subtle force for players to lean on without fully understanding who or what they are.
This guide will help you understand how to act as the Arbiter, what your role entails, and how to bring this unique character to life in an immersive and engaging way for your players.
Role and Responsibilities of the Arbiter
The Mysterious Guide and Companion
The Arbiter exists within the game world as a shadowy, wise figure who “accompanies” the players on their journey. They act like a well-traveled guide, offering cryptic insights, local knowledge, and occasional suggestions.
As the Arbiter, you’ll offer hints and information without explicitly directing players. You’re there to inform their choices, not control them. Think of the Arbiter as an advisor with vast knowledge of Tartaria’s ancient secrets who leaves the final decisions up to the group.
Subtle Guidance Through Cryptic Dialogue
The Arbiter aims to enrich the journey by offering lore and hints through indirect and mysterious phrases. For instance, instead of saying, “There’s a ruin to the east,” the Arbiter might say, “There’s an old ruin to the east... a place of whispers.”
You can suggest places of interest, share relevant lore, or point out risks while maintaining an air of intrigue. The Arbiter may say, “Not many return from these ruins unscathed... be wise with your steps.” These indirect comments add depth without forcing players down a particular path.
Communicating Difficulty Levels and Challenges
In Tartaria Prima, difficulty levels are woven into the Arbiter’s observations. When players attempt a challenging action, you can communicate the difficulty level through casual commentary. For example, if a player wants to jump across a gorge, you might respond, “Ah, I’ve seen a few try that leap before... only the nimblest make it across. I’d place that at a Very Hard if I had to guess.”
This allows players to get a feel for the challenge without breaking immersion and reinforces the Arbiter’s knowledge of Tartaria’s dangers.
World-Building and Local Knowledge
Use the Arbiter to breathe life into Tartaria’s cities, ruins, and landmarks by offering snippets of history or knowledge about people and places. If players arrive at a bustling city, the Arbiter might casually mention a local merchant they “remember” or recommend an inn nearby.
For example, “Ah, yes, old John Begotsnit, the merchant up the road—keeps a fine collection of rare relics if you’re interested.” This adds credibility to the Arbiter’s role as an all-knowing, well-traveled guide.
Acting as the Arbiter
Maintaining an Enigmatic Presence
The Arbiter is knowledgeable yet vague, offering wisdom without giving everything away. Speak with a tone that implies a wealth of experience, sometimes wistful, sometimes amused, but always slightly detached.
Embrace a slightly ethereal, curious demeanor. The Arbiter is fascinated by the players’ journey but doesn’t fully insert themselves. Instead, they watch, guide, and react like seeing Tartaria anew through the players’ eyes.
Hints of a Mysterious Past
While the Arbiter is active in the story, you don’t need to reveal their true identity. Occasionally, drop subtle hints that the Arbiter may have a deeper history, such as, “It’s strange, seeing it all so… empty now,” or, “If only I’d understood things sooner... but that was long ago.”
These small, cryptic phrases hint at a past connection to Tartaria without spelling it out, keeping the Arbiter’s identity a mystery.
Evoking Regret and Reflection
In quiet moments, allow the Arbiter to reflect on the ruins and landscapes of Tartaria, showing glimpses of nostalgia or even sorrow. They might say, “Once, this was a place of life and power... now, only whispers remain,” evoking the feeling of a world fallen from greatness.
These brief reflections make the Arbiter feel like a part of Tartaria itself, as though they, too, are a relic of the lost empire, bound to its memory and haunted by its mysteries.
Handling Player Questions and Guidance
If players ask direct questions, let the Arbiter respond with half-answers or philosophical reflections. If asked how they know so much, the Arbiter might reply with a soft smile and say, “Ah, one learns many things in a life like mine,” or “The Ether has a way of showing you things if you listen.” This keeps the character’s mystery intact.
When players seek specific guidance, give them enough information to encourage curiosity but allow them to interpret or follow the clues as they choose.
Practical Tips for Playing the Arbiter
Use Ambiguous Language: When describing threats, locations, or encounters, speak in a way that leaves things open to interpretation. This builds intrigue and lets players make decisions based on the Arbiter's offers.
Subtle Clues and Personal Observations: Add atmosphere to each area the players visit by having the Arbiter comment on small details. Their observations can include cryptic phrases or fragments of forgotten knowledge as if the world of Tartaria is whispering through them.
Avoid Direct Control: The Arbiter should never directly dictate the players’ actions. Instead, they offer suggestions, possibilities, or insights that enrich the setting. Their guidance is meant to open doors, not lead the way.
Use Moments of Silence and Reflection: Sometimes, saying nothing is just as powerful. The Arbiter might observe players quietly, allowing them to sense that they’re being watched or subtly judged, which adds to the feeling of mystery surrounding them.
Reinforce the Ether Connection: Let players catch glimpses of the Arbiter’s connection to the Ether—a soft glow in their eyes when near Etheric relics or an unexplainable knowledge of Etheric phenomena. These moments help players connect the Arbiter to Tartaria’s lost power.
The Arbiter as an Unfolding Mystery
The Arbiter is an anchor point of intrigue in Tartaria Prima, a figure who moves alongside players and the story without ever fully stepping into the spotlight. As the Arbiter, your purpose is to weave Tartaria’s lore, hazards, and history into every scene, making players feel they’re uncovering secrets alongside an ancient, knowledgeable soul who may or may not be who they appear.
Above all, maintain an air of timeless wisdom and gentle mystery—the Arbiter guides and witnesses, helping players explore a lost world while remaining a puzzle. Leave players questioning who the Arbiter truly is and wondering how deep their connection to Tartaria’s mysteries goes.
Glossary
Advantage – A condition where the PC rolling the dice also can roll an additional 1d6. This can be assigned in many ways.
Aetherkin: The Ether-infused undead of Tartaria, buried by the Great Mud Flood and trapped in an ethereal plane of peace. When the Aetherstone mud encasing them is disturbed, they reawaken with powerful Etheric abilities, emerging hostile to all intruders who threaten their longed-for rest. 
Aetherstone: The hardened, impenetrable form of mud that encased Tartarian cities after the Mud Flood. Aetherstone is extremely durable and resistant to excavation due to its Etheric properties.
Ancient Mud Golems: Guardians formed from Ether-infused mud surrounding a Tartarian citizen unable to make it to safety during the flood, believed to protect Tartarian ruins and artifacts. Their ability to blend with the mud makes them nearly invisible until provoked​.
Arbiter – The game master; they act as an intermediate for the world the PCs are playing in and the game itself. They are the final answer to all debates and play questions.
Arbiter difficulty roll – (DC level or roll) The Arbiter difficulty roll is the roll to try to beat the difficulty level assigned to a task by the Arbiter based on his chart. They consider your skills, abilities, and the current situation when assigning a difficulty level.
Architect’s Key: A small but powerful Tartarian relic can unlock ancient Tartarian doors and machines. 
Architectural Sorcery: The unique Tartarian practice of blending engineering with Aetheric principles to create structures that channel Etheric Power. Examples include self-repairing buildings and defenses that create illusions or repel intruders.
Blinded - Affected players and NPCs alike cannot see for 2 turns and cannot complete any tasks needing eyesight. At the beginning of the third turn, they roll a 1d6 for an even/odd roll. If odd, they roll for a disadvantage for one turn, then return to normal. If they roll even, then they return to normal on that turn.
Conspiracy Architects: A covert cabal focused on protecting humanity from the dangers of Etheric technology by suppressing knowledge of Tartaria. They control global narratives to keep the Unknowing Masses ignorant of Tartaria’s existence.
Disadvantage – Similar to Advantage, this term applies to dice rolls; this condition means that the PC rolling uses a 1d10 and a 1d6 instead of a 1d20. This effectively downgrades your point count total from a possible 20 to a 16.
Etheric Anomalies: Disturbances caused by residual Etheric energy in the Mud Seas and other Tartarian ruins, leading to magnetic field fluctuations, disorienting time effects, and phenomena like etheric lightning and hallucinations.
Etheric Guardians: Powerful automatons of varying sizes that protect Tartarian locations. These machines, from drones to towering war constructs, were programmed to defend important sites with lethal precision​.
Etheric Ruins: Locations with dense Etheric energy from Tartarian structures, where time and space distort. Many Etheric Ruins contain valuable relics, ghostly apparitions, and unpredictable energy phenomena.
Etheric Titan: A massive construct designed to protect Tartarian sites from intruders, considered a final challenge for those who attempt to activate or deactivate key Tartarian systems​.
Even/Odd Roll – This is a 1d6 roll used as a 50/50 chance roll. Even is always the best or positive outcome, whereas odd is always the worst or negative. The Arbiter can assign these as a chance roll for odd requests or can be applied by items or rules. A coin may be used for this purpose as well, this plays well with the Arbiters arbitrary guidance, almost as if fate was a whim.
Faction Points (FP): A game mechanic representing loyalty to specific factions earned by completing faction-specific tasks. FP can be exchanged for exclusive gear, access to restricted areas, or new abilities within that faction.
Forgotten Order: A secretive group of Tartarian scholars and engineers seeking to revive Etheric Power for humanity’s benefit. Often opposed by the Mud Monarchs, they conduct covert operations to rediscover Tartaria’s lost knowledge.
Ignite – This effect, when caused, ignites whatever item it is used on; it is engulfed in flames and continues to burn until extinguished. Ignite causes burn to start.
Max Roll – This means that you have rolled a max dice amount on your damage roll, not on your attack roll; this is not a crit. Tartaria Prima does not have a crit.
Mold Ether: A Tartarian spell used to repair, manipulate, or redirect Etheric relics. Users of this spell need a high Arcana skill to control or restore ancient Tartarian devices safely​.
Mud Flood: The catastrophic event that buried Tartaria in Ether-charged mud, encasing cities and technology beneath impenetrable Etherstone. Triggered by a malfunction in Etheric Power experiments, it led to Tartaria’s fall and the rise of factions trying to control its legacy.
Mud Golems: Etheric constructs made from mud, created either by Mud Magic or failed Mud Tech experiments. These beings are immensely strong but unpredictable, sometimes turning on their creators.
Mud Magic: A primitive form of energy manipulation practiced by those in contact with Etheric-charged mud, used to control terrain, heal, or create Mud Golems. It carries physical and mental risks for practitioners.
Mud Monarchs: A global faction of survivors from Tartaria’s royal bloodlines who control the few remaining Etheric technologies. They suppress knowledge of Tartaria, fearing the loss of their influence over modern society.
Mud Seas: A treacherous region with mud, silt, and submerged Tartarian ruins. Known for unpredictable currents, energy anomalies, and creatures altered by Etheric energy, making it a high-risk zone for explorers.
Mud Tech: A crude adaptation of Etheric technology derived from the resources found in Etheric-charged mud. Its use is dangerous, with unstable properties that often backfire or malfunction.
Reclaimers Guild: A network of treasure hunters and scavengers who sell Tartarian relics on the black market. Although not aligned with any faction, they clash with the Mud Monarchs and the Forgotten Order over control of valuable artifacts.
Relic Trade: The black-market economy surrounding the sale of Tartarian artifacts, especially those tied to Etheric Power. A high-stakes trade dominated by factions like the Reclaimers Guild and opposed by the Mud Monarchs and Forgotten Order.
Relic Trade Economy: A thriving black market for Tartarian relics. Common relics have minor abilities, while rarer artifacts, such as those containing Etheric energy, can command enormous prices due to their power and instability​.
Second Mud Flood: A feared second cataclysm theorized by some factions. Revising or misusing Etheric technology, environmental shifts, or cosmic disturbances could trigger another disaster like the original Mud Flood.
Servants of the Giants: A religious faction that venerates Tartarian Giants, believing in their eventual return to restore Tartaria. They conduct rituals at Tartarian ruins, hoping to awaken the giants and reclaim the lost empire.
Stone Builders: A hybrid religious-scientific group that studies Tartarian architecture as a divine and technological achievement, striving to replicate the secrets of Architectural Sorcery for a new era.
Tartarian Obelisk: An Etheric artifact that emits an Etheric energy field within arm's reach. It can give an advantage on up to three creatures once per long rest, but if damaged, it may release a destructive Etheric blast​.
Tartarian Revivalists: A radical faction intent on restoring Tartaria at any cost, even if it means triggering another cataclysm like the Mud Flood. They are dedicated to reactivating Etheric Power systems and achieving Tartaria’s former glory.
Tartaria Coins (TC): The currency used underground for purchasing rare relics and tools, often on the black market. Players earn TC by selling relics or completing tasks that are usable for high-risk items with potent abilities.
The Entombed: Tartarians who died sealed in their homes during the Great Mud Flood, now uncovered by the True Tartarians as they reclaim underground spaces. Treated with reverence, their bones are reburied on shelves lining thousands of miles of tunnels, serving as solemn reminders of Tartaria’s lost legacy.
Unknowing Masses: The general population, unaware of Tartaria’s history or the existence of Etheric technology. Their ignorance is maintained by factions like the Mud Monarchs and Conspiracy Architects for social control.

Drakova: A legendary Lost Capital of Tartaria, hidden beneath layers of Aetherstone mud and believed to hold a powerful Etheric Core, drawing explorers and factions alike.
Obsidian Pillars: A formation of black, magnetically charged spires that rise from the mud near a former Tartarian observatory. The pillars disrupt technology, and their location suggests they may have once been part of a cosmic energy center​.
Endless Stair: A vast staircase descending into the mud, its endpoint completely submerged. The Endless Stair is believed to lead to one of Tartaria’s greatest cities, but Etheric disturbances prevent explorers from reaching its depths​.
Zharak’s Teeth: A group of sharp, towering spires jutting from shallow waters, attracting explorers with their architectural mysteries. However, the area is home to dangerous mud sirens, which protect the ruins from intruders​.
Sinking Cathedral: The partially submerged steeple of a grand Tartarian cathedral. This structure is said to contain a powerful Tartarian artifact, but no explorers who have entered the cathedral have returned​.
Cradle of Dusk: A pocket within the Mud Seas where the sky glows with residual Etheric energy. The area is filled with wrecked ships and frozen statues of former crew members, an eerie reminder of the power of Etheric storms​.
Mud Seas: Shallow, murky waters covering Tartarian ruins, formed after the Great Mud Flood. The Etheric energy within the mud creates disorienting magnetic distortions and dangerous storms, making navigation treacherous​.
Lost Tartarian Temples: Scattered across Central Asia, these ancient temples once powered Tartaria’s Etheric network, providing limitless energy to the empire. Though abandoned, the temples still contain Etheric machinery, guarded by semi-conscious sentinels that attack any intruders​.
Etheric Chamber: The primary power source and control hub for Tartarian technology. Hidden deep within Tartarian ruins, the Etheric Chamber contains machinery to control Etheric energy, heavily guarded by ancient automatons and protected against intruders by sophisticated Etheric traps​.
Mud Flood Nexus: The subterranean control center believed to have regulated the disastrous Great Mud Flood. Restoring or deactivating this site’s systems could potentially influence the stability of Etheric energy, making it a focal point for factional conflicts​.
The Giant Vault: An ancient Tartarian vault believed to hold the last resting place of the Tartarian Giants, who symbolize Tartaria’s might. Legends claim it contains relics of immense power, but it is unclear if the giants within are protectors or a dormant threat​.
Tartarian Outskirts: The regions surrounding Tartarian cities, littered with defenses, dormant traps, and relics of Etheric technology. Explorers often find clues about Tartaria’s inner workings here, but they must navigate Etheric anomalies and ancient defense systems​.
The Buried Cities: Formerly vibrant Tartarian cities now sealed beneath Etherstone mud. These cities hold Tartaria’s most powerful secrets, preserved by impenetrable mud that resists excavation, where Etheric energy creates time distortions and magnetic anomalies​.
